# Formal Constitution v0.3 Pressure Test

## Purpose

This pressure test evaluates Formal Constitution v0.3 as a precision-and-usability draft.

The v0.3 draft added substantial definition, test, review, audit, and transition language. This pressure test asks whether those refinements made the Constitution:

- harder to evade;
- too complex to implement;
- easier or harder for the public to understand;
- internally consistent;
- ready to become a stable milestone;
- or in need of v0.4 before broader public presentation.

## Draft Under Review

- File: `constitutional-draft/formal_constitution_v0.3.md`
- Version: v0.3
- Status: third formal constitutional draft

---

# Review Method

Each test area should answer:

1. **What v0.3 improved**
2. **What v0.3 may have overcomplicated**
3. **Potential contradiction or overlap**
4. **Abuse scenario**
5. **Implementation concern**
6. **v0.4 recommendation**

---

# Test Area 1 — Definitions and Scope

## v0.3 Improvements

- v0.3 significantly improves definitional precision.
- Public authority now covers direct, delegated, automated, contracted, emergency, and publicly funded authority.
- Material Public Function Test helps prevent evasion by vendors, contractors, public-private partnerships, and informal systems.
- Material Influence Definition closes the “it was only advisory” loophole.
- The draft now better reflects how modern public power actually operates.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- Article I is becoming dense for constitutional text.
- The definitions are useful, but some may eventually belong in a definitions appendix or constitutional statute.
- “Materially shapes” and “materially affects” still require careful harmonization across articles.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Public Authority, Material Public Function, Material Influence, and Hidden Sovereign overlap.
- Overlap is not fatal, but v0.4 should consolidate or cross-reference these definitions to reduce repetition.
- The same actor could trigger multiple doctrines at once: public authority, material influence, hidden sovereign, and technology review.

## Abuse Scenarios

- A contractor argues it does not exercise public authority because the final legal authority sits with a public official.
- A vendor claims its risk score is only advisory despite changing decision probabilities.
- A public-private partnership claims private corporate form shields it from public accountability.
- Agencies exploit definitional complexity to route disputes into narrow technical arguments.

## Implementation Concerns

- Agencies and courts will need a practical checklist for applying these definitions.
- Public interface design must translate these definitions into ordinary language.
- Contractors and vendors will need clear contract clauses incorporating public-authority obligations.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Create a consolidated **Definitions Appendix** or “Definitions and Tests Schedule.”
- Add a short-form public explanation for public authority and material influence.
- Harmonize terms: “materially affects,” “materially shapes,” and “material influence.”
- Add standard public contract language: any entity performing a material public function accepts constitutional accountability duties.

# Test Area 2 — Public Authority, Hidden Sovereigns, and Safe Harbors

## v0.3 Improvements

- Hidden Sovereign Test is a major improvement over earlier rhetorical language.
- It now requires material control or influence, invisibility or inadequate accountability, and effect on public authority.
- Lawful Advocacy Safe Harbor protects journalism, advocacy, scholarship, protest, testimony, civic association, lobbying disclosed by law, and ordinary political persuasion.
- This balances anti-capture goals with civil liberties.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- The Hidden Sovereign Test may still be difficult for ordinary citizens to understand.
- It risks becoming a catchall accusation unless carefully cabined.
- Public officials may overuse the phrase politically even when legal standards are not met.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Hidden Sovereign overlaps with Integrity System, lobbying disclosure, party common control, vendor capture, and algorithmic governance.
- The safe harbor must be cross-referenced in Information Integrity and Party Governance to prevent inconsistent treatment.
- Donor networks can be both lawful advocacy networks and hidden sovereigns depending on control and disclosure.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Government labels hostile civil society, media, or protest movements as hidden sovereigns.
- A donor network shapes policy through undisclosed candidate pipelines and think tanks while claiming ordinary political association.
- An expert body informally controls public-health or AI policy while elected officials rubber-stamp recommendations.
- A vendor controls public system logic, updates, and data access while agencies retain nominal authority.

## Implementation Concerns

- Enforcement bodies need evidentiary standards and remedies.
- Remedies should prioritize disclosure, audit, and accountability before suppression or disqualification.
- Courts will need to protect lawful association and speech while exposing covert control.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add remedy hierarchy for hidden sovereign findings:
  1. disclosure;
  2. audit;
  3. recusal or firewall;
  4. contract correction;
  5. loss of public privilege;
  6. legal sanction for bad-faith covert control.
- Cross-reference Safe Harbor in Information Integrity and Party Governance.
- Require public reasoned findings and appeal for hidden-sovereign determinations.

# Test Area 3 — Democratic Kernel and Kernel Effectiveness

## v0.3 Improvements

- Material Impairment Standard sharpens the Kernel Effectiveness Test.
- It clarifies that impairment must be substantial, practical, and connected to democratic self-correction or the rights floor.
- Ordinary Reform Safe Harbor helps prevent Kernel litigation from blocking legitimate reforms.
- Fast Kernel Review makes the protection operational.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- Kernel review could become a common political litigation tool.
- Courts may be asked to decide whether ordinary reforms “materially impair” democratic self-correction.
- If overused, Kernel language could constitutionalize too many political disputes.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Kernel Effectiveness overlaps with rights review, election review, emergency review, court-packing safeguards, and amendment classification.
- This is acceptable because Kernel matters are foundational, but v0.4 should clarify priority among these routes.
- Ordinary Reform Safe Harbor may conflict with aggressive Kernel protection unless standards are clear.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Governing party preserves formal elections while weakening opposition information access and election administration.
- Legislature preserves courts but strips jurisdiction over politically sensitive cases.
- Executive uses emergency orders to restrict opposition activity while claiming temporary necessity.
- Opposition claims all electoral or court reforms are Kernel impairment.

## Implementation Concerns

- Fast Kernel Review requires judicial capacity and strict timelines.
- Courts need remedial restraint to avoid becoming constitutional managers of ordinary politics.
- Public must understand that Kernel protection is about preserving self-correction, not freezing every institution.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add cross-reference to Remedial Hierarchy Rule.
- Define Fast Kernel Review timeline by constitutional statute.
- Add examples of ordinary reform versus Kernel impairment in an interpretive appendix.
- Require claimants to show practical impairment, not mere disagreement with institutional design.

# Test Area 4 — Rights, Personhood, Limitations, and Fast Relief

## v0.3 Improvements

- “Under Commonwealth Authority” now clarifies who receives personhood protections.
- Political rights are properly distinguished from rights owed to all persons.
- Rights Limitation burden is now on the public authority.
- Heightened Review for high-risk rights strengthens protection for voting, detention, speech, surveillance, due process, and emergency restrictions.
- Minimum Public Obligation adds procedural dignity without creating unlimited entitlement to specific policies.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- Rights doctrine is now much stronger but also more legally complex.
- “Minimum public obligation” could be litigated heavily if not bounded.
- Heightened review categories may produce many constitutional challenges.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Minimum Public Obligation overlaps with due process, public accountability, administrative courts, and fast rights.
- Personhood rights may intersect with immigration, security, detention, and emergency powers.
- Data rights overlap with Technology System and Distributed Ledger safeguards.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Government uses legal-status distinctions to deny procedural protections.
- Agencies claim benefits are discretionary and therefore avoid due process.
- Emergency officials limit speech or assembly without proving necessity.
- Surveillance programs are justified through broad safety claims without proportionality.
- Automated systems deny services with nominal explanation and delayed appeal.

## Implementation Concerns

- Courts must distinguish procedural rights from substantive policy entitlements.
- Agencies need capacity to provide notice, appeal, and explanation.
- Heightened rights review requires sufficient judicial staffing.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Clarify that Minimum Public Obligation protects fair administration, not a fixed policy outcome.
- Add administrative due process schedule by decision type.
- Cross-reference Technology Article for automated rights harms.
- Add explicit emergency burden rule: emergency rights limits require continuing evidence.

# Test Area 5 — Public Accountability and Public Interface

## v0.3 Improvements

- Public Accountability Defect Categories are a major improvement.
- Named Accountable Office Rule prevents generic agency responsibility.
- Public Interface Audit Rule makes usability enforceable.
- Non-Digital Access Rule prevents digital-only accountability.
- The Cure Rule now has better structure.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- Public-accountability compliance may become administratively heavy.
- Classification of defects could create procedural fights.
- Agencies may focus on record production over substantive service delivery.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Public Accountability overlaps with nearly every article.
- This is intentional, but v0.4 should avoid duplicative reporting burdens.
- Interface audits overlap with stewardship, technology, fiscal, and court dashboards.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Agencies classify material omissions as clerical defects.
- Public records list an office but not a real accountable decision-owner.
- Digital interface works for lawyers but not ordinary people.
- Privacy/security exceptions are overused to hide controversial decisions.
- Non-digital access exists on paper but is underfunded.

## Implementation Concerns

- Requires strong records management, civic design, accessibility, and public administration capacity.
- Needs funding and staffing or it becomes symbolic.
- Public interface must be staged during transition.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add default cure windows by defect category in constitutional statute or schedule.
- Add “substance over form” rule: technical publication does not satisfy accountability if unusable.
- Require annual Public Interface Audit with public score.
- Define privacy/security exception review process.
- Add funding protection for the Public Constitutional Interface.

# Test Area 6 — Federalism, Capacity Gates, and Support Nodes

## v0.3 Improvements

- Capacity Metrics Requirement strengthens Capacity Gates.
- Support Node Exit Plan prevents temporary assistance from becoming permanent centralization.
- Capacity Findings must be public, reasoned, time-bounded, and reviewable.
- The model now better handles unequal state/local capacity.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- Capacity scoring may become bureaucratic and politically contested.
- National support-node assistance could be experienced as takeover.
- Local governments may face compliance burdens beyond their resources.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Capacity Gates overlap with Transition Readiness, Local Fiscal Distress, and Public Accountability.
- Rights Floor Supremacy can justify national intervention, while Local Ceiling protects local autonomy; the tension is productive but needs careful review.
- Support nodes may implicate federalism and fiscal constitution simultaneously.

## Abuse Scenarios

- National government declares incapacity to centralize local power.
- Local government falsely claims capacity while rights remedies fail.
- Wealthier jurisdictions achieve capacity faster, increasing inequality.
- Higher-level support node remains indefinitely after initial justification disappears.

## Implementation Concerns

- Requires measurable capacity standards and reliable data.
- Local governments may need funding, training, and technology support.
- Review bodies must distinguish bad-faith local resistance from genuine incapacity.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add capacity metric schedule outside core constitutional text.
- Require technical and fiscal assistance before punitive intervention where rights are not imminently threatened.
- Add periodic review and automatic expiration for support-node arrangements.
- Include local public participation in support-node exit planning.

# Test Area 7 — Five-System Routing and Jurisdiction

## v0.3 Improvements

- Emergency Provisional Lead Assignment solves urgent multi-system routing problems.
- Joint Review for Equal Multi-System Harm addresses genuinely overlapping matters.
- Jurisdiction Map Audit and Wrong-Door Transfer improve public usability.
- Primary Harm Rule remains a strong organizing device.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- There are now several routing doctrines: Lead System, Lead Challenge, Primary Harm, Emergency Provisional Lead, Joint Review, Wrong-Door Transfer.
- This is robust but complex.
- Officials and citizens may need automated or guided routing tools.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Emergency Provisional Lead Assignment could overlap with emergency powers and fast rights review.
- Joint Review may blur accountable ownership unless strictly managed.
- Wrong-Door Transfer overlaps with One-Door Filing in courts.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Agency chooses a friendly provisional lead during an emergency.
- Joint review panel delays remedy while systems debate responsibility.
- Public offices transfer claims repeatedly instead of resolving them.
- Jurisdiction maps are accurate but too complex for ordinary use.

## Implementation Concerns

- Requires a central routing function or public interface with real administrative authority.
- Needs deadlines for transfers and lead assignment.
- Must avoid creating a new procedural battlefield before merits review.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add transfer deadlines.
- Require one accountable implementation owner even in joint review.
- Add public routing assistant through the Public Constitutional Interface.
- Cross-reference court One-Door Filing to avoid duplicate doctrine.
- Add anti-circular-transfer rule: a matter cannot be bounced indefinitely between systems.

# Test Area 8 — Electoral System and Citizen Participation

## v0.3 Improvements

- v0.3 strengthens the electoral design by adding minimum electoral guarantees.
- It clarifies that mixed-member proportional representation must include proportional correction, independent boundaries, fair ballot access, public party role assignment, coalition disclosure, and delayed self-dealing protection.
- Electoral Parameter Review after two cycles is a strong adaptive mechanism.
- Citizen Assembly Integrity Rule improves legitimacy and reduces capture.
- Referendum Rights Limit protects the Kernel and rights floor from majoritarian override.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- The electoral system is now more robust but also more difficult to explain.
- Voters must understand local vote, party vote, proportional correction, coalitions, party roles, and public ledgers.
- Citizen participation mechanisms add democratic legitimacy but can create procedural fatigue if overused.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Electoral Parameter Review overlaps with Democratic System review, Public Party Status Ledger, and Transition first-election review.
- Citizen Assembly Integrity overlaps with Civic Review Mechanism.
- Referendum Rights Limit overlaps with Kernel and Rights articles, but this is appropriate.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Incumbents preserve MMP in name but manipulate thresholds, list rules, or district ratio.
- Electoral Parameter Review is used to justify changes that benefit current parties.
- Citizen assemblies are convened with biased briefing materials or narrow mandates.
- Referendums are framed to pressure courts or minority rights indirectly.
- Voter education is underfunded, causing public confusion and mistrust.

## Implementation Concerns

- Requires major civic education, election administration training, ballot design, and public simulations.
- Independent boundary drawing and proportional correction rules need technical capacity.
- Public trust depends on the first two election cycles being well-managed.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add a **Ballot Comprehension Standard** before major electoral changes.
- Require independent public testing of ballot design and voter materials.
- Define minimum review criteria for Electoral Parameter Review to prevent self-serving changes.
- Add public simulation requirements before first use of a new electoral formula.
- Cross-reference First Election Stabilization Protocol in the Transition article.

# Test Area 9 — Party Power Roles and Party Guardrails

## v0.3 Improvements

- v0.3 clarifies Stewardship Power as a nonpartisan constitutional function, not a party role.
- Confidence-and-Supply Status addresses ambiguity around Opposition classification.
- Provisional Party Role Assignment prevents post-election uncertainty.
- Common Control Evidence Standard is stronger and protects against fake pluralism.
- Coalition Side Agreement Disclosure closes a major backroom-governance loophole.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- Party classification is now legally sophisticated, but public understanding may lag.
- Common-control investigations could become fact-heavy and contentious.
- Provisional role assignment plus appeals may create temporary uncertainty.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Party Power Roles overlap with Electoral System, Integrity System, Public Party Status Ledger, and Amendment Rules.
- Common Control overlaps with hidden sovereigns and influence disclosure.
- Coalition Side Agreement Disclosure overlaps with Integrity and Public Accountability.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Large parties use allied entities to create fake Plurality or Emergent parties.
- Regulators overclassify independent aligned parties as commonly controlled.
- A party enters confidence-and-supply but still claims Opposition privileges.
- Secret side agreements trade appointments, budget concessions, or regional privileges.
- Governing Power delays provisional role assignment to weaken oversight.

## Implementation Concerns

- Requires a capable Election and Party Court.
- Requires fast, reliable party status publication.
- Requires evidence standards that expose shell parties without chilling lawful coalition politics.
- Requires public explanations so voters understand party roles.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Define **Party Role Threshold Schedule** in a constitutional statute or parameter appendix.
- Add deadline for provisional role assignment after certification.
- Add public explainer requirement for confidence-and-supply arrangements.
- Clarify that common-control remedies are functional, not party-dissolution tools.
- Add fast appeal path for party status disputes before government formation deadlines expire.

# Test Area 10 — Legislature, Civic Review, and Oversight

## v0.3 Improvements

- v0.3 adds clearer chamber-power allocation principles.
- Regional Chamber role is better tied to federal balance.
- Civic Review Integrity Rule is significantly stronger.
- High-Impact Classification Review helps prevent abuse of high-impact designation.
- Legislative Investigation Due Process improves protection against harassment.
- Oversight Allocation protects Opposition and Plurality participation.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- Legislature now has many procedural categories: ordinary legislation, high-impact legislation, Civic Review, chamber-specific powers, oversight allocation, enhanced rules.
- Civic Review could become another procedural bottleneck if triggered too often.
- Chamber-specific powers remain partly deferred to constitutional statute.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Civic Review overlaps with citizen assemblies and high-impact legislation review.
- Regional Chamber authority overlaps with federalism and national uniformity findings.
- Legislative oversight overlaps with Integrity System and courts.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Regional Chamber blocks ordinary legislation by claiming federal-balance impact.
- Governing Power classifies legislation as ordinary to avoid Civic Review.
- Opposition or Plurality blocs overuse Civic Review to delay governance.
- Legislative investigations target journalists, activists, or private citizens.
- Oversight committee allocation is manipulated through procedural rules.

## Implementation Concerns

- Requires well-drafted legislative rules.
- Requires staff capacity for high-impact review, fiscal review, rights review, and civic review.
- Needs public-facing explanation of when Civic Review applies and what it can do.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add default rule: Regional Chamber heightened role applies only where regional authority, national uniformity, constitutional statutes, or intergovernmental fiscal obligations are materially affected.
- Add cap or cooldown for repeated Civic Review triggers on substantially similar matters.
- Add public High-Impact Classification Ledger.
- Define oversight committee minimum allocation in constitutional statute.
- Add anti-harassment standard for legislative investigations.

# Test Area 11 — Executive, Civic President, and Emergency Council

## v0.3 Improvements

- Constructive No-Confidence Minimum Rule improves continuity.
- Civic President Selection Principles prevent unilateral capture by the Governing Power.
- Emergency Council Activation Rule clarifies scope, duration, reporting, rights review, fiscal review, and normalcy restoration.
- Executive Noncompliance Escalation adds teeth to lawful orders.
- Judicial Removal Limits reduce risk of courts being used for routine political disputes.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- The executive system has multiple centers: Chief Executive, Governing Council, Civic President, Emergency Council.
- The Civic President could be hard to explain publicly unless carefully framed.
- Emergency Council rules add process during crisis, though this is justified.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Emergency Council overlaps with Resilience System and Emergency Powers.
- Civic President overlaps with Stewardship Power and Constitutional Court referrals.
- Executive Noncompliance overlaps with court enforcement, Integrity review, and impeachment.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Civic President claims constitutional guardianship to interfere in ordinary policy.
- Chief Executive uses Emergency Council to centralize power.
- Legislature weaponizes no-confidence to destabilize government.
- Executive ignores stewardship findings and delays compliance.
- Courts are asked to remove executives over policy disagreements framed as constitutional violations.

## Implementation Concerns

- Civic President selection must be designed carefully.
- Emergency Council membership must avoid both partisan capture and technocratic overreach.
- Public must understand who actually governs.
- Constructive no-confidence requires clear replacement certification.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add public-facing executive authority map requirement.
- Define Civic President as continuity/referral office, not reserve executive.
- Require Emergency Council membership to include relevant executive officials plus rights, fiscal, and emergency oversight liaisons without giving stewardship bodies governing power.
- Define noncompliance escalation timelines.
- Add requirement that no-confidence replacement bloc publish governing agreement before confirmation.

# Test Area 12 — Courts, Tribunals, and Remedies

## v0.3 Improvements

- Remedial Hierarchy Rule improves judicial restraint.
- Election and Emergency Review Timing requires expedited review.
- Independent Technical Expert Pool strengthens technology adjudication.
- Court Compliance Dashboard makes enforcement visible.
- Judicial Structure Change Rule protects against court-packing.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- The court system is now powerful and specialized, but complex.
- Multiple tribunals may create routing confusion despite One-Door Filing.
- Public-facing legal access will require significant infrastructure.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Fast Rights Tribunal, Constitutional Court, Technology Review Tribunal, and Election and Party Court may overlap in urgent cases.
- Court Compliance Dashboard overlaps with Public Constitutional Interface.
- Structural remedies may conflict with Remedial Restraint if not tightly limited.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Litigants flood Fast Rights Tribunal with political disputes.
- Agencies route disputes to slower administrative channels.
- Technical expert pool becomes dominated by vendors or elite institutions.
- Courts impose broad structural remedies where narrow relief would suffice.
- Governing Power changes court structure under reform rhetoric.

## Implementation Concerns

- Requires judicial staffing, triage, legal aid, technical expertise, and public routing.
- Specialized tribunals must be coordinated.
- Court-order enforcement must be strong without creating judicial administration of agencies.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add **Court Routing Schedule** or appendix.
- Add timeline defaults for urgent election, emergency, and rights matters.
- Add technical expert pool governance rules.
- Add standard: structural remedies require persistent violation, failed lesser remedies, or systemic rights failure.
- Add legal aid/access requirement for high-risk rights claims.

# Test Area 13 — Integrity System and Whistleblowers

## v0.3 Improvements

- Severe Sanctions section improves due process for high-stakes integrity findings.
- Protected Whistleblower Channels are stronger.
- Pre-election integrity claims receive special timing protection.
- Good-faith whistleblowers are protected even if allegations are not proven.
- Criminal punishment remains judicial, preserving separation.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- Integrity processes are now robust but procedurally heavy.
- High-stakes claims could require accelerated review, public reasoning, and appeal simultaneously.
- Whistleblower channels, confidentiality, and classified reporting require sophisticated administration.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Integrity overlaps with Public Accountability, Party Governance, Fiscal Constitution, Security, and Technology.
- Whistleblower protections overlap with Security article and classified channels.
- Severe sanctions overlap with courts and party status rights.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Integrity complaints are timed to damage candidates before elections.
- Officials retaliate against whistleblowers through indirect means.
- Preliminary investigations leak to media.
- Minor errors are framed as bad faith.
- False reports are used to trigger public scandal.

## Implementation Concerns

- Requires independent investigators, appeal bodies, confidentiality systems, and anti-retaliation enforcement.
- Requires safeguards against both suppression and weaponization of complaints.
- Pre-election expedited claims need very tight calendars.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add confidential intake and triage rules.
- Define retaliation broadly to include demotion, access loss, clearance retaliation, harassment, contract loss, and blacklisting.
- Add leak discipline for preliminary integrity investigations.
- Add pre-election integrity calendar and provisional remedy rules.
- Cross-reference Security whistleblower article for classified matters.

# Test Area 14 — Stewardship Appointments and Stewardship Limits

## v0.3 Improvements

- Default Deadlock Sequence is a major improvement.
- Acting Appointment Limit prevents vacancy-based capture.
- Composition Balance Review prevents arbitrary exclusion.
- Independence-Risk Publication improves transparency.
- Citizen Review Panel Standards clarify role.
- Stewardship Classification Rule and Stewardship Finding Appeal reduce shadow-government risk.

## Overcomplexity Risk

- Appointment processes are now sophisticated but potentially slow.
- Composition balance and independence-risk statements may become politically contentious.
- Citizen panels require administration and public trust.

## Contradictions / Overlap

- Stewardship appointments overlap with legislature, Regional Chamber, courts, and transition readiness.
- Stewardship Classification overlaps with court review and public accountability.
- Performance Failure Review could conflict with independence if abused.

## Abuse Scenarios

- Governing bloc induces appointment deadlock to force acting officials.
- Professional guilds dominate qualification review.
- Independence-risk statements are weaponized against lawful prior advocacy.
- Performance Failure Review becomes retaliation for aggressive oversight.
- Composition balance is used to exclude disfavored but qualified candidates.

## Implementation Concerns

- Requires careful appointment calendars.
- Requires neutral qualification panels.
- Requires public education: stewardship bodies are guardians, not rulers.
- Requires funding to manage appointment and review processes.

## v0.4 Recommendations

- Add maximum acting appointment duration.
- Add default appointment timeline.
- Add independence-risk safe harbor for lawful public criticism and nonpartisan expertise.
- Define composition-balance standard as anti-domination, not quota.
- Add public stewardship role guide.
