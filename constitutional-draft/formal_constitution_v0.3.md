# Preamble

We establish the Modular Constitutional Commonwealth to secure democratic self-correction, protect equal rights, prevent capture, preserve public trust, and enable lawful adaptation across generations.

The Commonwealth exists so that the people may govern themselves through institutions that are transparent, accountable, resilient, and capable of improvement.

The central commitment of this Constitution is:

> The people must always retain the lawful power to correct, reform, restrain, and replace their government.

Its shortest promise is:

> Make democracy hard to kill.  
> Make policy easy to improve.  
> Make power impossible to hide.

# Article I — Foundational Principles and Definitions

## Section 1. Popular Sovereignty

Sovereignty resides in the people.

All public authority is exercised in trust, under law, and subject to review.

## Section 2. Democratic Self-Correction

No institution, party, official, emergency power, algorithm, donor network, security force, private entity, foreign actor, vendor, contractor, public-private partnership, or expert body may permanently remove the people’s ability to:

1. vote;
2. organize;
3. oppose;
4. petition;
5. protest;
6. challenge public authority;
7. access public records;
8. form parties;
9. replace officials;
10. amend the constitutional framework through lawful procedures.

## Section 3. Constitutional Layers

The Commonwealth is organized into three layers:

1. the Democratic Kernel;
2. the Constitutional Framework;
3. policy and administrative modules.

The Democratic Kernel protects the conditions of self-government.

The Constitutional Framework organizes public power.

Modules include ordinary policies, programs, budgets, regulations, agencies, pilots, technologies, services, and administrative systems.

## Section 4. Public Authority Defined

“Public authority” includes any power, decision, process, action, omission, system, or delegated function that materially affects public rights, obligations, benefits, status, services, public money, public records, elections, enforcement, surveillance, emergency authority, or access to government.

Public authority includes authority exercised by:

1. elected officials;
2. public agencies;
3. courts;
4. stewardship bodies;
5. state, regional, and local governments;
6. emergency bodies;
7. security forces;
8. contractors performing public functions;
9. public-private partnerships;
10. automated or algorithmic systems;
11. vendors operating public systems;
12. entities receiving delegated public power;
13. entities administering public funds or legally required public services.

## Section 5. Material Public Function Test

A person, entity, contractor, vendor, public-private partnership, automated system, or other actor performs a material public function when its conduct materially shapes or determines:

1. access to public rights;
2. public benefits;
3. public services;
4. public obligations;
5. enforcement priority;
6. surveillance exposure;
7. legal status;
8. voting access;
9. public records;
10. public funds;
11. eligibility for public programs;
12. operation of public infrastructure;
13. administration of legally required public duties.

Where a material public function is performed, the function is subject to public accountability, rights review, audit, and appeal requirements, regardless of whether the actor is formally public or private.

## Section 6. Material Influence Defined

A system, process, recommendation, risk score, ranking, workflow, model, contractor action, or advisory input materially influences public authority when it changes or materially shapes:

1. eligibility;
2. priority;
3. access;
4. legal status;
5. enforcement likelihood;
6. surveillance likelihood;
7. benefit approval;
8. public-service timing;
9. human reviewer behavior;
10. official decision probability.

An actor may not avoid accountability by describing a material influence as advisory, technical, internal, informal, or preliminary.

## Section 7. Hidden Sovereign Defined

A hidden sovereign is any person, institution, party, donor network, vendor, algorithm, emergency body, expert office, foreign actor, contractor, informal structure, or coordinated network that exercises material public authority without lawful basis, public accountability, appeal, audit, or review.

## Section 8. Hidden Sovereign Test

A hidden sovereign exists where:

1. an actor or system has material control or material influence over public authority;
2. the control or influence is not visible to the public or affected persons;
3. the control or influence lacks adequate legal basis, accountability, audit, appeal, or review;
4. the effect materially shapes rights, obligations, benefits, enforcement, public money, public access, elections, emergency authority, or public decision-making.

A finding of hidden sovereignty must be evidence-based, publicly reasoned where possible, proportionate, and reviewable.

## Section 9. Lawful Advocacy Safe Harbor

Nothing in this Constitution shall treat lawful advocacy, journalism, scholarship, protest, organizing, public-interest litigation, expert testimony, lobbying disclosed under law, civic association, or ordinary political persuasion as hidden sovereignty solely because it seeks to influence public decision-making.

The safe harbor does not protect bribery, coercion, fraud, covert foreign control, shell-party manipulation, undisclosed paid influence, unlawful threats, concealed conflicts, or material control over public authority without accountability.

## Section 10. No Hidden Sovereign

No public power may operate without:

1. visible owner;
2. legal basis;
3. appeal path;
4. audit path;
5. review, renewal, or expiration rule.

Public action lacking these elements is presumptively defective and must be cured, paused, or reviewed according to law.

## Section 11. Classification of Constitutional Layer

Disputes over whether a rule belongs to the Kernel, Framework, or Modules shall be reviewable by constitutional procedure.

Classification shall consider:

1. whether the rule protects democratic self-correction;
2. whether it protects the rights floor;
3. whether it structures public power;
4. whether it is ordinary policy;
5. whether changing it would alter future democratic competition.

# Article II — The Democratic Kernel

## Section 1. Kernel Definition

The Democratic Kernel contains the minimum conditions of legitimate self-government.

It includes:

1. free and fair elections;
2. equal citizenship;
3. civil liberties;
4. due process;
5. independent courts;
6. protected opposition;
7. peaceful transfer of power;
8. civilian control of security forces;
9. prohibition on permanent emergency rule;
10. public ability to challenge government action.

## Section 2. Kernel Protection

The Democratic Kernel may not be removed, suspended, hollowed out, or rendered ineffective by ordinary law, emergency decree, party rule, administrative action, algorithmic system, court restructuring, fiscal starvation, jurisdiction stripping, delegated private action, or ordinary constitutional amendment.

## Section 3. Kernel Effectiveness Test

Formal preservation of a Kernel institution or right is insufficient if public power materially disables its practical function.

A Kernel impairment exists where public authority materially prevents or degrades:

1. meaningful electoral competition;
2. peaceful transfer of power;
3. access to independent courts;
4. opposition capacity;
5. equal citizenship;
6. due process;
7. civil liberties;
8. civilian control of coercive forces;
9. lawful review of emergency power;
10. public ability to challenge government.

## Section 4. Material Impairment Standard

A Kernel impairment must be substantial, practical, and connected to democratic self-correction or the rights floor.

In determining material impairment, reviewing authorities shall consider:

1. actual practical effect, not merely formal institutional existence;
2. cumulative effect of multiple actions;
3. intent where evidence exists;
4. whether the public retains meaningful correction mechanisms;
5. whether affected persons retain timely access to review;
6. whether the action favors incumbents, one party, or one faction in future competition;
7. whether the impairment is temporary, necessary, proportionate, and reviewable.

## Section 5. Ordinary Reform Safe Harbor

Good-faith institutional reform is permitted when it preserves meaningful democratic function, rights protection, judicial independence, electoral fairness, opposition capacity, and peaceful transfer.

Reform shall not be deemed Kernel impairment merely because it changes institutional design, unless it materially disables democratic self-correction or the rights floor.

## Section 6. Fast Kernel Review

Alleged Kernel impairment shall have expedited constitutional review.

Fast Kernel Review must be available where public authority threatens election integrity, transfer of power, court access, opposition rights, civil liberties, emergency review, or other core self-correction functions.

## Section 7. Refounding

Any attempt to alter the Democratic Kernel requires a full constitutional refounding process, including:

1. public convention or equivalent public process;
2. rights continuity;
3. independent review;
4. public explanation;
5. supermajority approval;
6. public ratification;
7. transition safeguards;
8. no emergency shortcut.

# Article III — Rights of the People and Persons

## Section 1. Equal Citizenship

All citizens are equal under this Constitution and entitled to equal protection of the law.

## Section 2. Under Commonwealth Authority Defined

A person is under Commonwealth authority when the person is subject to Commonwealth custody, jurisdiction, legal process, public enforcement, public surveillance, public benefit administration, public service administration, public data systems, public algorithmic systems, or other public authority exercised by or on behalf of the Commonwealth.

## Section 3. Personhood Rights

Due process, bodily security, basic legal protection, privacy, access to courts, protection from unlawful detention, protection from secret punishment, and protection from unlawful surveillance apply to all persons under Commonwealth authority.

## Section 4. Political Rights of Citizens

Citizens have the right to:

1. vote;
2. run for office subject to neutral qualifications;
3. form political parties;
4. support candidates;
5. organize peacefully;
6. oppose government;
7. access fair electoral processes.

## Section 5. Civil Liberties

The Commonwealth shall protect:

1. speech;
2. press;
3. conscience;
4. religion;
5. peaceful assembly;
6. association;
7. petition;
8. lawful protest;
9. lawful dissent;
10. peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 6. Due Process

No person may be deprived of liberty, legal status, public entitlement, property, or protected rights without lawful process.

Due process shall include notice, reasoned basis, opportunity to respond, impartial review, and remedy proportionate to the nature and seriousness of the decision.

## Section 7. Lawful Limitations

Rights may be limited only by law and only where the public authority proves the limitation is:

1. publicly authorized;
2. necessary;
3. proportionate;
4. non-discriminatory;
5. time-bounded where appropriate;
6. reviewable;
7. consistent with the Democratic Kernel.

The burden of justification rests on the public authority imposing the limitation.

## Section 8. Heightened Review for High-Risk Rights

Limitations on voting, candidacy, due process, detention, speech, press, peaceful assembly, political opposition, surveillance, court access, emergency review, or other Kernel-connected rights require heightened review.

Heightened review requires a public authority to show:

1. compelling or weighty public purpose;
2. lawful basis;
3. narrow tailoring;
4. lack of adequate less-restrictive alternative where applicable;
5. meaningful review;
6. expiration or periodic renewal where continuing.

## Section 9. Privacy and Digital Rights

The people and persons under Commonwealth authority have rights against unlawful surveillance, secret automated punishment, and unauthorized use of personal data.

Data and digital rights include:

1. notice;
2. access where lawful;
3. correction;
4. data minimization;
5. purpose limitation;
6. appeal;
7. human review where rights are affected;
8. auditability;
9. remedy for unlawful use.

## Section 10. Algorithmic Due Process

Any rights-impacting automated system must provide:

1. notice;
2. explanation;
3. meaningful human review;
4. appeal;
5. auditability;
6. human accountability owner;
7. capacity for correction or rollback.

## Section 11. Fast Rights Protection

A fast rights injunction path shall exist where delay would cause serious or irreversible harm.

Fast relief requires:

1. plausible serious rights claim;
2. imminent or difficult-to-repair harm;
3. connection to public authority;
4. inadequate ordinary remedy;
5. proportional requested relief.

Relief shall be narrow where possible and broad only where necessary.

## Section 12. Minimum Public Obligation

Where the Commonwealth creates or administers public benefits, public services, enforcement systems, or public legal processes, it must provide minimum procedural dignity, access, notice, appeal, and non-discriminatory administration.

Nothing in this Section creates unlimited entitlement to any particular policy design, but public systems once created must be administered lawfully, fairly, and reviewably.

# Article IV — Public Accountability

## Section 1. Public Accountability Right

Every person has the right to know, for any exercise of public authority:

1. who made the decision;
2. what law authorized it;
3. how it may be challenged;
4. who audits it;
5. when it expires, renews, or is reviewed;
6. whether an algorithm was materially involved;
7. whether relevant conflicts were disclosed.

## Section 2. Public Decision Defined

A public decision includes formal, informal, automated, delegated, emergency, fiscal, procurement, regulatory, adjudicative, administrative, or contractor action that materially affects public authority, rights, obligations, public funds, public services, or public records.

## Section 3. Named Accountable Office Rule

Public accountability records shall identify a responsible office with sufficient specificity for the public to determine who owns the decision.

Generic agency labels are insufficient where a specific office, official role, program, contractor, automated system, or stewardship body materially owns the decision.

## Section 4. Public Constitutional Interface

The Commonwealth shall maintain a plain-language public interface showing:

1. public decisions;
2. accountable owners;
3. legal authority;
4. appeal paths;
5. audit paths;
6. algorithms used;
7. conflicts disclosed;
8. party status;
9. coalition agreements;
10. public funding;
11. active emergency powers;
12. review and expiration dates.

## Section 5. Usability

Public accountability records shall be searchable, timely, accessible, and understandable to ordinary persons.

Transparency that cannot be used shall not satisfy this Constitution.

## Section 6. Public Accountability Defect Categories

Public accountability defects shall be classified as:

1. clerical defect;
2. material omission;
3. rights-impacting defect;
4. bad-faith concealment.

A clerical defect is a minor error that does not impair public understanding or review.

A material omission is a missing or incomplete record that impairs public understanding, audit, or appeal.

A rights-impacting defect is a defect affecting rights, benefits, enforcement, legal status, surveillance, emergency power, or other material interests.

Bad-faith concealment is intentional or reckless failure to disclose required public accountability information.

## Section 7. Accountability Cure Rule

Where required accountability information is missing, false, incomplete, stale, or unusable, the responsible public authority must cure the defect within a defined period.

For rights-impacting or materially harmful action, failure to cure may result in pause, review, invalidation, corrective order, sanction, or other remedy defined by law.

Repeated defects by the same office shall trigger heightened audit.

## Section 8. Public Interface Audit Rule

The Public Constitutional Interface shall be independently audited for:

1. completeness;
2. timeliness;
3. accuracy;
4. accessibility;
5. searchability;
6. plain-language quality;
7. appeal usability;
8. algorithm disclosure;
9. conflict disclosure;
10. party status clarity;
11. emergency visibility;
12. non-digital access.

Audit results shall be public.

## Section 9. Non-Digital Access Rule

No person shall be denied practical access to accountability, appeal, rights, or public-service information solely because the person lacks internet access, digital identity, private devices, technical literacy, or assistive technology.

The Commonwealth shall maintain non-digital or assisted access channels for essential accountability and rights functions.

## Section 10. Exceptions

Privacy and security exceptions must be narrow, legally authorized, reviewable, time-bounded where appropriate, and accompanied by public surrogate explanation where possible.

# Article V — Federal, State, and Local Authority

## Section 1. Competent Subsidiarity

Public authority shall be exercised at the lowest level capable of:

1. protecting rights;
2. delivering results;
3. managing spillover effects;
4. maintaining capacity;
5. preserving accountability.

## Section 2. National Floor and Local Ceiling

The national government shall set minimum floors for:

1. rights;
2. election integrity;
3. anti-corruption;
4. public accountability;
5. public technology safety;
6. emergency limits;
7. fiscal transparency;
8. civil service neutrality.

State, regional, and local governments may exceed those floors unless uniformity is constitutionally necessary.

## Section 3. Rights Floor Supremacy

Subsidiarity shall not excuse violation of the rights floor.

## Section 4. Uniformity Necessity

National uniformity beyond minimum floors requires a public Uniformity Necessity Finding based on:

1. rights;
2. spillover;
3. national security;
4. systemic risk;
5. interoperability;
6. fiscal integrity;
7. constitutional duty.

A Uniformity Necessity Finding must explain why minimum floors are insufficient and why variation would defeat the constitutional purpose.

## Section 5. Subsidiarity Review

Lower governments may challenge national overreach through constitutional review.

A reviewing body shall consider:

1. local competence;
2. rights compliance;
3. spillover;
4. capacity;
5. transparency;
6. systemic risk;
7. constitutional duty.

## Section 6. Capacity Gates

State, regional, local, and stewardship nodes may not receive full constitutional duties until minimum operational capacity exists, including:

1. staffing;
2. funding;
3. records;
4. appeal path;
5. audit path;
6. secure technology;
7. accessibility;
8. continuity;
9. public interface;
10. legal authority.

Where capacity is lacking, higher-level support nodes may temporarily assist.

## Section 7. Capacity Metrics Requirement

Capacity shall be assessed through public metrics defined by constitutional statute.

Capacity metrics shall include, where relevant:

1. trained responsible officials;
2. functional budget;
3. public records capacity;
4. appeal and complaint processing;
5. audit access;
6. technology security;
7. accessibility;
8. non-digital access;
9. continuity planning;
10. rights-remedy capacity;
11. public-service performance;
12. local fiscal condition.

## Section 8. Support Node Exit Plan

Where a higher-level support node temporarily assists or performs a lower-level function due to capacity failure, the support arrangement must include:

1. public legal basis;
2. defined scope;
3. capacity-building plan;
4. rights safeguards;
5. local participation;
6. review date;
7. exit criteria;
8. appeal or challenge path.

Support-node intervention shall not become permanent centralization unless separately authorized through constitutional process.

## Section 9. Capacity Findings

Capacity findings must be public, reasoned, time-bounded, and reviewable.

A finding of incapacity shall not be used to punish a local community, suppress lawful local self-government, or reduce the rights of affected persons.

# Article VI — The Five Constitutional Systems

## Section 1. Establishment

The Commonwealth shall be organized through five constitutional systems:

1. the Democratic System;
2. the Rights and Justice System;
3. the Integrity System;
4. the Technology System;
5. the Resilience System.

## Section 2. System Design

Each system shall include:

1. national hub;
2. state or regional nodes;
3. local delivery channels where appropriate;
4. public accountability interface;
5. audit path;
6. appeal path;
7. lead-system assignment rules.

## Section 3. Lead System Rule

When a matter involves multiple systems, one system shall be publicly designated as lead.

Shared responsibility shall not become shared evasion.

## Section 4. Lead Assignment Challenge

A lead-system designation may be challenged where it appears designed to evade rights review, integrity review, technology review, fiscal review, emergency oversight, public accountability, or federal-state-local jurisdictional limits.

## Section 5. Primary Harm Rule

Where multiple systems could lead, lead responsibility shall be assigned according to the primary harm or authority at issue, including rights, elections, technology, agency action, fiscal authority, emergency power, or constitutional framework.

## Section 6. Emergency Provisional Lead Assignment

Where urgent harm requires immediate action and lead-system responsibility is disputed, a provisional lead may be assigned for temporary action.

A provisional lead assignment must be:

1. public where possible;
2. time-limited;
3. reviewable;
4. narrowly scoped;
5. subject to correction;
6. followed by final lead assignment review.

A provisional assignment shall not be used to evade rights, integrity, technology, fiscal, emergency, or federalism review.

## Section 7. Joint Review for Equal Multi-System Harm

Where two or more systems are equally and materially implicated, constitutional statute may provide joint review panels.

A joint review panel must still identify one public accountable owner for implementation and public communication.

## Section 8. Public Jurisdiction Maps

Each system shall publish plain-language maps showing:

1. what it does;
2. who leads;
3. where to file claims;
4. how to appeal;
5. how to audit;
6. how state and local nodes connect.

## Section 9. Jurisdiction Map Audit

Public jurisdiction maps shall be audited for accuracy, usability, accessibility, non-digital availability, and timeliness.

Maps shall identify accountable offices, not merely broad systems.

## Section 10. Wrong-Door Transfer

A person who makes a good-faith filing, appeal, complaint, or request through the wrong system or office shall not lose rights or remedies solely because of the filing location.

The receiving office must transfer or route the matter to the proper forum where the claim is timely and plausible.

# Article VII — Democratic System

## Section 1. Purpose

The Democratic System protects representation, competition, participation, and peaceful transfer of power.

## Section 2. Election Administration

Election administration belongs to nonpartisan Stewardship Power.

It shall be:

1. independent;
2. transparent;
3. auditable;
4. rights-compliant;
5. insulated from partisan control;
6. subject to judicial review.

## Section 3. Electoral Design

The Commonwealth shall use mixed-member proportional representation unless amended through the election and power rules procedure.

The electoral system shall include:

1. two votes where constitutionally practicable;
2. local representation;
3. proportional correction;
4. independent boundary drawing;
5. fair ballot access;
6. public party role assignment;
7. public coalition disclosure;
8. delayed self-dealing protection.

Final parameters shall be defined by constitutional statute.

## Section 4. Minimum Electoral Guarantees

The electoral system shall preserve:

1. meaningful local representation;
2. proportional correction sufficient to prevent systematic distortion;
3. realistic opportunity for emergent parties;
4. protected opposition rights;
5. public party status ledgers;
6. independent election administration;
7. transparent districting;
8. public explanation of ballot mechanics.

## Section 5. Parameter Safeguards

Changes to district/list ratio, thresholds, list rules, party finance, districting, coalition rules, or public funding rules shall follow election and power rule safeguards.

## Section 6. Electoral Parameter Review

After the first two election cycles under a major electoral design, the Democratic System shall conduct a public Electoral Parameter Review.

The review shall assess:

1. proportionality;
2. governability;
3. party competition;
4. emergent-party access;
5. regional representation;
6. voter comprehension;
7. ballot usability;
8. coalition formation;
9. district fairness;
10. public trust.

The review may recommend parameter changes, but changes to election and power rules remain subject to enhanced constitutional safeguards.

## Section 7. Citizen Participation

The Democratic System may use citizen assemblies, public comment periods, civic forums, referendums for constitutional questions, petitions, and public consultation.

Citizen participation supplements representative government. It does not replace rights, courts, elections, or lawful institutions.

## Section 8. Citizen Participation Standards

Citizen participation mechanisms shall include integrity standards appropriate to their role, including where relevant:

1. transparent selection;
2. fair access;
3. compensation where burdensome;
4. public mandate;
5. public record;
6. nonpartisan facilitation;
7. clear legal effect;
8. protection against capture.

## Section 9. Citizen Assembly Integrity Rule

Citizen assemblies used for high-impact public questions shall include:

1. fair selection or sortition safeguards;
2. conflict rules;
3. participant support;
4. public mandate;
5. balanced briefing materials;
6. independent facilitation;
7. public report;
8. clear statement of legal effect.

## Section 10. Referendum Rights Limit

Referendums, initiatives, or direct public votes may not override the Democratic Kernel, rights floor, due process, equal citizenship, lawful opposition, judicial independence, or peaceful transfer of power.

# Article VIII — Party Power Roles

## Section 1. Recognized Roles

The Commonwealth recognizes five public power functions:

1. Governing Power;
2. Opposition Power;
3. Plurality Power;
4. Emergent Power;
5. Stewardship Power.

Stewardship Power is nonpartisan constitutional function, not a political party role.

## Section 2. Governing Power

The Governing Power is the party or coalition with the lawful governing mandate.

A lawful governing mandate means confidence-backed or constitutionally recognized authority to form government under the electoral and coalition rules.

The Governing Power may govern, propose budgets, appoint executive officials subject to law, and implement policy.

It may not own election machinery, weaken opposition rights, capture stewardship systems, or change power rules for immediate benefit.

## Section 3. Opposition Power

The Opposition Power is the largest non-governing party or coalition.

It receives protected authority to audit, investigate, question, challenge, publish minority reports, participate in emergency review, and access budget and government information.

## Section 4. Confidence-and-Supply Status

A party or bloc that enters a formal confidence-and-supply arrangement with the Governing Power shall not automatically qualify as Opposition Power.

Constitutional statute shall define how confidence-and-supply affects Opposition, Plurality, and Emergent classification.

## Section 5. Plurality Power

Plurality Power belongs to qualifying minority blocs beyond the principal opposition.

It broadens debate and receives agenda time, proportional committee participation, minority impact statement rights, and review-trigger authority where defined by law.

## Section 6. Emergent Power

Emergent Power protects new, regional, reformist, or growing parties.

It provides lawful paths to ballot access, debate access, limited public funding, and progression into Plurality Power.

## Section 7. Stewardship Power

Stewardship Power is nonpartisan.

It protects elections, courts, public audit, integrity systems, technology governance, emergency review, fiscal scoring, and transfer of power.

Stewardship protects the democratic game. It does not play the game.

## Section 8. Party Role Assignment

Party roles shall be assigned by public support, coalition status, electoral results, common-control findings, constitutional compliance, and public qualification thresholds.

Constitutional statute shall define thresholds and procedures for:

1. Governing Power;
2. Opposition Power;
3. Plurality Power;
4. Emergent Power;
5. provisional status during dispute;
6. appeal.

## Section 9. Provisional Party Role Assignment

After certified election results, the Democratic System shall issue provisional party role assignments within a defined period.

Provisional assignments shall govern public accountability, debate, funding, committee participation, and coalition transparency until final assignment or lawful correction.

## Section 10. Enforcement

Violation of Governing, Opposition, Plurality, or Emergent rights may be challenged before the Election and Party Court or Constitutional Court where appropriate.

# Article IX — Party Governance Guardrails

## Section 1. Anti-Duopoly

The electoral system shall not force citizens into two permanent political camps.

## Section 2. Anti-Cartel

No fixed set of parties may become permanent constitutional gatekeepers.

## Section 3. Common Control

Parties may be treated as affiliated if they share material funding, leadership, vendors, data systems, donor networks, candidate pipelines, strategic control, or other evidence of common political control.

## Section 4. Common Control Evidence Standard

A common-control finding must be based on substantial evidence of material shared control, coordination, or dependency.

Relevant evidence may include:

1. shared major funders;
2. shared executive leadership;
3. shared vendors;
4. shared data infrastructure;
5. coordinated candidate placement;
6. undisclosed coalition agreements;
7. common strategic direction;
8. shared policy-drafting infrastructure;
9. recurring leadership exchange;
10. concealed financial dependency.

Ideological similarity alone is insufficient.

## Section 5. Common Control Process

Common-control findings require:

1. evidence-based determination;
2. public reasoning;
3. opportunity to respond;
4. appeal path;
5. proportionate remedy.

## Section 6. Provisional Status During Appeal

Where a party-status, common-control, funding, debate-access, or party-role finding is appealed, constitutional statute shall provide provisional treatment that preserves democratic competition while preventing evasion.

## Section 7. Common Control Remedies

Common-control remedies may include aggregation for public funding, debate access, committee allocation, Plurality Power qualification, Emergent Power qualification, or coalition leverage calculation.

A common-control finding shall not automatically dissolve a party or suppress lawful political association.

## Section 8. Proportional Leverage

Coalition influence shall remain broadly proportional to public support.

Minority parties may shape government. They may not use coalition leverage to obtain control over constitutional machinery, including elections, courts, integrity systems, technology stewardship, fiscal scoring, emergency renewal, or constitutional appointments.

## Section 9. Public Party Status Ledger

After every election or coalition change, the Commonwealth shall publish a plain-language Public Party Status Ledger showing:

1. governing bloc;
2. principal opposition;
3. plurality blocs;
4. emergent parties;
5. coalition agreement;
6. committee control;
7. public funding;
8. debate access;
9. compliance status;
10. common-control findings.

The Ledger shall be published within a defined period after certification or coalition change and shall be subject to appeal.

## Section 10. Coalition Side Agreement Disclosure

Coalition agreements and material side agreements affecting offices, budgets, committee control, appointments, public funding, procurement, regional concessions, or constitutional procedure must be disclosed.

Secret coalition terms affecting public authority are unenforceable and subject to integrity review.

## Section 11. Ideological Neutrality

Party regulation may target corruption, coercion, violence, foreign control, shell-party manipulation, election sabotage, refusal of lawful transfer, and anti-constitutional action.

It may not suppress lawful dissent, unpopular ideology, peaceful radicalism, criticism of government, or constitutional reform advocacy alone.

# Article X — Legislature

## Section 1. Chambers

The legislature shall contain:

1. the People’s Chamber;
2. the Regional Chamber;
3. a Civic Review Mechanism.

## Section 2. People’s Chamber

The People’s Chamber shall provide population-based representation and ordinary legislative authority.

The People’s Chamber shall ordinarily originate:

1. budgets;
2. ordinary legislation;
3. confidence proceedings;
4. executive oversight;
5. taxation measures, subject to Regional Chamber participation where federal balance is affected.

## Section 3. Regional Chamber

The Regional Chamber shall represent state or regional governments and preserve federal balance.

The Regional Chamber shall have defined authority over:

1. federal-state-local allocation of authority;
2. constitutional statutes affecting state or regional powers;
3. stewardship appointments requiring regional confirmation;
4. national uniformity beyond minimum floors;
5. intergovernmental compacts;
6. framework amendments requiring regional ratification;
7. emergency renewals affecting state or regional authority.

## Section 4. Joint and Chamber-Specific Powers

Constitutional statute shall define chamber-specific and joint powers for:

1. budgets;
2. taxation;
3. appointments;
4. treaties or external commitments;
5. emergency renewal;
6. federal-state disputes;
7. constitutional amendments;
8. major statutory architecture.

## Section 5. Civic Review Mechanism

The Civic Review Mechanism may review, delay, request explanation, or trigger public scrutiny for high-impact legislation.

It shall not function as a permanent veto chamber.

## Section 6. High-Impact Legislation

High-impact legislation includes measures with material rights impact, fiscal scale, emergency authority, constitutional structure, technology risk, environmental or infrastructure impact, or intergenerational burden.

## Section 7. High-Impact Classification Review

A dispute over whether legislation is high-impact shall be reviewable through a fast legislative classification process.

The classification process shall not be used to indefinitely delay ordinary legislation.

## Section 8. Civic Review Integrity Rule

The Civic Review Mechanism shall operate under integrity standards, including:

1. fair selection or sortition safeguards where citizen review is used;
2. conflict rules;
3. compensation where participation is burdensome;
4. balanced briefing materials;
5. public mandate;
6. independent facilitation;
7. public record;
8. time-limited review;
9. public findings;
10. clear statement of legal effect.

## Section 9. Civic Review Limits

Civic Review delay shall be time-limited, publicly reasoned, and subject to legislative override according to law.

Repeated or bad-faith Civic Review triggers may be procedurally limited by neutral rule.

## Section 10. Legislative Powers

The legislature may pass laws, approve budgets, authorize taxation, oversee the executive, confirm major appointments, renew emergency powers, investigate public authority, and amend the constitutional framework through prescribed procedures.

## Section 11. Legislative Investigation Due Process

Legislative investigations shall have public purpose, defined scope, procedural fairness, evidence standards, privacy safeguards, and protection against harassment.

Subpoenas, compelled testimony, confidential records, and private-person investigations shall be governed by law and subject to review.

## Section 12. Legislative Limits

The legislature may not:

1. abolish the Democratic Kernel;
2. cancel elections for ordinary political convenience;
3. eliminate opposition rights;
4. place election machinery under party control;
5. authorize permanent emergency rule;
6. legalize secret public authority;
7. use procedure to permanently silence recognized Opposition or Plurality rights.

## Section 13. Oversight Allocation

Key oversight committees shall include meaningful Opposition and Plurality participation.

Constitutional statute shall identify oversight bodies that may not be controlled exclusively by the Governing Power.

# Article XI — Executive

## Section 1. Executive Structure

The executive shall consist of:

1. Chief Executive or Prime Minister;
2. Governing Council or Cabinet;
3. Civic President or Constitutional Steward;
4. Emergency Council.

## Section 2. Chief Executive

The Chief Executive derives authority from the Governing Power.

The Chief Executive may administer law, propose budgets, coordinate policy, direct executive agencies, and respond to crises under law.

The Chief Executive may not control election administration, ignore courts, suspend opposition rights, politicize security forces, extend emergency authority unilaterally, or use public technology for partisan advantage.

## Section 3. Constructive No-Confidence Minimum Rule

A no-confidence motion against the Chief Executive or governing administration must identify a replacement governing bloc or transition mechanism sufficient to maintain lawful continuity.

Constitutional statute shall define:

1. who may file a motion;
2. required support threshold;
3. debate period;
4. vote threshold;
5. replacement certification;
6. interim administration rules;
7. limits on repeated bad-faith motions.

## Section 4. Civic President

The Civic President is nonpartisan.

The office may certify lawful transfer of power, convene constitutional review during crisis, refer constitutional violations for review, and represent continuity of the Commonwealth.

The office may not govern ordinary policy.

The selection, term, removal, and nonpartisanship standards of the Civic President shall be defined by constitutional statute.

## Section 5. Civic President Selection Principles

The Civic President shall be selected through a process designed to produce cross-bloc, regional, and public legitimacy.

The selection process shall not allow a single governing party or coalition to appoint the office unilaterally.

The office shall be subject to:

1. fixed term;
2. nonpartisanship duties;
3. conflict disclosure;
4. removal for cause;
5. prohibition on ordinary policy control;
6. public reasoning for constitutional referrals.

## Section 6. Emergency Council

The Emergency Council is temporary, legally scoped, time-limited, and subordinate to emergency law.

It may coordinate emergency response. It may not create permanent authority, suspend elections, govern ordinary policy, or bypass courts.

## Section 7. Emergency Council Activation Rule

Emergency Council activation requires:

1. lawful emergency declaration;
2. defined emergency type;
3. named accountable executive;
4. defined membership;
5. scope of authority;
6. duration;
7. reporting duty;
8. rights review;
9. fiscal review where spending is implicated;
10. normalcy restoration plan.

Emergency Council authority expires with the emergency authority that created it unless separately renewed by law.

## Section 8. Removal

Executive authority may be ended through regular election, constructive no-confidence, impeachment, incapacity process, resignation, or lawful judicial finding where narrowly defined.

## Section 9. Judicial Removal Limits

Judicial removal findings shall be limited to incapacity, unlawful usurpation, grave constitutional violation, or other narrowly defined constitutional cause.

## Section 10. Executive Accountability Duty

The executive shall maintain public accountability records and comply with lawful court, legislative, and stewardship orders.

## Section 11. Executive Noncompliance Escalation

Failure by the executive to comply with final lawful orders may trigger:

1. public notice;
2. compliance deadline;
3. legislative oversight;
4. court enforcement;
5. integrity review;
6. impeachment or removal process where bad faith, corruption, or constitutional violation exists.

# Article XII — Rights and Justice System

## Section 1. Judicial Authority

Judicial authority shall protect this Constitution, the rights floor, due process, and legality of public power.

## Section 2. Court Structure

The court system shall include:

1. Constitutional Court;
2. High Court or Supreme Appellate Court;
3. Administrative Courts;
4. Fast Rights Tribunal;
5. Election and Party Court;
6. Technology Review Tribunal.

## Section 3. Constitutional Court

The Constitutional Court shall protect the Democratic Kernel, constitutional framework, federal structure, emergency limits, and rights floor.

It may not govern ordinary policy where lawful democratic options remain.

## Section 4. Fast Rights Tribunal

The Fast Rights Tribunal shall hear urgent rights claims where delay could cause serious or irreversible harm.

## Section 5. Serious Claim Gate and Narrow Relief

Fast rights relief requires plausible serious rights harm, urgency, connection to public authority, inadequate ordinary remedy, and proportional relief.

Courts shall use the narrowest effective remedy where possible.

## Section 6. Remedial Hierarchy Rule

Courts shall generally prefer the least intrusive remedy sufficient to cure the violation.

Remedies may include:

1. notice;
2. disclosure;
3. procedural correction;
4. reconsideration;
5. individual relief;
6. limited class relief;
7. narrow injunction;
8. broader injunction where systemic harm exists;
9. compliance plan;
10. structural remedy only where necessary to correct persistent constitutional failure.

Courts shall leave lawful policy choices to democratic institutions where multiple constitutional options remain.

## Section 7. Election and Party Court

The Election and Party Court shall resolve election disputes, districting challenges, party role disputes, certification disputes, and related democratic competition matters.

## Section 8. Election and Emergency Review Timing

Constitutional statute shall establish accelerated timelines for:

1. election certification disputes;
2. ballot access disputes;
3. districting disputes;
4. party role disputes;
5. emergency-power challenges;
6. urgent rights claims.

Timelines shall be fast enough to preserve practical remedies and disciplined enough to prevent certification sabotage.

## Section 9. Technology Review Tribunal

The Technology Review Tribunal shall resolve disputes involving public algorithms, automated decision systems, public AI, surveillance tools, and digital public authority.

## Section 10. One-Door Filing

A timely and plausible claim filed in the wrong tribunal in good faith shall be transferred to the proper tribunal, not dismissed.

## Section 11. Primary Harm Rule

Jurisdiction shall be assigned according to primary harm: rights, election, technology, agency action, constitutional framework, or ordinary civil/criminal dispute.

## Section 12. Judicial Restraint

Courts shall remedy illegality, rights violations, procedural failures, and constitutional defects.

Courts shall not choose ordinary policy where lawful democratic choices remain.

## Section 13. Independent Technical Expert Pool

Technology-related cases shall have access to conflict-screened, rotating, independent technical expertise subject to adversarial challenge.

The expert pool shall include public-interest, academic, civil-society, and technical expertise and shall be governed by conflict disclosure and cooling-off rules.

## Section 14. Court Order Enforcement

Failure to comply with final constitutional or rights orders shall trigger public notice, compliance deadlines, responsible-officer identification, remedial order, and escalation where bad faith persists.

## Section 15. Court Compliance Dashboard

The Public Constitutional Interface shall include a court compliance dashboard showing final rights and constitutional orders, responsible offices, compliance deadlines, status, and lawful exceptions.

## Section 16. Judicial Structure Change Rule

Changes to court size, jurisdiction, appointment process, tenure, tribunal powers, or case-allocation rules require enhanced process, public impact statement, delayed effect where power rules are implicated, and constitutional review where Kernel rights are affected.

# Article XIII — Integrity System

## Section 1. Purpose

The Integrity System prevents corruption, capture, hidden influence, and misuse of public authority.

## Section 2. Functions

It shall oversee lobbying, ethics, conflicts of interest, procurement, public spending, campaign finance, party finance, whistleblower protection, influence disclosure, and public audits.

## Section 3. Public Case Standards

Integrity bodies shall publish case-opening standards, enforcement statistics, sanction categories, and appeal paths.

Case-opening standards must balance public accountability with protection against frivolous, retaliatory, or politically timed abuse.

## Section 4. Influence Ledger

Major legislation shall include a public influence ledger showing lobbying contacts, draft language sources, major donors, affiliated advocacy groups, party finance relationships, procurement beneficiaries, conflicts, late amendments, and common-control findings where relevant.

## Section 5. Integrity Sanctions

Sanctions shall distinguish clerical defect, curable error, negligence, bad faith, corruption, foreign or covert influence, and abuse of office.

Punish corruption severely. Cure honest mistakes fairly.

## Section 6. Cure and Appeal

Minor defects shall receive cure periods where consistent with public integrity.

Integrity findings and sanctions shall have appeal paths and public reasoning.

## Section 7. Severe Sanctions

Severe integrity sanctions affecting candidacy, party status, office eligibility, public funding, or constitutional office shall require:

1. public reasoning;
2. evidence standard defined by law;
3. opportunity to respond;
4. independent review;
5. appeal;
6. proportionality.

## Section 8. Whistleblower Protection

Whistleblowers reporting corruption, abuse, hidden influence, unlawful surveillance, procurement fraud, fiscal concealment, algorithmic abuse, or emergency overreach shall be protected against retaliation.

## Section 9. Protected Whistleblower Channels

The Commonwealth shall maintain protected reporting channels for whistleblowers, including confidential channels where lawful.

Whistleblower protections shall include:

1. anti-retaliation remedies;
2. protection against employment punishment;
3. protection against clearance or access retaliation where applicable;
4. confidentiality safeguards;
5. independent review;
6. safe reporting for classified or sensitive matters under law.

## Section 10. False or Bad-Faith Reports

False or bad-faith reports may be sanctioned after due process.

Mistaken but good-faith reports shall not be treated as bad faith solely because allegations are not proven.

## Section 11. Pre-Election Integrity Claims

Integrity claims materially affecting candidates, parties, ballot access, public funding, or election timing shall receive expedited process sufficient to protect both electoral integrity and due process.

## Section 12. Audit-the-Auditors

Integrity bodies shall be subject to transparent case standards, enforcement statistics, appeal, staggered appointments, external review, budget protection, and removal for cause.

Criminal punishment remains within judicial process.

# Article XIV — Stewardship Appointments

## Section 1. Covered Institutions

This Article applies to stewardship bodies, including election stewardship, integrity, technology stewardship, resilience, public audit, civil service, judicial appointments, and the public constitutional interface.

## Section 2. Appointment Standard

No single party, branch, level of government, profession, vendor network, or donor network may control a stewardship institution.

## Section 3. Appointment Process

Stewardship appointments shall use:

1. multi-source nomination;
2. plural qualification review;
3. public independence-risk statements;
4. citizen review panels;
5. enhanced confirmation;
6. staggered terms;
7. cause-based removal;
8. budget sufficiency review;
9. conflict and cooling-off rules.

## Section 4. Enhanced Confirmation

Enhanced confirmation shall require supermajority or cross-bloc approval defined by constitutional statute.

## Section 5. Default Deadlock Sequence

If constitutional statute does not provide a specific deadlock sequence, the default sequence shall include:

1. standard confirmation vote;
2. public reasons for rejection;
3. reopened shortlist;
4. ranked list from qualification panel;
5. temporary acting appointment from prequalified pool;
6. final fallback selection from a highly qualified publicly vetted shortlist by neutral method.

The fallback method shall not allow unilateral control by the Governing Power.

## Section 6. Acting Appointment Limit

Acting stewardship officials may serve only for a limited period defined by law.

An acting official may not make major structural, enforcement, or appointment decisions except where necessary to preserve continuity, rights, elections, security, or legal duties.

## Section 7. Composition Balance

Stewardship bodies shall not be dominated by one political, financial, professional, vendor, donor, sectoral, or regional bloc.

## Section 8. Composition Balance Review

Disputes over composition balance shall be reviewable through a neutral process.

Composition balance shall not be used to exclude nominees based solely on lawful viewpoint, criticism of government, or unpopular but lawful background.

## Section 9. Independence-Risk Publication

Every stewardship nominee shall receive a public independence-risk statement disclosing material ties to parties, donors, vendors, regulated entities, public contractors, advocacy networks, or other relevant interests.

## Section 10. Citizen Review Panel Standards

Citizen review panels shall be selected through fair and transparent process and shall review public trust, conflicts, civic explanation, accessibility commitments, and legitimacy concerns.

Citizen panels shall not replace technical qualification review unless separately authorized and qualified.

## Section 11. Performance Failure Review

Stewardship independence does not prevent review for persistent failure of duty, missed reporting, repeated unlawful decisions, severe public-interface failure, or chronic nonperformance.

## Section 12. Bad-Faith Removal Petitions

Bad-faith removal petitions intended to intimidate or retaliate against stewardship officials may be sanctioned.

## Section 13. Stewardship Classification Rule

Stewardship bodies may classify legality, compliance, risk, integrity, fiscal exposure, technical safety, rights impact, procedural validity, and emergency authority status.

They may not choose ordinary policy values, favor a party, govern in place of elected institutions, or define lawful ideology.

## Section 14. Stewardship Finding Appeal

Major stewardship findings that block, suspend, materially burden, or publicly sanction public authority shall include public reasoning and appeal or review path.

## Section 15. Limits

Stewardship institutions may audit, disclose, review, certify, block unlawful action, trigger judicial or legislative review, and enforce neutral rules.

They may not govern in place of elected institutions.

# Article XV — Technology and Algorithmic Governance

## Section 1. Purpose

The Technology System governs public use of AI, algorithms, data, surveillance systems, automated decision tools, public digital infrastructure, cyber systems, and public technology procurement.

## Section 2. Algorithmic Accountability

No public algorithm may materially affect rights, benefits, liberty, legal status, surveillance, enforcement, public access, voting access, or public service priority without:

1. legal basis;
2. public registry;
3. named human owner;
4. audit path;
5. appeal path;
6. rights review;
7. model versioning;
8. meaningful human review.

## Section 3. Material Influence Test

An automated system materially affects a decision when it changes or materially shapes eligibility, ranking, risk score, enforcement priority, benefit access, service access, surveillance targeting, legal status, public obligation, or likelihood of human approval.

## Section 4. Meaningful Human Review

Human review must be staffed, trained, authorized, timely, documented, accessible, auditable, and capable of reversing or modifying the automated outcome.

A decorative human signature is not review.

## Section 5. No Black-Box Public Authority

No unauditable system may make or materially shape rights-impacting public decisions.

Trade secrecy, vendor control, or technical complexity shall not defeat public audit, court review, or rights protection.

## Section 6. Prohibited Automated Actions

No algorithm may independently:

1. change voting eligibility;
2. certify elections;
3. remove officials;
4. suspend rights;
5. issue criminal punishment;
6. authorize surveillance without legal process;
7. rewrite law;
8. extend emergency powers;
9. secretly deny benefits without appeal.

## Section 7. Autonomic Governance

Predictive, self-learning, and self-healing systems may improve forecasting, service delivery, corruption detection, policy simulation, infrastructure monitoring, cyber resilience, and administrative repair.

They may not self-amend law, rights, elections, punishment, eligibility, or constitutional authority.

## Section 8. Public System Cards

High-risk public technology systems shall publish system cards describing purpose, legal authority, risk tier, data sources, human owner, audit status, appeal path, limitations, and rollback procedure.

# Article XVI — Distributed Public Ledger System

## Section 1. Purpose

Distributed ledgers may be used as constitutional evidence infrastructure.

They may verify public records. They may not govern the public.

## Section 2. Permitted Uses

Permitted modules include:

1. Public Accountability Ledger;
2. Procurement Integrity Ledger;
3. Legislative Provenance Ledger;
4. Fiscal Flow Ledger;
5. Public Algorithm Registry Ledger;
6. Credential Verification Ledger;
7. Election Audit Ledger.

## Section 3. Prohibited Uses

Distributed ledgers may not replace voting, courts, human review, privacy rights, emergency limits, benefit eligibility, punishment decisions, legal status decisions, or democratic authority.

## Section 4. Legal Truth Supremacy

The ledger proves record history. It does not determine final legal truth.

Courts and lawful authorities may correct or supersede ledger records through transparent correction overlays.

## Section 5. Validator Governance

Validator and protocol governance shall be balanced so that no single party, branch, agency, vendor, region, institutional class, or professional guild controls validation, upgrades, access rules, or correction governance.

## Section 6. Ledger Deployment Review

Before deployment, each ledger module shall receive Technology, Rights and Justice, and Integrity review where relevant.

## Section 7. DLT Necessity and Sunset

Each ledger module shall pass a necessity review showing that distributed verification materially outperforms simpler audited systems.

Each module shall have sunset or reauthorization review.

## Section 8. Oracle Accountability

Every real-world fact entered into a ledger shall have named certifying authority, evidence trail, challenge path, audit path, correction path, and conflict disclosure where relevant.

## Section 9. Ledger Guardrails

Ledger systems shall comply with metadata privacy, public exit and portability, human override, court supremacy, election boundary, plain-language proof, and disclosure quality rules.

# Article XVII — Resilience and Future Generations

## Section 1. Purpose

The Resilience System protects the Commonwealth against systemic and foreseeable risks.

## Section 2. Required Functions

It shall maintain:

1. national risk register;
2. emergency readiness plans;
3. fiscal sustainability analysis;
4. climate and infrastructure stress testing;
5. public health readiness;
6. cyber and systemic shock planning;
7. demographic and labor transition analysis;
8. normalcy restoration plans.

## Section 3. Future Generations

Major laws shall be reviewed for fiscal impact, rights impact, infrastructure impact, climate impact, implementation risk, and intergenerational burden.

## Section 4. Major Law Defined

A major law includes any measure with material fiscal scale, rights impact, emergency relevance, technology risk, infrastructure impact, climate impact, intergenerational burden, or constitutional-system effect.

## Section 5. Response to Resilience Findings

Elected institutions shall publicly respond to major Resilience findings.

Resilience findings are not ordinary policy commands unless constitutional violation, emergency authority, or statutory trigger is present.

## Section 6. Present Rights Priority

Future-generation stewardship shall not justify violation of the rights floor or denial of minimum present public obligations.

## Section 7. Limit

The Resilience System may not become permanent emergency government.

# Article XVIII — Emergency Powers

## Section 1. Activation

Emergency powers may be activated only for severe threats, including war, invasion, major cyberattack, pandemic, natural disaster, infrastructure collapse, constitutional crisis, systemic financial collapse, or comparable threats defined by law.

## Section 2. Comparable Threats

Comparable threats must satisfy severity, necessity, proportionality, and reviewability standards.

## Section 3. Required Terms

Emergency activation must include:

1. legal basis;
2. defined scope;
3. named accountable executive;
4. duration limit;
5. rights impact statement;
6. public notice unless narrowly classified;
7. legislative notification;
8. judicial review availability;
9. normalcy restoration plan.

## Section 4. Expiration

Emergency powers expire automatically unless renewed.

Longer renewal requires stronger legislative approval.

## Section 5. Renewal

Emergency renewal requires public necessity statement, rights review, fiscal review, evidence of continued emergency, and normalcy restoration progress.

## Section 6. Classified Actions

Classified emergency actions require classified legislative and judicial oversight where public disclosure is lawfully limited.

## Section 7. Non-Suspendable Principles

Emergency powers may not abolish due process, court access, lawful opposition, transfer-of-power rules, prohibition on permanent emergency rule, or protection against secret punishment.

# Article XIX — Fiscal Constitution

## Section 1. Fiscal Honesty

Government must account honestly for revenue, spending, debt, public assets, future obligations, pensions, infrastructure maintenance, emergency liabilities, and intergenerational burden.

## Section 2. Required Instruments

The Commonwealth shall maintain:

1. Annual Public Budget;
2. Public Balance Sheet;
3. Long-Term Fiscal Outlook;
4. Independent Fiscal Score;
5. Infrastructure Maintenance Ledger;
6. Pension and Benefit Stress Test;
7. Emergency Spending Ledger;
8. Tax Expenditure Ledger;
9. Procurement Ledger;
10. Intergenerational Impact Statement;
11. Local Fiscal Distress Register.

## Section 3. Cost of Action and Inaction

Fiscal review must assess both the cost of action and the cost of inaction.

## Section 4. Economic Substance

Public obligations must be disclosed based on real fiscal risk, not legal form.

## Section 5. Assumption Transparency

Fiscal scores shall publish assumptions, data sources, confidence ranges, sensitivity analysis, alternative scenarios, discount rates where used, risk bands, dissenting technical notes where material, and model limitations.

## Section 6. Tax Expenditure Sunset

Tax expenditures shall have expiration or periodic review, public cost estimate, beneficiary analysis, performance evaluation, and influence disclosure where material.

## Section 7. Local Fiscal Distress

The Commonwealth shall maintain a local fiscal distress framework including early warning, technical assistance, service continuity, anti-corruption audit, rights safeguards, and restructuring path where necessary.

## Section 8. Stewardship Funding

A budget cut is unconstitutional if it materially prevents a stewardship body from performing required constitutional duties without lawful, non-retaliatory justification.

## Section 9. Fiscal Purpose

Fiscal governance is truth machinery, not austerity machinery.

# Article XX — Public Service and Anti-Capture

## Section 1. Fiduciary Duty

Public office is a fiduciary trust, not private property.

## Section 2. Public Service Standards

Officials may be required to comply with conflict disclosures, asset disclosures, ethics training, constitutional training, blind trusts or index-only holding rules, stock-trading restrictions, gift bans, revolving-door restrictions, and public integrity oaths.

## Section 3. Beneficial Ownership and Affiliated Entities

Conflict disclosure may include beneficial ownership, affiliated entities, family conflicts where material, trusts, nonprofits, consulting arrangements, and other structures used to conceal material interest.

## Section 4. Term and Rotation Rules

The Commonwealth may impose executive term limits, consecutive legislative term limits, committee leadership rotation, party leadership rotation for public funding eligibility, stewardship office staggered terms, and cooling-off periods before lobbying.

## Section 5. Public Trust License

Certain high offices may require a Public Trust License based on constitutional knowledge, ethics certification, conflict disclosure, audit agreement, anti-corruption oath, and post-service limits.

The license may not exclude candidates based on ideology alone.

## Section 6. Licensing Due Process

Public Trust License denial, suspension, or revocation shall include notice, reason, cure opportunity where appropriate, appeal, and ideological neutrality.

## Section 7. Revolving Door

Post-service restrictions may include lobbying, access brokerage, paid strategic advising, vendor employment, regulated-sector employment, and shadow lobbying related to former authority.

# Article XXI — Information Integrity

## Section 1. Free Expression

The Commonwealth protects speech, disagreement, criticism, satire, parody, dissent, and peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 2. Disclosure Rules

The Commonwealth may require transparency for political ad funding, synthetic media, coordinated influence campaigns, platform amplification systems, media ownership, foreign-linked political communication, and major political data operations.

## Section 3. Manipulation Defined

Covered manipulation may include funding opacity, synthetic deception, foreign control, coordinated inauthentic behavior, material non-disclosure, or fraudulent impersonation.

## Section 4. Default Remedy

Disclosure shall be the default remedy.

Suppression shall be limited to narrow unlawful categories defined by law and subject to independent review.

## Section 5. Viewpoint Neutrality

Information-integrity rules shall not discriminate based on lawful viewpoint.

## Section 6. Limit

The state shall not create a monopoly on truth.

# Article XXII — Security Neutrality

## Section 1. Civilian Control

Military, police, intelligence, and emergency forces are subordinate to lawful constitutional civilian authority.

## Section 2. Lawful Command

Security forces owe obedience to constitutional authority, not personal, partisan, unlawful, or anti-constitutional orders.

## Section 3. Nonpartisanship

Security forces may not serve a party, interfere in elections, intimidate opposition, suppress lawful protest, conduct unlawful surveillance, or enforce emergency orders beyond legal scope.

## Section 4. Surveillance

Surveillance shall require legal authority, court supervision where required, minimization, audit, and remedy for abuse.

## Section 5. Oversight

Security institutions are subject to legislative oversight, judicial review, inspector oversight, whistleblower protection, public reporting where security permits, and strict domestic deployment rules.

## Section 6. Security Whistleblowers

Security personnel shall have protected channels for reporting unlawful orders, illegal surveillance, election interference, corruption, abuse, or emergency overreach, subject to lawful classified-handling safeguards.

# Article XXIII — Amendment Rules

## Section 1. Ordinary Policy

Ordinary policy may be changed through normal legislative procedure.

## Section 2. Major Statutory Architecture

Major statutory architecture requires enhanced majority, public review, fiscal analysis, and rights analysis.

Major statutory architecture includes election administration, courts, stewardship systems, emergency powers, fiscal constitution, technology governance, civil service, party finance, and public accountability infrastructure.

## Section 3. Election and Power Rules

Changes to election rules, party rules, districting, office powers, court structure, campaign finance, emergency authority, or public funding rules require:

1. enhanced majority;
2. independent review;
3. public plain-language impact statement;
4. delayed effect after an intervening election.

## Section 4. Amendment Classification Review

Disputes over amendment category shall be reviewable before constitutional adoption.

## Section 5. Framework Amendments

Framework amendments require supermajority, election delay, public referendum or regional ratification, and rights review.

## Section 6. Kernel Protection

The Democratic Kernel may not be removed through ordinary amendment.

Full constitutional refounding is required to alter the Kernel.

## Section 7. Emergency Amendment Limit

Emergency conditions shall not be used to alter election or power rules except for narrow continuity measures subject to review and delayed self-dealing protections.

## Section 8. Public Amendment Guides

All proposed amendments shall be accompanied by plain-language public explanation.

# Article XXIV — Transition and Implementation

## Section 1. Transition Doctrine

The transition to this Constitution shall be lawful, staged, rights-preserving, capacity-gated, auditable, anti-sabotage, publicly legible, and legacy-continuous.

## Section 2. Transition Phases

The Commonwealth shall transition through phases:

1. Kernel Ratification;
2. Rights, Accountability, and Fiscal Baseline;
3. Election Continuity and Reform Preparation;
4. Stewardship Appointments;
5. Court Capacity and Rights Enforcement;
6. First Electoral Transition Cycle;
7. Full Fiscal Constitution;
8. Technology Freeze, Register, and Audit;
9. Resilience System Buildout;
10. Distributed Public Ledger Modules;
11. Federal, State, and Local Node Alignment.

## Section 3. No Gap Rules

During transition there shall be no rights gap, accountability gap, election gap, court gap, data gap, funding gap, or public access gap.

## Section 4. Legacy Continuity

Existing lawful institutions shall continue performing essential functions until successors are operational, funded, audited, and publicly accessible.

## Section 5. Transition Anti-Sabotage

Bad-faith delay, defunding, disabling, or manipulation of constitutionally required transition duties shall be reviewable and correctable.

## Section 6. Transition Authority Limitation

Transition bodies may coordinate implementation, publish schedules, manage migration, audit readiness, and identify risks.

They may not rewrite constitutional substance, suspend rights, alter election outcomes, choose party winners, bypass courts, appoint permanent stewards outside approved process, extend emergency powers, or replace elected authority.

## Section 7. Public Transition Ledger

Every transition step shall be published with responsible office, legal authority, deadline, status, budget, dependencies, risks, delay explanation if any, appeal path, and next review date.

## Section 8. Readiness Certification

Each phase shall receive readiness certification before full activation.

Readiness shall consider legal completion, rights impact, fiscal readiness, institutional capacity, local capacity, technology readiness, court capacity, public interface readiness, unresolved disputes, and public understanding.

## Section 9. Technology Freeze and Register

High-risk public algorithms, databases, and vendor systems shall be frozen, registered, risk-classified, audited, and assigned accountable owners before migration or expansion.

## Section 10. First Election Stabilization

The first election under the new electoral design shall include readiness certification, public voter education, sample ballots, district map challenge period, party compliance audit, election technology audit, dispute calendar, public certification timeline, and post-election audit.

## Section 11. Civic Translation

Every transition phase shall include plain-language guidance explaining what changes, who is responsible, how to challenge decisions, and where to get help.

This Constitution is adopted to ensure that public power remains visible, lawful, challengeable, audited, and correctable.

Its shortest promise is:
