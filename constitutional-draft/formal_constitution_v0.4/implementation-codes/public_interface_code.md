## Purpose

This implementation code defines practical standards for the Public Constitutional Interface.

Core rule:

> Public power must be visible, understandable, challengeable, audited, and usable.

---

# I. Interface Mandate

## Section 1. Function

The Public Constitutional Interface exists to make public authority understandable and challengeable.

It shall help people:

1. identify who made a public decision;
2. find the legal basis for public authority;
3. find appeal and challenge paths;
4. identify audit and oversight paths;
5. see public algorithms and technology systems;
6. see active emergency powers;
7. see public party status;
8. see coalition agreements;
9. see public fiscal records;
10. see procurement and spending records;
11. track court compliance;
12. track transition milestones.

## Section 2. Public Usability

The Interface shall be designed for ordinary people, not only lawyers, officials, analysts, or technical experts.

## Section 3. Non-Substitution

The Interface supports public access to rights and accountability.

It does not replace courts, agencies, statutory appeal paths, public records laws, or direct assistance channels.

---

# II. Required Core Modules

## Section 1. Decision Lookup

The Interface shall include a public decision lookup module.

Each material public decision record shall show:

1. decision title;
2. responsible office;
3. accountable owner;
4. legal basis;
5. decision date;
6. affected public function;
7. appeal path;
8. audit path;
9. review or expiration date where applicable;
10. technology involvement where material;
11. conflict disclosure where material;
12. public record location.

## Section 2. Appeal Routing

The Interface shall include an appeal routing module that helps users identify where and how to challenge public authority.

## Section 3. Public Dashboard Hub

The Interface shall include a dashboard hub for major constitutional dashboards.

## Section 4. Records Search

The Interface shall include search across public records, offices, legal authorities, programs, vendors, technologies, fiscal records, emergency powers, and transition milestones.

---

# III. Public Record Schema

## Section 1. Minimum Record Fields

Every material public authority record should use a standard schema with:

1. record_id;
2. title;
3. summary;
4. decision_type;
5. accountable_office;
6. accountable_human_role;
7. legal_basis;
8. legal_basis_summary;
9. decision_date;
10. effective_date;
11. review_date;
12. expiration_date;
13. appeal_path;
14. audit_path;
15. related_documents;
16. related_systems;
17. technology_involved;
18. conflict_disclosures;
19. fiscal_authority;
20. emergency_authority;
21. public_contact;
22. non_digital_access;
23. record_status;
24. last_updated.

## Section 2. Public Identifiers

Records shall use stable public identifiers so they can be cited, tracked, corrected, appealed, and audited.

## Section 3. Version History

Material records shall preserve version history.

## Section 4. Correction Record

Corrections shall identify:

1. original record;
2. correction date;
3. correcting office;
4. reason for correction;
5. legal basis;
6. appeal path where relevant.

---

# IV. Accountable Owner Display

## Section 1. Named Office

The Interface shall display a named accountable office for each material public authority record.

Generic agency names are insufficient where a specific office, program, vendor, automated system, or official role materially owns the decision.

## Section 2. Human Owner

Where technology, contractors, vendors, or public-private partnerships materially shape public authority, the Interface shall display the public human accountability owner.

## Section 3. Shared Responsibility

Where multiple offices are involved, the Interface shall identify:

1. lead accountable office;
2. decision owner;
3. implementation owner;
4. audit owner;
5. appeal forum;
6. supporting offices.

## Section 4. Public Contact

The Interface shall provide a public contact or access channel for each material authority area.

---

# V. Legal Basis Display

## Section 1. Legal Authority

The Interface shall display the legal authority for material public action.

Legal authority may include:

1. constitutional provision;
2. schedule;
3. statute;
4. regulation;
5. court order;
6. emergency order;
7. appropriation;
8. contract or delegation authority;
9. administrative rule;
10. public program authorization.

## Section 2. Plain-Language Summary

Every legal basis display shall include a plain-language explanation of what authority the law gives and what limits apply.

## Section 3. Missing Legal Basis Flag

If legal basis is missing, incomplete, unclear, or unavailable, the record shall be flagged for cure review.

---

# VI. Appeal Routing Module

## Section 1. Required Fields

Appeal routing shall show:

1. first review forum;
2. filing method;
3. filing deadline;
4. required documents;
5. available remedies;
6. escalation path;
7. assistance options;
8. non-digital access;
9. urgent review path where applicable.

## Section 2. User Questions

The routing module should ask plain-language questions such as:

1. What happened?
2. Who made the decision?
3. When did it happen?
4. Are rights, benefits, legal status, enforcement, surveillance, voting, or public services affected?
5. Is there urgent harm?
6. Was technology or an algorithm involved?
7. Is this about an election, emergency, public service, court order, or public record?

## Section 3. Routing Result

The routing result shall identify:

1. likely forum;
2. filing path;
3. urgency level;
4. deadline risk;
5. public assistance channel;
6. wrong-door transfer protection where applicable.

## Section 4. No Legal Advice Representation

The routing module may provide public navigation support but shall not falsely represent itself as personal legal counsel.

---

# VII. Wrong-Door and Transfer Workflow

## Section 1. Good-Faith Filing

Good-faith filings through the wrong public office shall be routed to the correct forum.

## Section 2. Transfer Ticket

The Interface shall generate or display a transfer ticket where a matter is rerouted.

The ticket should include:

1. original submission date;
2. receiving office;
3. transfer office;
4. reason for transfer;
5. new forum;
6. deadline preservation status;
7. tracking number.

## Section 3. Anti-Circular Transfer

The Interface shall detect repeated transfers and flag potential circular routing.

## Section 4. Escalation

If a matter is transferred more than the permitted number of times, a provisional accountable office shall be assigned.

# XV. Accessibility and Non-Digital Access

## Section 1. Accessibility

The Interface shall comply with accessibility standards and support people with disabilities.

## Section 2. Non-Digital Access

No person shall be denied practical access to accountability, appeal, rights, or public-service information solely because the person lacks internet access, digital identity, private devices, technical literacy, or assistive technology.

## Section 3. Assisted Access

The Commonwealth shall provide assisted access channels, including where appropriate:

1. phone support;
2. in-person assistance;
3. paper forms;
4. public kiosks;
5. library or civic access points;
6. translation;
7. disability accommodation;
8. public navigators.

## Section 4. Equal Validity

Non-digital submissions shall be treated as valid where legally sufficient.

---

# XVI. Language, Comprehension, and Civic Design

## Section 1. Plain Language

Major public records shall include plain-language summaries.

## Section 2. Multilingual Access

Multilingual access shall be provided where needed for meaningful public use.

## Section 3. Civic Usability Testing

The Interface shall be tested with ordinary users, including people who are not lawyers, technologists, or government officials.

## Section 4. Public Comprehension Metrics

The Interface shall track whether users can find decisions, identify accountable offices, locate appeal paths, and understand deadlines.

---

# XVII. Privacy, Security, and Redaction

## Section 1. Privacy

Public accountability shall not require disclosure of irrelevant personal details.

## Section 2. Sensitive Information

Sensitive information may be redacted or withheld where lawful and necessary.

## Section 3. Surrogate Explanation

Where details are lawfully withheld, the public record shall provide a surrogate explanation where possible.

## Section 4. Review

Withholding, redaction, or classification shall be reviewable.

---

# XVIII. Cure Workflow

## Section 1. Defect Intake

The Interface shall allow users, auditors, courts, and public offices to flag defective accountability records.

## Section 2. Defect Categories

Defects shall be categorized as:

1. clerical defect;
2. material omission;
3. rights-impacting defect;
4. bad-faith concealment.

## Section 3. Cure Ticket

A cure ticket shall identify:

1. record affected;
2. defect category;
3. responsible office;
4. cure deadline;
5. correction status;
6. escalation path.

## Section 4. Escalation

Uncured material or rights-impacting defects shall be escalated to the proper audit, appeal, court, or integrity path.

---

# XIX. Governance and Audit

## Section 1. Interface Authority

The Public Constitutional Interface Authority shall maintain the Interface.

## Section 2. Independent Audit

The Interface shall receive periodic independent audit for completeness, timeliness, accuracy, accessibility, searchability, plain-language quality, appeal usability, algorithm disclosure, conflict disclosure, emergency visibility, non-digital access, machine readability, and public comprehension.

## Section 3. Public Report

Audit results shall be public.

## Section 4. Correction Plan

Persistent failure requires a public correction plan, timeline, and review.

---
