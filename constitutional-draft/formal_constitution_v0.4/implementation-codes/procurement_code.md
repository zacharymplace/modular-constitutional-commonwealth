## Purpose

This implementation code defines practical procurement standards for public contracts, beneficial ownership, vendor integrity, emergency procurement, change orders, public contract ledgers, audit rights, and anti-capture controls.

Core rule:

> Public procurement buys public capacity. It must not sell public authority.

---

# I. Procurement Mandate

## Section 1. Function

The Procurement Code governs public acquisition of goods, services, systems, infrastructure, technology, emergency supplies, consulting, public-private partnerships, and contracted public functions.

## Section 2. Goals

Procurement shall support:

1. public value;
2. fair competition;
3. transparency;
4. fiscal honesty;
5. conflict control;
6. beneficial ownership visibility;
7. vendor accountability;
8. auditability;
9. rights protection;
10. anti-capture safeguards;
11. service continuity;
12. public trust.

## Section 3. Constitutional Boundary

No procurement contract may transfer public sovereignty, evade public accountability, defeat audit rights, bypass rights review, or create hidden public authority.

---

# II. Covered Procurement

## Section 1. Covered Contracts

This Code applies to material public contracts, including:

1. goods;
2. services;
3. professional services;
4. consulting;
5. technology systems;
6. AI and algorithmic systems;
7. surveillance systems;
8. cybersecurity systems;
9. infrastructure;
10. emergency contracts;
11. public-private partnerships;
12. leases with fiscal substance;
13. grants or pass-through arrangements where procurement-like;
14. delegated public functions.

## Section 2. High-Risk Procurement

High-risk procurement includes:

1. public technology;
2. rights-impacting systems;
3. security systems;
4. surveillance systems;
5. emergency contracts;
6. major infrastructure;
7. public health systems;
8. public finance systems;
9. election systems;
10. public records systems;
11. sole-source contracts;
12. contracts with significant vendor lock-in risk.

## Section 3. Material Public Function

A contractor performing a material public function is subject to public accountability, audit, appeal support, conflict disclosure, and rights compliance duties.

---

# III. Procurement Lifecycle

## Section 1. Standard Lifecycle

Material procurement shall follow a lifecycle:

1. need identification;
2. market scan;
3. conflict screen;
4. procurement method selection;
5. public solicitation where required;
6. bid or proposal evaluation;
7. award decision;
8. contract publication;
9. performance monitoring;
10. change-order control;
11. payment verification;
12. audit;
13. closeout;
14. post-contract review.

## Section 2. Lifecycle Record

Each material procurement shall maintain a lifecycle record in the Procurement Ledger.

## Section 3. No Fragmentation

Public authorities may not split or fragment procurement to avoid thresholds, review, public posting, audit, or competition.

---

# IV. Need and Public Purpose Statement

## Section 1. Required Statement

Before material procurement, the responsible office shall produce a public purpose statement.

## Section 2. Contents

The statement shall identify:

1. public need;
2. legal authority;
3. responsible office;
4. expected public benefit;
5. estimated cost;
6. funding source;
7. alternatives considered;
8. implementation risk;
9. rights impact where material;
10. technology impact where material;
11. fiscal impact where material;
12. maintenance or operating burden.

## Section 3. No Vendor-Written Need Without Disclosure

If a vendor, consultant, donor network, lobbyist, or outside entity materially drafts or shapes the need statement, that influence shall be disclosed.

---

# V. Procurement Method

## Section 1. Permitted Methods

Procurement methods may include:

1. open competitive bidding;
2. request for proposals;
3. framework agreements;
4. cooperative procurement;
5. small-purchase procedure;
6. emergency procurement;
7. sole-source procurement;
8. pilot or sandbox procurement;
9. public-private partnership process.

## Section 2. Competitive Default

Open competition is the default for material procurement unless a lawful exception applies.

## Section 3. Exception Justification

Any exception to open competition shall include a public justification, legal basis, responsible office, duration, and audit path.

## Section 4. Sole Source

Sole-source procurement requires specific justification and heightened audit where material.

---

# VI. Solicitation Standards

## Section 1. Public Solicitation

Public solicitations shall clearly state:

1. scope;
2. eligibility;
3. evaluation criteria;
4. required disclosures;
5. conflict rules;
6. audit rights;
7. public records duties;
8. data rights where relevant;
9. technology audit rights where relevant;
10. performance milestones;
11. termination rights;
12. protest or challenge path.

## Section 2. Neutral Specifications

Specifications shall not be written to improperly favor a vendor, incumbent, donor, affiliated entity, or preferred contractor.

## Section 3. Vendor-Drafted Specifications

Vendor-drafted or vendor-shaped specifications shall be disclosed.

## Section 4. Accessibility

Solicitations shall be published in accessible public form, with reasonable time for response.

---

# VII. Bid and Proposal Evaluation

## Section 1. Evaluation Criteria

Evaluation criteria shall be public before bids or proposals are reviewed.

## Section 2. Evaluation Record

The evaluation record shall identify:

1. evaluators;
2. scoring criteria;
3. score summary;
4. conflicts disclosed;
5. recusals;
6. clarifications requested;
7. best-value reasoning;
8. award recommendation;
9. dissenting evaluation notes where material.

## Section 3. Conflict Screen

Evaluators shall disclose conflicts before evaluation.

## Section 4. No Secret Criteria

Material award criteria may not be invented after bids are received.

---

# VIII. Beneficial Ownership

## Section 1. Disclosure Requirement

Material vendors shall disclose beneficial ownership where required by law.

## Section 2. Disclosure Scope

Disclosure may include:

1. parent entities;
2. controlling owners;
3. affiliated entities;
4. major subcontractors;
5. shell entities;
6. foreign-linked ownership where relevant;
7. politically exposed persons where relevant;
8. family or official ties where material.

## Section 3. False Ownership Disclosure

False or materially incomplete beneficial ownership disclosure may trigger disqualification, contract correction, termination, repayment, sanction, or integrity referral.

## Section 4. Public Record

Beneficial ownership information shall be public where legally required and privacy/security rules permit.

---

# IX. Vendor Integrity Screen

## Section 1. Integrity Review

Material vendors may be screened for integrity risk.

## Section 2. Risk Factors

Risk factors may include:

1. prior fraud;
2. corruption findings;
3. sanctions;
4. repeated nonperformance;
5. procurement manipulation;
6. false claims;
7. serious cybersecurity failures;
8. unresolved audit findings;
9. hidden beneficial ownership;
10. conflicts of interest;
11. foreign control where relevant;
12. public records noncompliance;
13. labor or rights violations where material.

## Section 3. Proportionality

Integrity review shall distinguish serious integrity risks from minor, dated, cured, or irrelevant issues.

## Section 4. Appeal

Vendor exclusion or major restriction shall include notice, reason, and review path.

---

# X. Conflict Rules

## Section 1. Covered Conflicts

Procurement conflicts may involve:

1. public officials;
2. evaluators;
3. vendors;
4. subcontractors;
5. consultants;
6. lobbyists;
7. former officials;
8. family members where material;
9. affiliated entities;
10. donors;
11. party officers;
12. regulated entities.

## Section 2. Required Mitigation

Conflict mitigation may include disclosure, recusal, firewall, independent review, reassignment, contract modification, or disqualification.

## Section 3. Revolving Door

Former officials may not use prior public authority, confidential knowledge, or access relationships to distort procurement.

## Section 4. Public Disclosure

Material procurement conflicts and mitigation shall be recorded in the Procurement Ledger.

---

# XI. Contract Award

## Section 1. Award Record

Each material award shall publish:

1. winning vendor;
2. beneficial ownership where required;
3. contract amount;
4. contract purpose;
5. contract term;
6. evaluation summary;
7. conflict disclosures;
8. funding source;
9. responsible office;
10. performance milestones;
11. audit rights;
12. protest or challenge path.

## Section 2. Award Explanation

The award explanation shall be plain enough for public review.

## Section 3. Protest Period

Material awards shall include a protest or challenge period unless emergency conditions justify delayed review.

---

# XII. Contract Terms

## Section 1. Required Terms

Material contracts shall include:

1. scope;
2. deliverables;
3. performance standards;
4. payment terms;
5. audit rights;
6. public records duties;
7. conflict duties;
8. beneficial ownership duties;
9. subcontractor disclosure;
10. change-order rules;
11. termination rights;
12. data rights where relevant;
13. technology audit rights where relevant;
14. continuity obligations;
15. exit and portability rights where relevant.

## Section 2. No Waiver of Public Accountability

A contract may not waive public accountability, audit, rights review, court review, or lawful correction duties for material public functions.

## Section 3. No Secret Public Authority

Contract terms may not create hidden public authority.

---

# XIII. Vendor Audit Rights

## Section 1. Audit Access

Material contracts shall provide audit access sufficient to verify cost, performance, compliance, conflicts, public records, technology behavior, security, and public-purpose fulfillment.

## Section 2. Technology Audit

Technology contracts shall include audit rights over documentation, system behavior, data provenance, security, model updates, failure logs, and exit processes where relevant.

## Section 3. Subcontractors

Audit rights shall extend to material subcontractors where necessary.

## Section 4. Refusal

Refusal to provide required audit access may trigger payment pause, corrective order, termination, sanction, or integrity referral.

---

# XIV. Change Orders and Amendments

## Section 1. Public Record

Material change orders and amendments shall be recorded publicly.

## Section 2. Required Fields

Change-order records shall identify:

1. original contract;
2. change description;
3. reason;
4. cost impact;
5. schedule impact;
6. scope impact;
7. approving office;
8. vendor request;
9. conflict review;
10. cumulative change total;
11. audit path.

## Section 3. Change-Order Abuse

Change orders may not be used to evade competition, inflate contract value, alter scope materially, or reward favored vendors without review.

## Section 4. Threshold Review

Cumulative change orders beyond defined thresholds shall trigger heightened review.

---

# XV. Performance Monitoring

## Section 1. Milestones

Material contracts shall include performance milestones.

## Section 2. Monitoring Record

Performance records shall track:

1. milestone status;
2. delivery evidence;
3. payment status;
4. quality issues;
5. complaints;
6. audit findings;
7. corrective actions;
8. delays;
9. responsible office;
10. acceptance certification.

## Section 3. False Certification

False milestone or delivery certification may trigger integrity review, payment recovery, sanction, or legal referral.

## Section 4. Public Service Impact

Where contracts affect public services, performance monitoring shall include service continuity and affected-person impact.

---

# XVI. Payments

## Section 1. Payment Control

Payments shall be tied to legal authority, contract terms, delivery evidence, milestone certification, and fiscal authorization.

## Section 2. Payment Record

Payment records shall identify:

1. contract;
2. vendor;
3. amount;
4. payment date;
5. funding source;
6. milestone or invoice;
7. approving office;
8. audit status;
9. exception where applicable.

## Section 3. Improper Payment

Improper payments may trigger recovery, audit, sanction, or integrity referral.

---

# XVII. Emergency Procurement

## Section 1. Emergency Exception

Emergency procurement may move faster than ordinary procurement but remains accountable.

## Section 2. Required Record

Emergency procurement records shall identify:

1. legal basis;
2. emergency authority;
3. urgency justification;
4. vendor;
5. beneficial ownership where available;
6. conflict review where possible;
7. price reasonableness where possible;
8. delivery milestone;
9. expiration or review date;
10. audit path;
11. exception from ordinary procurement rules.

## Section 3. Post-Emergency Review

Emergency procurement shall receive post-emergency audit and integrity review.

## Section 4. No Permanent Exception

Emergency procurement status shall not continue after emergency need ends.

---

# XVIII. Technology Procurement

## Section 1. High-Risk Technology

High-risk technology procurement shall receive Technology System and Integrity System review before award where practicable.

## Section 2. Required Contract Rights

Technology contracts shall include:

1. audit access;
2. security review;
3. documentation;
4. appeal support;
5. exit rights;
6. portability;
7. model or system evaluation rights;
8. incident reporting;
9. update review;
10. rights-impact assessment.

## Section 3. No Lock-In

Vendor lock-in shall not be permitted for constitutional records, rights-impacting systems, election systems, emergency systems, or critical public infrastructure unless independently justified and reviewable.

## Section 4. No Black-Box Contracting

A contract may not prevent public audit, court review, rights protection, or meaningful human review for rights-impacting technology.

---

# XIX. Public-Private Partnerships

## Section 1. Substance Over Form

Public-private partnerships shall be evaluated by economic substance and public effect, not formal label.

## Section 2. Required Review

Material public-private partnerships shall receive:

1. fiscal review;
2. public purpose review;
3. risk transfer analysis;
4. long-term cost analysis;
5. public accountability review;
6. conflict review;
7. service continuity review;
8. exit review.

## Section 3. Hidden Debt

Public-private partnerships may not be used to hide public obligations, debt, maintenance burdens, guarantees, or public service risk.

## Section 4. Public Authority

If a public-private partnership performs a material public function, public accountability and rights review apply.

---

# XX. Procurement Ledger

## Section 1. Ledger Requirement

The Commonwealth shall maintain a Procurement Ledger for material public contracts.

## Section 2. Required Fields

The Ledger shall include:

1. bid announcement;
2. bidders;
3. award recipient;
4. beneficial ownership;
5. contract amount;
6. contract purpose;
7. contract term;
8. change orders;
9. amendments;
10. emergency exceptions;
11. vendor conflicts;
12. pre-procurement influence where material;
13. performance milestones;
14. payments;
15. audit findings;
16. responsible office.

## Section 3. Public Interface

The Procurement Ledger shall be available through the Public Constitutional Interface.

---

# XXI. Enforcement and Remedies

## Section 1. Procurement Violations

Procurement violations may include:

1. bid manipulation;
2. false ownership disclosure;
3. undisclosed conflict;
4. improper sole source;
5. change-order abuse;
6. emergency procurement abuse;
7. false milestone certification;
8. audit refusal;
9. vendor capture;
10. public records evasion;
11. technology audit evasion.

## Section 2. Remedies

Remedies may include:

1. cure order;
2. public correction;
3. procurement pause;
4. award cancellation;
5. contract modification;
6. repayment;
7. termination;
8. debarment where lawful;
9. integrity referral;
10. civil or criminal referral where lawful.

## Section 3. Due Process

Major sanctions require notice, reasoned explanation, opportunity to respond, proportional remedy, and appeal path.

## Section 1. Periodic Review

This Code shall be reviewed periodically to ensure procurement remains fair, transparent, competitive, fiscally honest, and resistant to capture.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. competition;
2. cost control;
3. beneficial ownership disclosure;
4. conflict disclosure;
5. emergency procurement use;
6. change-order frequency;
7. vendor concentration;
8. audit access;
9. public ledger completeness;
10. service outcomes;
11. technology procurement integrity;
12. public trust.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Code shall follow the process applicable to procurement implementation standards.
