## Purpose

This schedule defines the electoral mechanics for the Commonwealth.

Core rule:

> Representation should be proportional enough to reflect the public, local enough to remain accountable, and structured enough to govern.

---

# I. Electoral System

## Section 1. System Type

The Commonwealth shall use a mixed-member proportional electoral system unless amended through the election and power rules procedure.

## Section 2. Two Votes

Each voter shall cast two votes where constitutionally practicable:

1. a **local representative vote** for a district representative;
2. a **party or proportional vote** that determines overall party strength.

## Section 3. Local Representation

Local representatives shall be elected from districts drawn by an independent boundary process.

## Section 4. Proportional Representation

Proportional seats shall correct disproportionality between local district results and the party/proportional vote.

## Section 5. Public Explanation

Ballots and public election materials shall explain:

1. what the local vote does;
2. what the party/proportional vote does;
3. how proportional correction works;
4. how party roles are assigned;
5. how coalitions may form;
6. how voters may challenge election problems.

---

# II. Seat Structure

## Section 1. Default Seat Ratio

The default legislative seat structure shall be:

| Seat type | Default share |
|---|---:|
| Local district seats | 50% |
| Compensatory proportional seats | 50% |

## Section 2. Permitted Adjustment Range

The district/proportional ratio may be adjusted by constitutional statute within the following presumptive range:

| Option | Use case |
|---|---|
| 60% district / 40% proportional | stronger local representation |
| 50% district / 50% proportional | default balance |
| 40% district / 60% proportional | stronger proportionality |

## Section 3. Safeguard

Any adjustment to the district/proportional ratio must preserve meaningful proportional correction and may not be used for immediate partisan advantage.

## Section 4. Review

The district/proportional ratio shall be reviewed after the first two election cycles under this system and periodically thereafter.

---

# III. Thresholds

## Section 1. Threshold Doctrine

Entry into democratic competition shall be easier than access to formal power, public funding, or constitutional privileges.

## Section 2. Threshold Categories

The electoral system shall distinguish between:

1. ballot access threshold;
2. debate access threshold;
3. limited public funding threshold;
4. legislative representation threshold;
5. Plurality Power threshold;
6. committee rights threshold;
7. coalition governing eligibility;
8. constitutional procedural privilege threshold.

## Section 3. Ballot Access

Ballot access shall require a low but serious showing of public support, such as signatures, membership, prior vote share, or recognized local representation.

Ballot access rules shall not be designed to entrench existing parties.

## Section 4. Debate Access

Debate access may require a moderate public-support threshold based on polling, prior vote share, signatures, regional support, or other transparent criteria.

Debate access rules shall include a path for verified emergent parties.

## Section 5. Public Funding

Public funding shall be staged according to demonstrated public support and compliance.

Funding shall support democratic competition without encouraging fake parties or funding mills.

## Section 6. Legislative Representation Threshold

A party may qualify for proportional legislative seats through:

1. national vote threshold;
2. regional threshold;
3. direct local district victories;
4. other constitutionally authorized mechanisms that demonstrate genuine public support.

## Section 7. Default National Threshold

The default national legislative threshold shall be 4% of the party/proportional vote.

This default may be revised only through the election and power rules procedure.

## Section 8. Default Regional Threshold

The default regional threshold shall be between 8% and 10% within a defined region, as set by constitutional statute.

Regional thresholds shall protect genuine regional representation without enabling hidden regional veto power over national constitutional machinery.

## Section 9. District-Seat Exception

A party that wins local district seats may qualify for representation or proportional adjustment according to constitutional statute, even if it does not meet the national threshold.

## Section 10. Anti-Cartel Review

Thresholds shall be periodically reviewed to ensure they do not become cartel barriers against emergent parties.

---

# IV. Emergent Party Escalator

## Section 1. Purpose

The Emergent Party Escalator gives new, regional, reformist, or growing parties a lawful path into public competition.

## Section 2. Stages

| Stage | Status | Rights |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Registered party | ballot pathway |
| Stage 2 | Verified emergent party | limited debate access |
| Stage 3 | Public support party | limited public funding |
| Stage 4 | Legislative party | seat eligibility |
| Stage 5 | Plurality party | Plurality Power rights |

## Section 3. Compliance Requirements

Emergent parties must comply with:

1. funding disclosure;
2. anti-shell-party rules;
3. internal governance standards;
4. anti-coercion rules;
5. constitutional compliance;
6. common-control review where applicable.

## Section 4. Anti-Gatekeeping

Established parties may not use ballot access, debate access, public funding, or party-status rules to suffocate genuine emergent parties.

---

# V. Party Lists

## Section 1. List Type

The Commonwealth shall use semi-open lists unless changed through election and power rule safeguards.

## Section 2. Party Nomination

Parties may nominate and rank candidates according to internal democratic rules and transparency requirements.

## Section 3. Voter Preference

Voters shall have a meaningful ability to influence the order of candidates through preference votes.

## Section 4. List Override Rule

A candidate receiving a defined preference-vote threshold may move upward on the party list despite party ranking.

## Section 5. Anti-Insider Safeguard

Party list rules shall not allow party leadership to permanently insulate insiders from voter accountability.

---

# VI. Boundary Drawing

## Section 1. Independent Boundary Commission

Districts shall be drawn by an independent boundary commission.

## Section 2. Criteria

Boundary drawing shall consider:

1. population equality;
2. contiguity;
3. community coherence;
4. minority rights;
5. accessibility;
6. geographic reality;
7. regional integrity;
8. anti-gerrymandering metrics;
9. public comment;
10. transparent map publication.

## Section 3. Prohibited Criteria

Districts may not be drawn to entrench a party, incumbent, faction, donor network, or governing coalition.

## Section 4. Public Challenge

Boundary maps shall have a public challenge period and expedited judicial review.

## Section 5. Technology Use

Technology may assist boundary analysis, but final maps must be explainable, auditable, and reviewable.

---

# VII. Proportional Correction

## Section 1. Purpose

Proportional correction ensures final legislative seat totals reflect the party/proportional vote as closely as practicable.

## Section 2. Overhang Seats

Where a party wins more district seats than its proportional vote share would otherwise justify, overhang seats may be recognized according to constitutional statute.

## Section 3. Adjustment Seats

Adjustment seats shall be added where necessary to preserve proportional fairness.

## Section 4. Public Explanation

The Public Constitutional Interface shall explain:

1. district seats won;
2. proportional vote share;
3. proportional entitlement;
4. overhang seats;
5. adjustment seats;
6. final seat totals.

---

# VIII. Coalition Formation

## Section 1. Coalition Deadline

A governing coalition shall be formed within 45 days after final certified results.

## Section 2. Extension

One 15-day extension may be granted through a public procedure defined by constitutional statute.

## Section 3. Public Coalition Agreement

A governing coalition must publish:

1. member parties;
2. governing priorities;
3. cabinet allocation;
4. budget commitments;
5. committee and control arrangements;
6. constitutional red lines;
7. dispute-resolution process;
8. duration or review date;
9. confidence agreement terms.

## Section 4. Coalition Signal Requirement

Before elections, parties shall publish where feasible:

1. preferred coalition partners;
2. parties they refuse to govern with;
3. non-negotiable platform commitments;
4. constitutional commitments;
5. major policy red lines.

Departure from pre-election coalition signals must be publicly explained.

## Section 5. Side Agreement Disclosure

Material coalition side agreements affecting offices, budgets, appointments, public funding, procurement, regional concessions, committee control, or constitutional procedure must be disclosed.

Secret coalition terms affecting public authority are unenforceable and subject to integrity review.

---

# IX. Confidence and No-Confidence

## Section 1. Confidence Vote

A governing coalition shall receive confidence according to constitutional statute before exercising full governing authority.

## Section 2. Constructive No-Confidence

No-confidence shall be constructive.

A no-confidence motion must identify a replacement governing bloc or lawful transition mechanism sufficient to maintain continuity.

## Section 3. Required Elements

Constructive no-confidence rules shall define:

1. filing authority;
2. support threshold;
3. debate period;
4. vote threshold;
5. replacement certification;
6. interim administration rules;
7. limits on repeated bad-faith motions.

## Section 4. Stability Window

A limited stability window after government formation may restrict repeated no-confidence motions except in cases of:

1. grave misconduct;
2. coalition collapse;
3. budget failure;
4. rights violation;
5. emergency overreach;
6. corruption finding;
7. constitutional breach.

---

# X. Party Role Assignment

## Section 1. Public Party Status Ledger

After every election, coalition formation, coalition collapse, confidence vote, material party split, material party merger, or common-control finding, the Democratic System shall publish a Public Party Status Ledger.

## Section 2. Ledger Contents

The Ledger shall show:

1. vote share;
2. district seats;
3. proportional seats;
4. overhang seats;
5. adjustment seats;
6. final seats;
7. governing bloc;
8. principal opposition;
9. plurality blocs;
10. emergent parties;
11. coalition agreement;
12. committee control;
13. public funding;
14. debate access;
15. common-control findings;
16. compliance status;
17. next confidence or review date.

## Section 3. Provisional Status

Provisional party role assignments shall be issued within a defined period after certified election results.

Provisional status remains effective until final assignment or lawful correction.

## Section 4. Appeal

Party role assignments, funding status, debate access, common-control findings, and coalition-status determinations shall have expedited appeal paths.

---

# XI. Regional Parties

## Section 1. Legitimacy

Regional parties are legitimate participants in Commonwealth democracy.

## Section 2. Qualification

Regional parties may qualify for representation through:

1. regional threshold;
2. local district victories;
3. constitutionally authorized regional support criteria.

## Section 3. Regional Leverage Cap

Regional parties may advocate regional interests and join coalitions, but they may not trade support for control over:

1. election administration;
2. courts;
3. emergency rules;
4. fiscal scoring;
5. technology stewardship;
6. integrity systems;
7. constitutional appointments;
8. transfer-of-power rules.

## Section 4. Regional Rights Floor

Regional party influence may not be used to weaken the rights floor or public accountability requirements.

---

# XII. Public Funding

## Section 1. Staged Public Funding

Public funding shall be staged by:

1. demonstrated support;
2. transparency compliance;
3. internal governance compliance;
4. anti-shell-party compliance;
5. prior audit results.

## Section 2. Funding Purpose

Public funding exists to support democratic competition, not fake parties, donor laundering, or shell organizations.

## Section 3. Compliance

Public funding may be suspended, reduced, or recovered for serious noncompliance, subject to due process and appeal.

## Section 4. Emergent Party Support

Verified emergent parties may receive limited public support sufficient to compete, subject to compliance.

---

# XIII. Public Education and Ballot Comprehension

## Section 1. Public Education

The Democratic System shall provide public education for:

1. two-vote ballots;
2. local representation;
3. proportional correction;
4. party thresholds;
5. coalition formation;
6. party role assignment;
7. public party status ledger;
8. rights of appeal.

## Section 2. Ballot Comprehension Standard

Before major electoral changes take effect, public materials and ballot design shall be tested for voter comprehension, accessibility, and usability.

## Section 3. Sample Ballots

Sample ballots and plain-language guides shall be published before each election.

## Section 4. Non-Digital Access

Election education shall be available through digital and non-digital means.

---

# XIV. First Election Stabilization

## Section 1. Purpose

The first election under a new electoral design shall receive additional stabilization safeguards.

## Section 2. Required Safeguards

Before the first election under a new design, the Democratic System shall provide:

1. readiness certification;
2. public voter education;
3. accessible sample ballots;
4. district map challenge period;
5. party compliance audit;
6. election technology audit;
7. observer and audit procedures;
8. emergency election dispute calendar;
9. public certification timeline.

## Section 3. Post-Election Review

After the first election, the Democratic System shall publish a post-election review assessing:

1. voter comprehension;
2. ballot usability;
3. proportionality;
4. coalition formation;
5. party role assignment;
6. dispute resolution;
7. access issues;
8. public trust;
9. recommended parameter adjustments.

---

# XV. Electoral Parameter Review

## Section 1. Timing

After the first two election cycles under a major electoral design, the Democratic System shall conduct a public Electoral Parameter Review.

## Section 2. Required Review Criteria

The review shall assess:

1. proportionality;
2. governability;
3. party competition;
4. emergent-party access;
5. regional representation;
6. voter comprehension;
7. ballot usability;
8. coalition formation;
9. district fairness;
10. public trust;
11. election administration capacity;
12. rights impact.

## Section 3. Recommendations

The review may recommend changes to thresholds, list rules, district/proportional ratio, public funding, debate access, and party role procedures.

## Section 4. Safeguard

Recommended changes do not take effect unless adopted through the election and power rules procedure.

---

# XVI. Anti-Manipulation Rules

## Section 1. Delayed Self-Dealing

Changes to election rules, party rules, districting, office powers, campaign finance, public funding, or coalition rules may not benefit the officials or parties enacting them until after an intervening election.

## Section 2. No Shell Parties

Parties may not create fake independence to manipulate party roles, funding, debate access, coalition leverage, or public status.

## Section 3. No Election Machinery Ownership

Election machinery shall not be owned, controlled, or directed by the Governing Power, party officials, candidates, donor networks, or private vendors without public accountability.

## Section 4. Emergency Election Protection

Emergency conditions may not be used to alter electoral competition except for narrow continuity measures subject to review, public explanation, and delayed self-dealing safeguards.

---

# XVII. Schedule Review

## Section 1. Periodic Review

This Schedule shall be reviewed after the first two election cycles under the Commonwealth electoral system and periodically thereafter.

## Section 2. Review Standard

Review shall assess whether the electoral system remains:

1. fair;
2. proportional;
3. governable;
4. locally accountable;
5. open to emergent parties;
6. resistant to cartelization;
7. resistant to fragmentation;
8. understandable to voters;
9. protective of rights;
10. consistent with democratic self-correction.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Schedule shall follow the election and power rules procedure.
