## Purpose

This schedule defines civilian control, lawful command, security-force nonpartisanship, election-period neutrality, domestic deployment, surveillance limits, classified whistleblower channels, oversight, and transparency.

Core rule:

> The coercive power of the state must never become the possession of a party.

---

# I. Security Neutrality Doctrine

## Section 1. Covered Institutions

This Schedule applies to:

1. military forces;
2. police forces;
3. intelligence services;
4. emergency forces;
5. border or territorial security forces;
6. cyber defense units;
7. domestic deployment forces;
8. public safety forces;
9. contracted security performing public functions;
10. any public body exercising coercive authority.

## Section 2. Civilian Control

Security forces are subordinate to lawful constitutional civilian authority.

## Section 3. Constitutional Loyalty

Security forces owe loyalty to the Constitution, not to any party, faction, individual leader, donor network, private interest, or ideology.

## Section 4. Nonpartisan Role

Security institutions shall not be used to advantage or disadvantage any lawful party, candidate, opposition bloc, civic movement, press organization, or lawful protest.

---

# II. Lawful Command

## Section 1. Lawful Command Standard

Security forces shall obey lawful constitutional civilian commands.

They shall not obey personal, partisan, unlawful, or anti-constitutional orders.

## Section 2. Unlawful Orders

Unlawful orders include orders to:

1. interfere in elections;
2. intimidate voters;
3. suppress lawful opposition;
4. suppress lawful protest;
5. conduct unlawful surveillance;
6. detain without lawful process;
7. conceal corruption;
8. obstruct courts;
9. assist a party or candidate;
10. ignore lawful court orders;
11. enforce expired emergency authority;
12. violate the rights floor.

## Section 3. Refusal Protection

Security personnel who refuse a clearly unlawful order in good faith shall be protected from retaliation.

## Section 4. Escalation

Security personnel receiving an apparently unlawful order shall have access to protected legal, inspector, judicial, or classified reporting channels.

---

# III. Nonpartisanship Rules

## Section 1. Prohibited Conduct

Security forces may not:

1. serve a party;
2. interfere in elections;
3. intimidate opposition;
4. suppress lawful protest;
5. conduct unlawful surveillance;
6. enforce emergency orders beyond legal scope;
7. campaign while acting in official capacity;
8. use public security resources for partisan purposes;
9. selectively enforce law for political advantage;
10. conceal public authority abuse.

## Section 2. Official Capacity

Security personnel acting in official capacity shall not display partisan loyalty, campaign materials, factional symbols, or political messaging inconsistent with neutrality duties.

## Section 3. Private Rights

Security personnel retain private civil rights, subject to neutral limits necessary to preserve public trust, chain of command, classified obligations, and nonpartisan enforcement.

## Section 4. Enforcement

Violations of security neutrality may trigger disciplinary process, integrity review, court review, removal, or criminal referral where lawful.

---

# IV. Election-Period Security Neutrality

## Section 1. Heightened Neutrality

During election periods, security forces operate under heightened neutrality protocols.

## Section 2. Election Period Defined

Election period shall be defined by constitutional statute and shall include, at minimum, periods covering:

1. candidate registration;
2. active campaigning;
3. early voting;
4. election day;
5. counting;
6. certification;
7. recounts or election disputes;
8. transfer of power.

## Section 3. Required Protections

Election-period security protocols shall include:

1. prohibition on partisan deployment;
2. prohibition on voter intimidation;
3. public reporting of extraordinary deployments where lawful;
4. election stewardship notification;
5. judicial review access;
6. protection of lawful campaign activity;
7. protection of opposition activity;
8. strict limits on surveillance of political actors;
9. restrictions on visible militarization near polling places unless necessary for safety;
10. incident logging.

## Section 4. Election Stewardship Notification

Extraordinary security deployments affecting election administration, voting locations, ballot custody, counting, certification, campaigns, or opposition activity shall be reported to Election Stewardship where lawful and practicable.

## Section 5. Political Surveillance

Surveillance of candidates, campaigns, parties, election workers, journalists, protest groups, or political organizations during election periods requires heightened legal review.

## Section 6. Emergency Exception

Emergency conditions may justify election-period security action only where necessary, proportionate, reviewable, and not designed to advantage a party or suppress lawful participation.

---

# V. Domestic Deployment

## Section 1. Covered Deployment

Domestic deployment includes use of military, intelligence, emergency, or extraordinary security forces within the Commonwealth for public order, emergency response, election support, infrastructure protection, or public safety.

## Section 2. Required Terms

Domestic deployment requires:

1. legal authority;
2. defined scope;
3. responsible command;
4. duration limit;
5. rights safeguards;
6. public reporting where lawful;
7. oversight;
8. review;
9. withdrawal criteria;
10. complaint path.

## Section 3. Prohibited Uses

Domestic deployment may not be used to suppress:

1. lawful opposition;
2. lawful protest;
3. voting;
4. journalism;
5. political organization;
6. civic association;
7. constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 4. Duration

Domestic deployments shall be time-limited and reviewed periodically.

## Section 5. Public Reporting

Public reporting shall identify the legal basis, responsible command, purpose, duration, geographic scope, and rights safeguards, subject to lawful security limits.

---

# VI. Surveillance

## Section 1. Legal Authority

Surveillance requires legal authority.

## Section 2. Heightened Review

Surveillance materially affecting rights, political activity, protected association, journalism, protest, legal status, or public benefits requires heightened review.

## Section 3. Required Safeguards

Surveillance shall include, where applicable:

1. necessity;
2. proportionality;
3. minimization;
4. court supervision where required;
5. access controls;
6. audit logs;
7. retention limits;
8. use limits;
9. remedy for abuse;
10. independent review.

## Section 4. Prohibited Surveillance

Surveillance may not be used for:

1. partisan advantage;
2. voter intimidation;
3. unlawful protest suppression;
4. retaliation against dissent;
5. discrimination;
6. harassment;
7. public-benefit coercion;
8. suppressing lawful journalism;
9. circumventing due process.

## Section 5. Political Surveillance Review

Surveillance of political actors, parties, candidates, campaigns, journalists, civic organizations, or protest groups requires independent review and heightened justification.

---

# VII. Intelligence Oversight

## Section 1. Oversight Requirement

Intelligence institutions shall be subject to legislative, judicial, inspector, and lawful classified oversight.

## Section 2. Oversight Access

Oversight bodies shall have lawful access to information necessary to evaluate legality, rights compliance, fiscal use, surveillance, emergency activity, and abuse allegations.

## Section 3. Classified Limits

Classification may protect legitimate security information.

Classification may not be used to conceal illegality, corruption, rights violations, partisan abuse, or emergency overreach.

## Section 4. Opposition Participation

Classified legislative oversight shall include opposition participation where compatible with lawful security requirements.

## Section 5. Inspector Function

Security inspectors shall have authority to investigate misconduct, unlawful orders, surveillance abuse, procurement abuse, retaliation, and rights violations.

---

# VIII. Classified Whistleblower Process

## Section 1. Protected Channels

Security personnel shall have protected channels for reporting:

1. unlawful orders;
2. illegal surveillance;
3. election interference;
4. corruption;
5. procurement abuse;
6. emergency overreach;
7. rights violations;
8. classified program abuse;
9. retaliation;
10. misuse of security forces.

## Section 2. Channel Requirements

Classified whistleblower channels shall provide:

1. protected recipients;
2. secure submission;
3. anti-retaliation protection;
4. independent review;
5. lawful handling of classified information;
6. escalation where internal channels fail;
7. protection against punishment for good-faith use of lawful channels.

## Section 3. Good-Faith Protection

Good-faith whistleblowers shall not lose protection solely because a claim is not ultimately proven.

## Section 4. Retaliation

Retaliation may include:

1. termination;
2. demotion;
3. reassignment;
4. clearance retaliation;
5. harassment;
6. prosecution threat for protected reporting;
7. blacklisting;
8. benefit denial;
9. reputation attack using classified asymmetry.

## Section 5. False or Bad-Faith Claims

Knowingly false or bad-faith reports may be sanctioned after due process.

---

# IX. Use of Force and Public Order

## Section 1. Legal Basis

Use of force by public security must have legal basis.

## Section 2. Principles

Use of force shall be:

1. necessary;
2. proportionate;
3. accountable;
4. documented;
5. reviewable;
6. non-discriminatory;
7. subject to remedy where unlawful.

## Section 3. Protest Context

Lawful protest shall not be treated as a security threat merely because it is disruptive, unpopular, critical of government, or politically inconvenient.

## Section 4. Public Order

Public order measures shall not be used to suppress lawful opposition, journalism, civic participation, or constitutional reform advocacy.

---

# X. Emergency Security Powers

## Section 1. Cross-Reference

Emergency security powers are governed by this Schedule and Schedule J.

## Section 2. Emergency Limits

Emergency security powers must remain:

1. legally authorized;
2. scoped;
3. time-limited;
4. rights-reviewed;
5. publicly accountable where lawful;
6. subject to court access;
7. subject to normalcy restoration.

## Section 3. No Permanent Security Emergency

Security emergency powers may not become permanent governing authority.

## Section 4. Post-Emergency Review

Security actions taken during emergency shall receive post-emergency review.

---

# XI. Security Procurement

## Section 1. Covered Procurement

Security procurement includes contracts for:

1. weapons;
2. surveillance systems;
3. intelligence tools;
4. cyber tools;
5. biometric systems;
6. data systems;
7. emergency response systems;
8. private security services;
9. detention infrastructure;
10. public safety technology.

## Section 2. Required Review

High-risk security procurement requires:

1. legal basis;
2. rights impact review;
3. procurement integrity review;
4. technology review where applicable;
5. beneficial ownership disclosure;
6. vendor conflict review;
7. audit rights;
8. use restrictions;
9. public accountability record.

## Section 3. Secret Procurement

Classified procurement shall still be subject to lawful classified audit, inspector review, and anti-corruption controls.

## Section 4. Vendor Capture

Security procurement shall not create vendor control over public authority, surveillance policy, enforcement priorities, or public data.

---

# XII. Data and Records

## Section 1. Security Records

Security institutions shall maintain records sufficient for audit, legal review, rights review, and public accountability.

## Section 2. Required Records

Records shall include, where applicable:

1. legal basis;
2. responsible command;
3. deployment orders;
4. surveillance authorization;
5. use-of-force records;
6. procurement records;
7. emergency activation records;
8. complaint records;
9. whistleblower records;
10. audit records.

## Section 3. Retention and Minimization

Security data shall be retained only as legally necessary and minimized where rights are implicated.

## Section 4. Public Access

Public access shall be provided where lawful, subject to narrow privacy and security limits.

---

# XIII. Security Transparency

## Section 1. Transparency Reports

The Commonwealth shall publish periodic security transparency reports.

## Section 2. Report Contents

Reports may include:

1. domestic deployments;
2. emergency security powers;
3. surveillance authorizations;
4. public-order operations;
5. election-period deployments;
6. procurement summaries;
7. complaints;
8. substantiated abuse findings;
9. whistleblower statistics where lawful;
10. court compliance;
11. audit findings;
12. rights-impact assessments.

## Section 3. Security Limits

Reports may withhold details only under lawful, narrow, reviewable security limits.

## Section 4. Surrogate Reporting

Where details cannot be public, surrogate reporting shall provide meaningful accountability without exposing protected information.

---

# XIV. Training and Constitutional Culture

## Section 1. Training Requirement

Security personnel shall receive training in:

1. constitutional authority;
2. lawful command;
3. rights floor;
4. election neutrality;
5. protest rights;
6. surveillance limits;
7. emergency limits;
8. anti-corruption;
9. whistleblower channels;
10. nonpartisanship.

## Section 2. Leadership Duty

Security leadership shall maintain institutional culture of constitutional neutrality.

## Section 3. Certification

High-risk security roles may require periodic constitutional and ethics certification.

---

# XV. Security Abuse Remedies

## Section 1. Remedies

Security abuse may trigger:

1. complaint review;
2. inspector investigation;
3. judicial review;
4. suppression of unlawfully obtained evidence where applicable;
5. civil remedy;
6. disciplinary action;
7. removal;
8. integrity referral;
9. criminal referral where lawful;
10. public reporting.

## Section 2. Pattern or Practice

Repeated or systemic security abuse shall trigger structural review and corrective plan.

## Section 3. Victim Access

Affected persons shall have accessible complaint, appeal, and remedy pathways.
