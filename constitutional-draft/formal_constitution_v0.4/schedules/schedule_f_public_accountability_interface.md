# Schedule F — Public Accountability and Interface Standards
## Formal Constitution v0.4

## Status

## Purpose

This schedule defines the public accountability records, interface standards, cure rules, audit requirements, and public access obligations required by the Constitution.

Core rule:

> Public power must be visible, understandable, challengeable, audited, and usable.

---

# I. Public Accountability Standard

## Section 1. Required Accountability Fields

Every exercise of material public authority shall identify:

1. accountable owner;
2. legal basis;
3. decision date;
4. public decision or action taken;
5. affected public function;
6. appeal or challenge path;
7. audit or review path;
8. review, renewal, expiration, or sunset date where applicable;
9. technology involvement where material;
10. conflict disclosure where material;
11. emergency authority used where applicable;
12. fiscal authority or funding source where material;
13. public record location;
14. responsible contact or access channel.

## Section 2. Material Public Authority

For purposes of this Schedule, material public authority includes public action that materially affects:

1. rights;
2. benefits;
3. legal status;
4. enforcement;
5. surveillance;
6. voting access;
7. public services;
8. public funding;
9. procurement;
10. emergency powers;
11. public technology;
12. public records;
13. constitutional roles;
14. access to remedy.

## Section 3. Accountability by Default

The default rule is disclosure.

Nondisclosure, redaction, delay, classification, or restricted access must be legally authorized, narrowly tailored, reviewable, and accompanied by surrogate public explanation where possible.

---

# II. Accountable Owner Requirements

## Section 1. Named Accountable Office

Public accountability records shall identify a named accountable office with enough specificity for the public to determine who owns the decision.

Generic agency labels are insufficient where a specific office, program, official role, contractor, vendor, automated system, or stewardship body materially owns or controls the decision.

## Section 2. Human Accountability Owner

Where automated systems, vendors, contractors, public-private partnerships, or technical workflows materially shape public authority, a public human accountability owner must be identified.

## Section 3. Contractor and Vendor Responsibility

Where a contractor or vendor performs a material public function, the public record shall identify:

1. public agency owner;
2. contractor or vendor;
3. contract or delegation authority;
4. relevant audit rights;
5. appeal path;
6. public records obligations;
7. technology disclosure where relevant.

## Section 4. Shared Responsibility

Where more than one office is involved, the record shall identify:

1. lead accountable office;
2. supporting offices;
3. decision owner;
4. implementation owner;
5. audit owner;
6. appeal forum.

Shared responsibility shall not become shared evasion.

---

# III. Legal Basis Format

## Section 1. Required Legal Basis

Each public accountability record shall identify the legal basis for public authority.

The legal basis may include:

1. constitutional provision;
2. statute;
3. constitutional schedule;
4. regulation;
5. court order;
6. emergency order;
7. appropriation;
8. contract or delegation authority;
9. administrative rule;
10. public program authorization.

## Section 2. Plain-Language Explanation

The legal basis shall include a plain-language explanation of what authority the law gives and what limits apply.

## Section 3. Missing or Defective Legal Basis

Where a legal basis is missing, vague, incorrect, or not publicly available, the action is subject to Public Accountability Cure under this Schedule.

Rights-impacting actions with no identifiable legal basis may be paused, reviewed, corrected, or invalidated according to law.

---

# IV. Appeal and Challenge Path

## Section 1. Required Appeal Path

Every material public decision shall identify how an affected person may challenge it.

The appeal path shall include:

1. first review forum;
2. filing method;
3. filing deadline;
4. required documents or information;
5. available remedies;
6. escalation or appeal route;
7. assistance options;
8. non-digital access option;
9. emergency or fast-review option where applicable.

## Section 2. No Hidden Appeal

Appeal rights must be visible before or at the time the affected person receives notice of the decision, unless urgent circumstances make later notice lawful and necessary.

## Section 3. Wrong-Door Protection

A person who attempts in good faith to appeal through the wrong office or forum shall be routed to the proper forum.

A good-faith wrong-door filing shall preserve timeliness where the claim is plausible and timely.

## Section 4. Appeal Usability

Appeal processes must be usable by ordinary persons and shall not require unnecessary technical, legal, digital, or procedural knowledge.

---

# V. Audit and Review Path

## Section 1. Required Audit Path

Each material public authority record shall identify the office or body responsible for audit, review, or oversight.

## Section 2. Audit Types

Audit or review may include:

1. legal review;
2. rights review;
3. fiscal audit;
4. technology audit;
5. integrity review;
6. procurement review;
7. public-service performance review;
8. emergency review;
9. algorithmic review;
10. accessibility review;
11. security review.

## Section 3. Audit Visibility

Audit outcomes shall be publicly available unless legally restricted.

Where audit information is withheld, the responsible office shall provide a surrogate public explanation where possible.

## Section 4. Repeated Failure

Repeated audit failures by the same office, vendor, program, or system shall trigger heightened review.

---

# VI. Review, Renewal, Expiration, and Sunset

## Section 1. Required Review Date

Public authority that is temporary, extraordinary, emergency-based, pilot-based, algorithmic, delegated, or high-impact shall identify a review, renewal, expiration, or sunset date.

## Section 2. Expiration Visibility

The Public Constitutional Interface shall display active expiration dates for:

1. emergency powers;
2. temporary delegations;
3. pilot programs;
4. high-risk public technology deployments;
5. public algorithm authorizations;
6. temporary procurement exceptions;
7. temporary funding authorities;
8. interim stewardship appointments;
9. transitional authorities.

## Section 3. Renewal Requirements

Renewal shall require public explanation, current legal basis, review of performance, and disclosure of relevant risks.

## Section 4. Expired Authority

Expired authority shall not continue by administrative inertia.

Continuation of expired authority is presumptively invalid unless lawfully renewed or temporarily preserved by continuity rule.

---

# VII. Technology Disclosure

## Section 1. Required Disclosure

Where technology materially shapes public authority, the record shall identify:

1. system name;
2. public owner;
3. vendor or contractor where applicable;
4. legal authority;
5. purpose;
6. risk tier where applicable;
7. type of system;
8. decision role;
9. human review path;
10. audit status;
11. appeal path;
12. system card location where required;
13. deployment date;
14. review or expiration date.

## Section 2. Algorithmic Material Influence

A system must be disclosed where it materially affects eligibility, ranking, priority, risk score, enforcement likelihood, benefit approval, surveillance exposure, legal status, public service timing, or human reviewer behavior.

## Section 3. No Black-Box Accountability

A public authority may not avoid technology disclosure by citing vendor secrecy, technical complexity, internal workflow status, or advisory label where the system materially shapes a public decision.

## Section 4. Emergency Technology

Emergency technology deployments shall be marked as provisional, time-limited, and subject to post-deployment audit.

---

# VIII. Conflict Disclosure

## Section 1. Required Conflict Disclosure

Where material, public records shall disclose relevant conflicts involving:

1. decision-makers;
2. agencies;
3. contractors;
4. vendors;
5. lobbyists;
6. donors;
7. affiliated entities;
8. beneficial owners;
9. family or business interests;
10. prior employment;
11. campaign or party ties;
12. procurement relationships.

## Section 2. Conflict Categories

Conflict disclosures may identify:

1. financial conflict;
2. party conflict;
3. vendor conflict;
4. family conflict;
5. employment conflict;
6. donor conflict;
7. lobbying conflict;
8. regulated-entity conflict;
9. revolving-door conflict;
10. foreign-linked conflict.

## Section 3. Privacy Limits

Conflict disclosure shall reveal material public-interest conflicts without exposing irrelevant personal details.

## Section 4. Conflict Mitigation

Where a conflict exists, the record shall identify any mitigation, including:

1. recusal;
2. divestment;
3. blind trust;
4. firewall;
5. independent review;
6. reassignment;
7. audit;
8. public explanation.

---

# IX. Public Accountability Defect Categories

## Section 1. Defect Categories

Public accountability defects shall be classified as:

1. clerical defect;
2. material omission;
3. rights-impacting defect;
4. bad-faith concealment.

## Section 2. Clerical Defect

A clerical defect is a minor error that does not impair public understanding, appeal, audit, rights protection, or democratic accountability.

## Section 3. Material Omission

A material omission is a missing or incomplete record that impairs public understanding, appeal, audit, conflict review, public oversight, or remedy.

## Section 4. Rights-Impacting Defect

A rights-impacting defect affects rights, benefits, enforcement, legal status, surveillance, emergency power, voting access, public services, or other material interests.

## Section 5. Bad-Faith Concealment

Bad-faith concealment is intentional or reckless failure to disclose required public accountability information.

## Section 6. Repeated Defect

Repeated defects by the same office, program, vendor, contractor, or system shall trigger heightened review even if individual defects are minor.

---

# X. Cure Rules

## Section 1. Cure Duty

The responsible public authority shall cure defective accountability records within timelines established by constitutional statute.

## Section 2. Cure Standards

A cure must correct the public accountability defect in substance, not merely in form.

A cure is insufficient if the public record remains unusable, misleading, inaccessible, incomplete, or unclear.

## Section 3. Indicative Cure Categories

Constitutional statute shall define cure periods for:

| Defect type | Presumptive response |
|---|---|
| Clerical defect | prompt correction |
| Material omission | correction and notice |
| Rights-impacting defect | expedited correction and review |
| Bad-faith concealment | investigation, sanction, and possible invalidation |

## Section 4. Rights-Impacting Consequence

For rights-impacting defects, failure to cure may result in:

1. pause;
2. review;
3. reconsideration;
4. reinstatement;
5. temporary relief;
6. invalidation;
7. corrective order;
8. sanction;
9. referral to Integrity System.

## Section 5. Bad-Faith Consequence

Bad-faith concealment may trigger integrity review, legal sanction, public finding, or removal process where appropriate.

---

# XI. Public Constitutional Interface

## Section 1. Interface Purpose

The Public Constitutional Interface exists to make public power understandable and challengeable.

## Section 2. Required Interface Functions

The Interface shall allow the public to:

1. search public decisions;
2. identify accountable owners;
3. find legal authority;
4. file or locate appeals;
5. identify audit paths;
6. view active emergency powers;
7. view public algorithms;
8. view conflicts where disclosed;
9. view party status;
10. view coalition agreements;
11. view public funding;
12. view procurement records;
13. view fiscal dashboards;
14. view court compliance;
15. view transition milestones.

## Section 3. Public Routing Assistant

The Interface shall provide routing assistance for claims, appeals, complaints, records requests, and rights petitions.

The routing assistant shall not be the sole access method.

## Section 4. Non-Digital Access

The Commonwealth shall maintain non-digital and assisted access channels for essential accountability and rights functions.

---

# XII. Interface Design Standards

## Section 1. Plain Language

Major public records shall include plain-language explanations.

## Section 2. Accessibility

The Interface shall be accessible to persons with disabilities and shall comply with accessibility standards established by constitutional statute.

## Section 3. Searchability

Records shall be searchable by:

1. decision;
2. office;
3. legal authority;
4. date;
5. location;
6. program;
7. vendor;
8. algorithm;
9. emergency authority;
10. appeal path;
11. public funding;
12. party status where relevant.

## Section 4. Timeliness

Records must be published quickly enough to permit meaningful public understanding, appeal, audit, or review.

## Section 5. Multilingual and Assisted Access

Where needed, public access shall include multilingual materials, assistance channels, and local access support.

## Section 6. Machine Readability

Public records shall be machine-readable where appropriate and consistent with privacy, security, and accessibility.

---

# XIII. Public Interface Audit

## Section 1. Required Audit

The Public Constitutional Interface shall be independently audited.

## Section 2. Audit Dimensions

The audit shall assess:

1. completeness;
2. timeliness;
3. accuracy;
4. accessibility;
5. searchability;
6. plain-language quality;
7. appeal usability;
8. algorithm disclosure;
9. conflict disclosure;
10. party status clarity;
11. emergency visibility;
12. non-digital access;
13. machine readability;
14. public comprehension.

## Section 3. Public Score

The Interface may receive a public usability and completeness score.

## Section 4. Correction Plan

Persistent failure requires a public correction plan, timeline, and review.

## Section 5. Independent Review

The audit shall be performed by an independent body not under unilateral control of the office being audited.

---

# XIV. Public Dashboards

## Section 1. Required Dashboards

The Interface shall include dashboards for:

1. public decisions;
2. appeals;
3. audit findings;
4. emergency powers;
5. public algorithms;
6. procurement;
7. fiscal status;
8. tax expenditures;
9. public party status;
10. coalition agreements;
11. court compliance;
12. transition milestones;
13. local fiscal distress;
14. stewardship funding.

## Section 2. Dashboard Warnings

Dashboards shall not substitute for legal records.

They are public navigation tools.

## Section 3. Update Duty

Dashboards shall identify the date of last update and responsible office.

---

# XV. Public Records and Privacy

## Section 1. Privacy Protection

Public accountability shall not require disclosure of irrelevant personal details.

## Section 2. Sensitive Information

Sensitive information may be redacted or withheld where lawful and necessary.

Sensitive information may include:

1. private medical information;
2. personal financial details;
3. protected addresses;
4. security-sensitive information;
5. confidential whistleblower identity;
6. protected personal data;
7. sealed court information;
8. classified information.

## Section 3. Surrogate Explanation

Where details are lawfully withheld, the public record shall provide surrogate explanation where possible.

## Section 4. Review of Withholding

Withholding, redaction, or classification shall be reviewable.

---

# XVI. Security, Classification, and Emergency Exceptions

## Section 1. Narrow Exception

Security, classification, and emergency exceptions shall be narrow, legally authorized, reviewable, and time-bounded where appropriate.

## Section 2. Emergency Records

Emergency actions shall be recorded even where immediate publication is limited.

Publication shall occur as soon as lawful and safe.

## Section 3. Classified Accountability

Classified accountability records shall be available to authorized oversight bodies, courts, or inspectors according to law.

## Section 4. Abuse Prevention

Security or emergency exceptions may not be used to conceal corruption, unlawful surveillance, political abuse, rights violations, or public authority evasion.

---

# XVII. Public Record Integrity

## Section 1. Version History

Material public records shall preserve version history.

## Section 2. Corrections

Corrections shall show:

1. original record where lawful;
2. correction date;
3. correcting authority;
4. reason for correction;
5. appeal path where relevant.

## Section 3. Tamper Evidence

Where appropriate, public records may use tamper-evident systems or public ledgers.

## Section 4. Legal Truth

A public record proves record history. It does not by itself determine final legal truth where a court or lawful authority corrects the record.

---

# XVIII. Metrics and Reporting

## Section 1. Public Accountability Metrics

The Commonwealth shall track:

1. percentage of material decisions with complete accountability records;
2. appeal path visibility;
3. audit path visibility;
4. algorithm disclosure compliance;
5. conflict disclosure compliance;
6. cure timeliness;
7. repeated defect rates;
8. public interface search success;
9. non-digital access usage;
10. accessibility compliance;
11. public comprehension indicators.

## Section 2. Annual Report

The Public Constitutional Interface Authority shall publish an annual public accountability report.

## Section 3. Independent Evaluation

Periodic independent evaluation shall assess whether public accountability systems are usable by ordinary persons.
