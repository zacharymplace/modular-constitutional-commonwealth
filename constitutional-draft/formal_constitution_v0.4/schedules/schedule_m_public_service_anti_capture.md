## Purpose

This schedule defines public service duties, anti-capture rules, Public Trust License standards, disclosure requirements, revolving-door limits, beneficial ownership rules, candidate compliance assistance, ethics obligations, and enforcement safeguards.

Core rule:

> Public office is a fiduciary trust, not private property.

---

# I. Public Service Doctrine

## Section 1. Fiduciary Duty

Public office is a fiduciary trust.

Public authority shall be exercised for the public good, under law, and subject to accountability.

## Section 2. No Private Ownership of Office

No public office may be treated as private property, family property, party property, donor property, vendor property, or factional property.

## Section 3. Public Service Standard

Officials shall act with:

1. legality;
2. loyalty to constitutional duty;
3. honesty;
4. care;
5. impartial administration;
6. conflict disclosure;
7. public accountability;
8. respect for rights;
9. stewardship of public resources;
10. resistance to capture.

## Section 4. Public Trust

The public has a constitutional interest in knowing whether public officials are acting under hidden conflicts, improper influence, or private incentives.

---

# II. Covered Officials and Offices

## Section 1. Covered Persons

This Schedule may apply to:

1. elected officials;
2. appointed officials;
3. executive officers;
4. legislative officers;
5. judges where consistent with judicial independence;
6. stewardship officials;
7. senior civil servants;
8. procurement officials;
9. public finance officials;
10. public technology officials;
11. emergency officials;
12. security officials;
13. public contractors performing material public functions;
14. party officers receiving public privileges;
15. candidates for covered offices.

## Section 2. High-Risk Offices

High-risk offices include offices involving:

1. public finance;
2. procurement;
3. emergency powers;
4. election administration;
5. public technology;
6. public algorithms;
7. security or intelligence;
8. adjudication;
9. licensing or regulatory enforcement;
10. public land or public assets;
11. integrity enforcement;
12. fiscal scoring;
13. stewardship appointment power.

## Section 3. Risk-Tiered Duties

Disclosure, licensing, recusal, cooling-off, and post-service duties shall scale with office risk level.

---

# III. Public Service Standards

## Section 1. Required Standards

Officials may be required to comply with:

1. conflict disclosures;
2. asset disclosures;
3. beneficial ownership disclosures;
4. gift restrictions;
5. ethics training;
6. constitutional training;
7. stock-trading restrictions;
8. blind trusts or index-only holding rules;
9. recusal duties;
10. public integrity oaths;
11. revolving-door restrictions;
12. audit cooperation.

## Section 2. Constitutional Training

Covered officials shall receive training in:

1. Democratic Kernel;
2. rights floor;
3. public accountability;
4. conflict rules;
5. stewardship limits;
6. emergency limits;
7. technology governance where relevant;
8. fiscal honesty where relevant;
9. security neutrality where relevant;
10. anti-corruption duties.

## Section 3. Ethics Certification

Covered officials may be required to certify understanding of ethics, disclosure, conflict, and public accountability obligations.

## Section 4. Public Integrity Oath

Covered officials may be required to swear or affirm that public authority shall be used for lawful public purposes and not for personal, party, donor, vendor, or factional capture.

---

# IV. Conflict Disclosure

## Section 1. Material Conflicts

Covered officials shall disclose material conflicts where relevant to their office.

Material conflicts may include:

1. financial interests;
2. employment interests;
3. family interests;
4. beneficial ownership;
5. business relationships;
6. donor relationships;
7. vendor relationships;
8. lobbying relationships;
9. party relationships;
10. foreign-linked interests;
11. regulated-entity relationships;
12. litigation interests;
13. trust or nonprofit interests;
14. consulting relationships.

## Section 2. Disclosure Standard

Disclosure shall reveal material public-interest conflicts without exposing irrelevant private details.

## Section 3. Timing

Disclosure may be required:

1. before assuming office;
2. periodically during service;
3. before major decisions where specific conflict exists;
4. after material change;
5. before post-service employment where required.

## Section 4. Public and Confidential Disclosure

Some disclosures may be public.

Sensitive details may be submitted confidentially to an authorized ethics or integrity body where public disclosure would create unnecessary privacy, safety, or security risk.

## Section 5. Failure to Disclose

Failure to disclose material conflicts may trigger cure, recusal, ethics review, integrity referral, sanction, or removal process where appropriate.

---

# V. Beneficial Ownership and Affiliated Entities

## Section 1. Beneficial Ownership

Disclosure rules may require officials to disclose beneficial ownership in entities materially affected by their public authority.

## Section 2. Affiliated Entities

Disclosure may include material interests held through:

1. corporations;
2. partnerships;
3. trusts;
4. nonprofits;
5. foundations;
6. shell entities;
7. consulting vehicles;
8. family-controlled entities;
9. investment vehicles;
10. foreign-linked entities.

## Section 3. Anti-Evasion

Public service integrity rules apply to indirect arrangements used to evade conflict, lobbying, disclosure, or post-service restrictions.

## Section 4. Family and Household Interests

Family or household interests may be disclosed where material to public authority, subject to privacy safeguards and proportionality.

## Section 5. Blind Trusts and Index Holdings

High-risk officials may be required to use blind trusts, diversified index holdings, divestment, recusal, or other conflict-mitigation tools.

---

# VI. Gifts, Benefits, and Private Advantage

## Section 1. Gift Restrictions

Covered officials may be restricted from accepting gifts, benefits, travel, hospitality, compensation, or favors from persons or entities seeking public action.

## Section 2. Prohibited Benefits

Prohibited benefits may include:

1. cash or cash equivalents;
2. luxury gifts;
3. sponsored travel without public purpose;
4. below-market transactions;
5. preferential loans;
6. hidden compensation;
7. family benefits tied to public office;
8. future employment promises;
9. donor-funded personal benefits;
10. vendor-funded personal benefits.

## Section 3. Exceptions

Narrow exceptions may exist for ordinary hospitality, ceremonial gifts, de minimis items, public events, or official travel, subject to disclosure and limits.

## Section 4. Public Record

Material gifts or benefits accepted under lawful exceptions shall be publicly recorded.

---

# VII. Stock Trading and Financial Restrictions

## Section 1. Trading Restrictions

Covered officials may be subject to restrictions on trading individual securities, derivatives, commodities, crypto assets, or other financial instruments where public authority creates conflict risk.

## Section 2. High-Risk Officials

Stricter rules may apply to officials with authority over:

1. regulation;
2. procurement;
3. public finance;
4. emergency response;
5. defense or security;
6. public technology;
7. infrastructure;
8. tax policy;
9. monetary or fiscal risk;
10. market-sensitive information.

## Section 3. Permitted Structures

Permitted structures may include:

1. diversified funds;
2. index funds;
3. blind trusts;
4. divestment;
5. managed ethics-approved arrangements.

## Section 4. Use of Nonpublic Information

Officials may not use nonpublic public information for private financial gain.

---

# VIII. Recusal and Firewall Duties

## Section 1. Recusal

Officials shall recuse from matters where material conflict prevents impartial or lawful service.

## Section 2. Firewall

Where recusal alone is insufficient, a firewall may be required.

## Section 3. Public Explanation

Material recusals and firewalls shall be publicly disclosed where lawful.

## Section 4. Failure to Recuse

Failure to recuse from a material conflict may trigger ethics review, integrity referral, sanction, or invalidation of affected action where appropriate.

---

# IX. Public Trust License

## Section 1. Purpose

A Public Trust License may be required for certain high offices to confirm objective competence, disclosure, ethics, and constitutional-process readiness.

## Section 2. Covered Offices

Covered offices may include:

1. high fiduciary offices;
2. election offices;
3. emergency offices;
4. public finance offices;
5. public technology offices;
6. security offices;
7. procurement offices;
8. integrity offices;
9. stewardship offices;
10. judicial or quasi-judicial offices where consistent with judicial independence.

## Section 3. Objective Criteria

Public Trust License criteria shall be objective, public, non-ideological, and limited to:

1. constitutional process knowledge;
2. ethics and conflicts compliance;
3. required disclosures;
4. fiduciary-duty certification;
5. anti-corruption oath;
6. audit agreement;
7. post-service restriction acknowledgment;
8. minimum competence for high-risk office duties.

## Section 4. Ideological Neutrality

The license may not exclude candidates or officials based on ideology, party, viewpoint, lawful dissent, criticism of government, or peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 5. Due Process

License denial, suspension, or revocation shall include:

1. notice;
2. reasoned explanation;
3. cure opportunity where appropriate;
4. appeal;
5. proportional remedy;
6. ideological neutrality.

## Section 6. Public Status

License status may be public where relevant to public office qualification.

Irrelevant private details shall not be public.

---

# X. Candidate and Official Compliance Assistance

## Section 1. Assistance Duty

The Commonwealth shall provide reasonable compliance assistance for candidates and officials subject to disclosure, ethics, or Public Trust License requirements.

## Section 2. Purpose

Compliance systems shall not be designed so that only wealthy, incumbent, or professionally connected candidates can realistically comply.

## Section 3. Assistance May Include

Assistance may include:

1. plain-language forms;
2. public templates;
3. advisory opinions;
4. training;
5. help desks;
6. filing support;
7. cure guidance;
8. small-candidate compliance support;
9. multilingual access where needed;
10. disability-accessible tools.

## Section 4. Good-Faith Safe Harbor

Good-faith complete filings may receive safe harbor against severe sanction for minor, curable, or non-bad-faith errors.

## Section 5. Cure

Non-bad-faith defects shall receive cure opportunity where consistent with public integrity and election timing.

---

# XI. Revolving Door and Post-Service Restrictions

## Section 1. Covered Activities

Post-service restrictions may apply to:

1. lobbying;
2. access brokerage;
3. paid strategic advising;
4. vendor employment;
5. regulated-sector employment;
6. shadow lobbying;
7. procurement consulting;
8. representation before former agency;
9. paid use of confidential public knowledge;
10. employment related to matters personally and substantially handled.

## Section 2. Risk-Tiered Restrictions

Post-service limits shall scale with risk of office held.

Higher restrictions may apply to offices involving:

1. procurement;
2. regulation;
3. public technology;
4. emergency powers;
5. national security;
6. public finance;
7. election administration;
8. integrity enforcement;
9. adjudication.

## Section 3. Proportionality

Restrictions shall remain proportionate and shall not operate as unreasonable exclusion from ordinary livelihood.

## Section 4. Cooling-Off Period

Cooling-off periods shall be defined by constitutional statute according to office risk level.

## Section 5. Shadow Lobbying

Shadow lobbying includes paid behind-the-scenes influence, access brokerage, strategic advising, or intermediary activity designed to evade lobbying restrictions.

---

# XII. Lobbying and Access Brokerage

## Section 1. Lobbying Disclosure

Lobbying rules may require disclosure of:

1. client;
2. subject matter;
3. public offices contacted;
4. compensation source where material;
5. draft language submitted;
6. affiliated entities;
7. former officials involved;
8. procurement interest;
9. campaign or party finance links.

## Section 2. Access Brokerage

Access brokerage is the sale or monetization of personal access to public officials, stewardship bodies, procurement officials, regulators, or public decision-makers.

## Section 3. Restrictions

Access brokerage may be restricted where it exploits former public office, confidential knowledge, procurement relationships, regulatory authority, or emergency authority.

## Section 4. Public Record

Material lobbying and access brokerage shall be recorded where required by law.

---

# XIII. Public Contractors and Vendors

## Section 1. Covered Contractors

Contractors and vendors performing material public functions may be subject to public service integrity duties.

## Section 2. Required Duties

Covered contractors may be required to comply with:

1. conflict disclosure;
2. beneficial ownership disclosure;
3. gift restrictions;
4. revolving-door restrictions;
5. audit access;
6. public records duties;
7. anti-corruption rules;
8. procurement integrity rules;
9. technology audit rights;
10. post-contract restrictions where appropriate.

## Section 3. Vendor Capture

Public contractors and vendors may not use technical control, data access, proprietary systems, procurement dependence, or post-service employment to capture public authority.

## Section 4. Contract Clauses

Contracts for material public functions shall include public accountability, audit, conflict, and exit clauses.

---

# XIV. Party Officers Receiving Public Privileges

## Section 1. Covered Party Officers

Party officers receiving public funding, public debate privileges, committee privileges, public party status, or constitutional procedural privileges may be subject to integrity and disclosure duties.

## Section 2. Duties

Such duties may include:

1. funding disclosure;
2. conflict disclosure;
3. anti-shell-party compliance;
4. common-control disclosure;
5. public funding compliance;
6. anti-coercion rules;
7. foreign-control prohibition;
8. public privilege audit.

## Section 3. Limits

Public service rules shall not control lawful party ideology, platform, internal debate, or peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

---

# XV. Enforcement

## Section 1. Enforcement Bodies

Public service and anti-capture rules may be enforced by:

1. Integrity System;
2. ethics bodies;
3. courts;
4. election bodies where party or candidate status is affected;
5. stewardship bodies where stewardship office is involved;
6. public audit bodies;
7. other lawful bodies created by constitutional statute.

## Section 2. Due Process

Enforcement requires:

1. notice;
2. reasoned explanation;
3. evidence standard;
4. opportunity to respond;
5. proportional remedy;
6. appeal path.

## Section 3. Sanction Categories

Sanctions may include:

1. warning;
2. cure order;
3. public correction;
4. recusal;
5. divestment;
6. repayment;
7. fine where lawful;
8. public censure;
9. suspension of privilege;
10. license suspension;
11. removal process;
12. referral for civil or criminal enforcement where lawful.

## Section 4. Proportionality

Sanctions shall distinguish honest mistake, curable error, negligence, bad faith, corruption, concealment, and abuse of office.

## Section 5. Anti-Weaponization

Public service rules may not be used in bad faith to exclude lawful candidates, harass opposition, punish dissent, or entrench incumbents.

---

# XVI. Public Records

## Section 1. Required Records

The Commonwealth shall maintain public records for:

1. official disclosures;
2. Public Trust License status where relevant;
3. conflicts;
4. recusals;
5. gifts where reportable;
6. beneficial ownership disclosures where public;
7. lobbying records;
8. revolving-door restrictions;
9. ethics findings;
10. enforcement outcomes;
11. cure status;
12. appeal outcomes.

## Section 2. Privacy

Public records shall reveal material public-interest information without exposing irrelevant private details.

## Section 3. Searchability

Records shall be searchable through the Public Constitutional Interface.

## Section 4. Correction

Officials and affected persons shall have correction paths for inaccurate records.
