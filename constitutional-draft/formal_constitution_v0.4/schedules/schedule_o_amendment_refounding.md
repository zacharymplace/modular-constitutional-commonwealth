## Purpose

This schedule defines amendment categories, amendment classification, public amendment process, delayed self-dealing, emergency amendment limits, and constitutional refounding.

Core rule:

> Those who change the rules of power may not be the first to benefit from those changes.

---

# I. Amendment Doctrine

## Section 1. Tiered Change

The Commonwealth distinguishes between:

1. ordinary policy;
2. major statutory architecture;
3. election and power rules;
4. framework amendments;
5. Democratic Kernel alteration.

## Section 2. Principle

The more a change affects future democratic competition, rights, courts, emergency authority, constitutional systems, or transfer of power, the stronger the process required.

## Section 3. Anti-Evasion

A proposal may not be placed in a lower amendment category merely to avoid public review, enhanced approval, delayed effect, rights analysis, fiscal analysis, or ratification.

## Section 4. Public Legibility

All amendment procedures shall be explained in plain language before public or legislative approval.

---

# II. Ordinary Policy

## Section 1. Definition

Ordinary policy includes laws, programs, budgets, services, regulations, administrative arrangements, and public policies that do not materially alter constitutional structure, power rules, the rights floor, or the Democratic Kernel.

## Section 2. Process

Ordinary policy may be changed through ordinary legislative procedure.

## Section 3. Limits

Ordinary policy may not be used to:

1. alter election rules;
2. alter party power rules;
3. restructure courts;
4. weaken stewardship institutions;
5. expand emergency authority;
6. hide public authority;
7. evade public accountability;
8. materially impair the rights floor;
9. alter the Democratic Kernel.

## Section 4. Reclassification

A proposal labeled ordinary policy may be reclassified where its effect is constitutional, structural, or power-altering.

---

# III. Major Statutory Architecture

## Section 1. Definition

Major statutory architecture includes laws that structure important public systems without directly amending the constitutional framework.

## Section 2. Covered Areas

Major statutory architecture includes changes to:

1. election administration;
2. courts;
3. stewardship systems;
4. emergency powers;
5. fiscal constitution;
6. technology governance;
7. civil service;
8. party finance;
9. public accountability infrastructure;
10. public audit systems;
11. procurement integrity systems;
12. public algorithm registries;
13. constitutional schedules;
14. system charters.

## Section 3. Required Process

Major statutory architecture requires:

1. enhanced legislative approval;
2. public review;
3. fiscal analysis;
4. rights analysis;
5. public impact statement;
6. implementation plan;
7. review or sunset where appropriate.

## Section 4. Public Review

Public review shall include reasonable opportunity for comment, expert input, affected-person input, and plain-language explanation.

## Section 5. Rights and Fiscal Analysis

Rights and fiscal analysis must be published before final approval except where urgent continuity measures justify temporary action subject to later review.

---

# IV. Election and Power Rules

## Section 1. Definition

Election and power rules include changes to:

1. election rules;
2. party rules;
3. districting;
4. office powers;
5. court structure;
6. campaign finance;
7. public funding rules;
8. emergency authority;
9. stewardship appointment rules;
10. transfer-of-power rules;
11. coalition formation rules;
12. public party status rules;
13. security powers affecting political competition.

## Section 2. Required Process

Election and power rule changes require:

1. enhanced approval;
2. independent review;
3. public plain-language impact statement;
4. rights analysis;
5. fiscal analysis where material;
6. delayed effect after an intervening election;
7. public challenge period.

## Section 3. Delayed Self-Dealing Rule

Those who change election or power rules may not be the first to benefit from those changes.

## Section 4. Immediate Effect Exception

Immediate effect may be allowed only for narrow continuity measures necessary to preserve rights, elections, courts, public safety, or lawful administration.

Immediate effect may not be used for partisan advantage.

## Section 5. Independent Review

Independent review shall assess whether the proposal:

1. benefits incumbents;
2. harms opposition rights;
3. restricts emergent parties;
4. affects transfer of power;
5. alters court independence;
6. changes emergency authority;
7. affects public funding or campaign finance;
8. affects democratic competition.

---

# V. Framework Amendments

## Section 1. Definition

Framework amendments alter constitutional structure without altering the Democratic Kernel.

Framework amendments may affect:

1. constitutional systems;
2. branches of government;
3. federal-state-local structure;
4. amendment procedures;
5. stewardship architecture;
6. court hierarchy;
7. emergency structure;
8. fiscal constitution;
9. technology constitution;
10. transition architecture.

## Section 2. Required Process

Framework amendments require:

1. supermajority approval;
2. public deliberation;
3. independent legal review;
4. rights review;
5. fiscal review where material;
6. delayed effect where power rules are implicated;
7. public referendum or regional ratification;
8. plain-language public guide.

## Section 3. Public Ratification

Public ratification may occur through referendum, regional ratification, or another constitutionally authorized democratic process.

## Section 4. Regional Ratification

Where federal, state, regional, or local authority is materially affected, regional participation or ratification shall be required.

## Section 5. Framework Limit

Framework amendments may not remove, suspend, hollow out, or materially impair the Democratic Kernel.

---

# VI. Democratic Kernel Protection

## Section 1. Kernel Alteration

A Kernel alteration is any change that removes, suspends, hollows out, or materially impairs the Democratic Kernel.

## Section 2. Ordinary Amendment Prohibited

The Democratic Kernel may not be altered through ordinary amendment, ordinary law, emergency decree, administrative action, party rule, court restructuring, fiscal starvation, or delegated private action.

## Section 3. Refounding Required

Kernel alteration requires full constitutional refounding.

## Section 4. Fast Kernel Review

Alleged Kernel impairment through amendment, statute, emergency action, or institutional restructuring shall receive expedited constitutional review.

---

# VII. Amendment Classification Review

## Section 1. Purpose

Amendment Classification Review determines whether a proposal is ordinary policy, major statutory architecture, election and power rule, framework amendment, or Kernel alteration.

## Section 2. Timing

Classification review shall occur before final legislative adoption or public vote where classification is disputed or where the proposal materially affects power rules.

## Section 3. Review Factors

Review shall consider whether the proposal affects:

1. rights;
2. elections;
3. party competition;
4. office powers;
5. courts;
6. stewardship bodies;
7. emergency powers;
8. fiscal constitution;
9. technology governance;
10. security neutrality;
11. federal structure;
12. amendment rules;
13. transfer of power;
14. Democratic Kernel.

## Section 4. Expedited Review

Classification review shall be expedited so it does not become a tool of delay.

## Section 5. Public Reasoning

Classification decisions shall include public reasoning.

---

# VIII. Public Amendment Guides

## Section 1. Requirement

All proposed amendments shall be accompanied by a plain-language public amendment guide.

## Section 2. Required Contents

A public amendment guide shall explain:

1. what the amendment changes;
2. why it is proposed;
3. who proposed it;
4. what powers are affected;
5. what rights are affected;
6. fiscal effect where material;
7. party or election effects where material;
8. implementation timeline;
9. arguments for and against;
10. public challenge or comment process;
11. effective date;
12. delayed self-dealing effect where applicable.

## Section 3. Independent Preparation

Public amendment guides shall be prepared or reviewed by an independent body.

## Section 4. Bias Prohibited

Public amendment guides shall not be written as partisan advocacy.

Advocacy materials may exist separately, but official public guides must be fair and explanatory.

---

# IX. Amendment Integrity Disclosures

## Section 1. Required Disclosures

Amendment processes shall disclose:

1. sponsors;
2. draft language sources;
3. lobbying contacts;
4. major funders where required by law;
5. affiliated entities;
6. fiscal impact;
7. rights impact;
8. party effects;
9. public authority effects;
10. influence records required by law.

## Section 2. Draft Language Provenance

Material draft language sources shall be disclosed, including where language is provided by:

1. public officials;
2. parties;
3. lobbyists;
4. think tanks;
5. advocacy groups;
6. vendors;
7. donor networks;
8. foreign-linked entities;
9. public agencies;
10. courts or stewardship bodies.

## Section 3. Influence Ledger

Major amendments shall include an amendment influence ledger.

## Section 4. Failure to Disclose

Material failure to disclose influence, sponsors, or draft sources may trigger public correction, delay, integrity review, or other lawful remedy.

---

# X. Emergency Amendment Limits

## Section 1. Emergency Restriction

Emergency conditions shall not be used to alter election or power rules except for narrow continuity measures.

## Section 2. Continuity Measures

A continuity measure must be:

1. necessary;
2. temporary;
3. proportionate;
4. rights-preserving;
5. publicly justified;
6. independently reviewed;
7. not designed for partisan advantage;
8. subject to delayed self-dealing where applicable.

## Section 3. Prohibited Emergency Amendments

Emergency authority may not be used to:

1. cancel future elections indefinitely;
2. entrench incumbents;
3. weaken opposition rights;
4. capture courts;
5. capture election administration;
6. extend emergency power permanently;
7. alter the Democratic Kernel;
8. suppress lawful dissent;
9. remove public accountability;
10. weaken transfer-of-power rules.

## Section 4. Post-Emergency Review

Emergency continuity amendments shall receive post-emergency review.

---

# XI. Refounding Procedure

## Section 1. Refounding Defined

Refounding is the lawful replacement or alteration of the Democratic Kernel or constitutional order through a full constituent process.

## Section 2. Required Elements

A constitutional refounding process must include:

1. public notice;
2. public deliberation;
3. representative or civic convention process;
4. rights continuity;
5. independent legal review;
6. public explanation;
7. supermajority approval;
8. public ratification;
9. transition safeguards;
10. no emergency shortcut.

## Section 3. Rights Continuity

During refounding, existing rights protections remain in effect unless and until lawfully replaced by equal or stronger protections through legitimate public ratification.

## Section 4. Public Participation

Refounding must include broad public participation and cannot be controlled exclusively by incumbents, one party, one branch, one region, one donor network, or one expert body.

## Section 5. Anti-Capture

Refounding processes shall include safeguards against:

1. incumbent capture;
2. donor capture;
3. party cartelization;
4. security coercion;
5. emergency manipulation;
6. foreign control;
7. exclusion of lawful opposition;
8. suppression of dissent.

## Section 6. Ratification

Refounding requires public ratification through a legitimate democratic process.

## Section 7. Transition

A refounding process must include a transition plan preserving rights, courts, elections, public accountability, and essential services.

---

# XII. Amendment Timelines

## Section 1. Public Notice Period

Constitutional amendments shall have public notice periods sufficient for deliberation.

## Section 2. Challenge Period

Amendments shall have a public challenge period before final adoption or ratification.

## Section 3. Expedited Process

Expedited process may exist only for narrow continuity, technical correction, or urgent rights-preserving measures.

## Section 4. Anti-Rush Rule

Amendments affecting elections, power rules, courts, emergency authority, or the Democratic Kernel shall not be rushed through procedures that prevent meaningful public review.

---

# XIII. Amendment Recordkeeping

## Section 1. Public Record

Each amendment proposal shall have a public record.

## Section 2. Required Fields

The record shall show:

1. text;
2. sponsor;
3. classification;
4. legal effect;
5. fiscal analysis;
6. rights analysis;
7. election or party impact;
8. emergency impact where relevant;
9. public comments;
10. influence ledger;
11. version history;
12. vote history;
13. ratification process;
14. effective date.

## Section 3. Version History

Material amendment language changes shall preserve version history.

## Section 4. Public Interface

Amendment records shall be available through the Public Constitutional Interface.

---

# XIV. Constitutional Schedules

## Section 1. Schedule Status

Constitutional schedules are part of the constitutional order.

## Section 2. Schedule Amendments

A schedule may be amended according to the process applicable to its subject matter.

## Section 3. Schedule Conflicts

Where a schedule conflicts with the core Constitution, the core Constitution controls.

## Section 4. Technical Updates

Technical updates to schedules may be permitted through simplified process only where they do not materially affect rights, power rules, democratic competition, emergency authority, or the Democratic Kernel.

## Section 5. Public Notice

Schedule amendments require public notice and version history.

---

# XV. System Charters and Implementation Codes

## Section 1. System Charters

System charters define operating rules for constitutional systems.

## Section 2. Implementation Codes

Implementation codes define administrative procedures, technical standards, data schemas, interface rules, and operational practices.

## Section 3. Legal Status

System charters and implementation codes may be legally binding where adopted by constitutional statute.

## Section 4. Limits

System charters and implementation codes may not override the core Constitution or constitutional schedules.

## Section 5. Review

System charters and implementation codes shall be reviewable for rights compliance, public accountability, and consistency with constitutional purpose.

---

# XVI. Anti-Weaponization Rule

## Section 1. Rule

Amendment procedures may not be used in bad faith to disable lawful opposition, suppress dissent, obstruct ordinary administration, entrench incumbents, or evade accountability.

## Section 2. Applies To

This rule applies to:

1. amendment classification;
2. public guides;
3. emergency amendment claims;
4. schedule amendments;
5. transition amendments;
6. refounding processes;
7. power-rule changes;
8. public ratification procedures.

## Section 3. Remedy

Bad-faith weaponization may result in classification correction, delay, public finding, integrity review, court review, or invalidation where lawful.

---

The amendment system shall be reviewed periodically.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

A public report shall summarize amendment-system performance and recommend changes where needed.
