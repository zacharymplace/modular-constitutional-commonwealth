## Purpose

This schedule defines party power roles, party qualification, common-control standards, coalition transparency, public party ledgers, public funding, debate access, party compliance, and anti-cartel protections.

Core rule:

> Parties compete for power. They do not own the state.

---

# I. Party Governance Doctrine

## Section 1. Parties as Democratic Infrastructure

Political parties are democratic infrastructure.

They may organize political competition, present programs, nominate candidates, form governments, oppose governments, and participate in coalitions.

They may not own election machinery, courts, stewardship systems, public funding rules, districting rules, emergency powers, public technology, or the machinery of future competition.

## Section 2. No Permanent Party Ownership

No party receives permanent constitutional status by age, wealth, incumbency, historical dominance, donor support, media dominance, or prior officeholding.

## Section 3. Voter-Ranked Power

Party power is ranked by voter support, coalition status, constitutional compliance, and public qualification thresholds.

## Section 4. Stewardship Separation

The machinery of political competition shall be protected by nonpartisan Stewardship Power.

Stewardship Power is not a party role.

---

# II. Recognized Party Power Roles

## Section 1. Roles

The Commonwealth recognizes:

1. Governing Power;
2. Opposition Power;
3. Plurality Power;
4. Emergent Power;
5. Stewardship Power as a nonpartisan constitutional function.

## Section 2. Governing Power

The Governing Power is the party or coalition with the lawful governing mandate.

It may:

1. form government;
2. propose budgets;
3. lead ordinary legislative agenda;
4. appoint executive officials subject to law;
5. implement policy;
6. negotiate coalition agreements.

It may not:

1. control election administration;
2. weaken opposition rights;
3. capture stewardship institutions;
4. change power rules for immediate benefit;
5. use emergency powers for entrenchment;
6. use public technology or security forces for partisan advantage.

## Section 3. Opposition Power

The Opposition Power is the largest non-governing party or coalition.

It receives protected authority to:

1. audit;
2. investigate;
3. question government;
4. challenge executive action;
5. publish minority reports;
6. participate in emergency review;
7. access budget information;
8. access government information subject to lawful limits;
9. offer a public governing alternative.

## Section 4. Plurality Power

Plurality Power belongs to qualifying minority blocs beyond the principal opposition.

It may receive:

1. agenda time;
2. proportional committee participation;
3. minority impact statement rights;
4. review-trigger authority where defined;
5. debate access;
6. public funding access subject to compliance;
7. participation in constitutional and emergency reviews where relevant.

## Section 5. Emergent Power

Emergent Power protects new, regional, reformist, or growing parties.

It may provide paths to:

1. ballot access;
2. verified emergent status;
3. limited debate access;
4. limited public funding;
5. legislative representation;
6. Plurality Power.

## Section 6. Stewardship Power

Stewardship Power protects democratic machinery, including:

1. elections;
2. courts;
3. public audit;
4. integrity systems;
5. technology governance;
6. emergency review;
7. fiscal scoring;
8. peaceful transfer of power.

Stewardship Power does not govern ordinary policy and shall not become a party.

---

# III. Party Role Assignment

## Section 1. Assignment Factors

Party roles shall be assigned based on:

1. certified election results;
2. vote share;
3. seat share;
4. governing coalition status;
5. confidence status;
6. opposition status;
7. common-control findings;
8. constitutional compliance;
9. public qualification thresholds;
10. party mergers, splits, or affiliations.

## Section 2. Lawful Governing Mandate

A lawful governing mandate means confidence-backed or constitutionally recognized authority to form government under the electoral and coalition rules.

## Section 3. Provisional Party Role Assignment

After certified election results, the Democratic System shall issue provisional party role assignments within a defined period.

Provisional assignments remain effective until final assignment, appeal resolution, or lawful correction.

## Section 4. Appeal

Party role assignments shall be appealable through the Election and Party Court, with constitutional review where Kernel, rights, or framework issues are implicated.

## Section 5. Public Explanation

Each party role assignment shall include a public explanation understandable to ordinary voters.

---

# IV. Confidence-and-Supply

## Section 1. Definition

A confidence-and-supply arrangement exists where a party or bloc agrees to support government formation, confidence votes, budgets, or essential supply without formally entering the governing coalition.

## Section 2. Opposition Classification

A party in a formal confidence-and-supply arrangement shall not automatically qualify as Opposition Power.

## Section 3. Public Disclosure

Confidence-and-supply agreements must be publicly disclosed.

They shall identify:

1. participating parties;
2. support obligations;
3. duration;
4. budget commitments;
5. policy commitments;
6. offices or committee arrangements;
7. dispute-resolution process;
8. termination conditions.

## Section 4. Status Review

The Democratic System shall review whether a confidence-and-supply party remains eligible for Plurality or Emergent rights.

## Section 5. Anti-Evasion

A party may not claim Opposition Power while materially supporting government in ways equivalent to governing coalition participation.

---

# V. Party Qualification Thresholds

## Section 1. Threshold Categories

Party qualification thresholds may include:

1. ballot access threshold;
2. verified emergent party threshold;
3. debate access threshold;
4. limited public funding threshold;
5. legislative representation threshold;
6. Plurality Power threshold;
7. committee rights threshold;
8. coalition governing eligibility;
9. constitutional procedural privilege threshold.

## Section 2. Entry and Formal Power

Entry into political competition shall be easier than access to formal power, public funding, or constitutional privileges.

## Section 3. Anti-Cartel Requirement

Thresholds shall not be designed to preserve incumbent parties or block genuine emergent parties.

## Section 4. Review

Thresholds shall be periodically reviewed for cartelization, fragmentation, governability, voter choice, and public trust.

## Section 5. Parameter Location

Exact thresholds shall be defined in Schedule B or constitutional statute, consistent with this Schedule.

---

# VI. Emergent Party Escalator

## Section 1. Purpose

The Emergent Party Escalator gives new, regional, reformist, or growing parties a lawful path into public competition.

## Section 2. Stages

| Stage | Status | Rights |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Registered party | ballot pathway |
| Stage 2 | Verified emergent party | limited debate access |
| Stage 3 | Public support party | limited public funding |
| Stage 4 | Legislative party | seat eligibility |
| Stage 5 | Plurality party | Plurality Power rights |

## Section 3. Compliance Requirements

Emergent parties must comply with:

1. funding disclosure;
2. anti-shell-party rules;
3. internal governance standards;
4. anti-coercion rules;
5. constitutional compliance;
6. common-control review where applicable.

## Section 4. Anti-Gatekeeping

Established parties may not use ballot access, debate access, public funding, or party-status rules to suffocate genuine emergent parties.

---

# VII. Common Control

## Section 1. Common Control Defined

Parties may be treated as affiliated where they share material control, funding, leadership, vendors, data systems, donor networks, candidate pipelines, strategic direction, or other evidence of common political control.

## Section 2. Evidence Standard

A common-control finding must be based on substantial evidence of material shared control, coordination, or dependency.

Relevant evidence may include:

1. shared major funders;
2. shared executive leadership;
3. shared vendors;
4. shared data infrastructure;
5. coordinated candidate placement;
6. undisclosed coalition agreements;
7. common strategic direction;
8. shared policy-drafting infrastructure;
9. recurring leadership exchange;
10. concealed financial dependency;
11. common campaign operations;
12. shared beneficial ownership or affiliated entities.

## Section 3. Ideological Similarity Insufficient

Ideological similarity, shared policy goals, public coalition openness, or lawful cooperation alone is insufficient for a common-control finding.

## Section 4. Process

Common-control findings require:

1. evidence-based determination;
2. public reasoning;
3. opportunity to respond;
4. appeal path;
5. proportionate remedy.

## Section 5. Remedies

Common-control remedies may include aggregation for:

1. public funding;
2. debate access;
3. committee allocation;
4. Plurality Power qualification;
5. Emergent Power qualification;
6. coalition leverage calculation;
7. anti-cartel review.

## Section 6. Limits

A common-control finding shall not automatically dissolve a party or suppress lawful political association.

## Section 7. Appeal

Common-control findings shall have expedited appeal where election timing, coalition formation, funding, debate access, or party role status is materially affected.

---

# VIII. Anti-Shell-Party Rules

## Section 1. Shell Party Defined

A shell party is a party that presents itself as independent while materially controlled by another party, donor network, governing coalition, foreign actor, vendor network, or concealed strategic controller.

## Section 2. Prohibited Purpose

A party may not create fake independence to manipulate:

1. party roles;
2. public funding;
3. debate access;
4. coalition leverage;
5. committee allocation;
6. opposition rights;
7. plurality rights;
8. emergent party protections;
9. public party status;
10. campaign finance limits.

## Section 3. Disclosure

Parties must disclose material:

1. shared funding;
2. shared vendors;
3. shared leadership;
4. shared data infrastructure;
5. affiliated entities;
6. donor networks;
7. coalition agreements;
8. strategic control relationships.

## Section 4. Remedy

Where shell-party manipulation is found, remedies shall be proportional and may include aggregation, loss of privilege, repayment of public funds, public correction, integrity referral, or other lawful remedy.

---

# IX. Coalition Transparency

## Section 1. Coalition Agreement Required

A governing coalition must publish a coalition agreement.

## Section 2. Required Contents

A coalition agreement shall disclose:

1. member parties;
2. governing priorities;
3. cabinet allocation;
4. budget commitments;
5. committee and control arrangements;
6. constitutional red lines;
7. dispute-resolution process;
8. duration or review date;
9. confidence agreement terms.

## Section 3. Coalition Signal Requirement

Before elections, parties shall publish where feasible:

1. preferred coalition partners;
2. parties they refuse to govern with;
3. non-negotiable platform commitments;
4. constitutional commitments;
5. major policy red lines.

Departure from pre-election coalition signals must be publicly explained.

## Section 4. Side Agreement Disclosure

Material coalition side agreements affecting offices, budgets, appointments, public funding, procurement, regional concessions, committee control, or constitutional procedure must be disclosed.

## Section 5. Secret Terms

Secret coalition terms affecting public authority are unenforceable and subject to integrity review.

---

# X. Proportional Leverage Rule

## Section 1. Rule

Coalition influence shall remain broadly proportional to public support.

## Section 2. Permitted Negotiation

Minority parties may negotiate:

1. policy commitments;
2. cabinet participation;
3. committee seats;
4. regional priorities;
5. legislative agenda items.

## Section 3. Prohibited Bargaining Chips

Minority or regional parties may not trade support for control over:

1. election administration;
2. courts;
3. integrity bodies;
4. technology stewardship;
5. emergency renewal rules;
6. public audit systems;
7. party finance rules;
8. prosecutorial discretion;
9. constitutional appointment channels;
10. fiscal scoring;
11. transfer-of-power safeguards.

## Section 4. Public Review

Coalition arrangements that appear to violate proportional leverage may be reviewed by the Election and Party Court or Integrity System where appropriate.

---

# XI. Public Party Status Ledger

## Section 1. Requirement

After every election, coalition formation, coalition collapse, confidence vote, material party split, material party merger, or common-control finding, the Democratic System shall publish a Public Party Status Ledger.

## Section 2. Ledger Contents

The Ledger shall show:

1. vote share;
2. district seats;
3. proportional seats;
4. overhang seats;
5. adjustment seats;
6. final seats;
7. governing bloc;
8. principal opposition;
9. plurality blocs;
10. emergent parties;
11. coalition agreement;
12. confidence-and-supply agreements;
13. committee control;
14. public funding;
15. debate access;
16. common-control findings;
17. compliance status;
18. next confidence or review date.

## Section 3. Publication Deadline

The Ledger shall be published within a defined period after certification or coalition change.

## Section 4. Provisional Ledger

A provisional Ledger shall be issued where appeals or disputes remain pending.

## Section 5. Public Explanation

The Ledger shall include plain-language explanation of:

1. who governs;
2. who is opposition;
3. which parties hold plurality or emergent status;
4. what coalition agreements exist;
5. how the public may challenge errors.

## Section 6. Appeal

Ledger entries shall be appealable where they materially affect party status, public funding, debate access, committee rights, or coalition authority.

---

# XII. Public Funding

## Section 1. Purpose

Public funding exists to support democratic competition, not fake parties, donor laundering, or shell organizations.

## Section 2. Staged Funding

Public funding shall be staged by:

1. demonstrated support;
2. transparency compliance;
3. internal governance compliance;
4. anti-shell-party compliance;
5. prior audit results.

## Section 3. Emergent Party Support

Verified emergent parties may receive limited public support sufficient to compete, subject to compliance.

## Section 4. Compliance

Public funding may be suspended, reduced, or recovered for serious noncompliance, subject to due process and appeal.

## Section 5. Funding Transparency

Public funding amounts, basis, conditions, and compliance status shall be published in the Public Party Status Ledger.

## Section 6. No Funding Mills

Public funding rules shall be designed to prevent artificial parties, sham candidacies, or affiliated entities created primarily to extract public funds.

---

# XIII. Debate Access

## Section 1. Public Debate Access

Debate access rules shall balance openness, seriousness, public relevance, and anti-cartel protections.

## Section 2. Criteria

Debate access may consider:

1. polling;
2. prior vote share;
3. petition signatures;
4. regional support;
5. verified emergent status;
6. local district representation;
7. public funding qualification;
8. constitutional compliance.

## Section 3. Emergent Access

Verified emergent parties shall have a realistic path to public debate access.

## Section 4. Anti-Gatekeeping

Debate access rules may not be designed to protect incumbent parties from genuine competition.

## Section 5. Appeal

Debate access decisions shall be public, reasoned, and appealable.

---

# XIV. Party Internal Governance

## Section 1. Public-Privilege Condition

Parties receiving public funding, formal debate access, party role privileges, or constitutional procedural rights may be required to meet internal governance standards.

## Section 2. Minimum Standards

Minimum standards may include:

1. leadership selection transparency;
2. candidate selection transparency;
3. financial controls;
4. anti-coercion rules;
5. member rights;
6. internal dispute resolution;
7. conflict disclosure;
8. anti-harassment standards;
9. public funding compliance;
10. constitutional compliance.

## Section 3. Ideological Neutrality

Internal governance rules shall not be used to control party ideology, platform, lawful dissent, or peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 4. Party Autonomy

Parties retain autonomy over lawful platform, strategy, values, and internal political debate, subject to constitutional compliance and public-privilege conditions.

---

# XV. Party Finance and Influence

## Section 1. Disclosure

Parties must disclose material funding sources, donor networks, affiliated entities, vendors, campaign infrastructure, and policy-drafting support as required by law.

## Section 2. True Source

Party finance rules shall require true-source disclosure where legally required and shall prohibit concealment through intermediaries, shell entities, pass-through organizations, or affiliated networks.

## Section 3. Foreign Control

Foreign control of party finance, candidate selection, campaign infrastructure, or strategic control is prohibited.

## Section 4. Influence Ecosystem Disclosure

Parties may be required to disclose material relationships with:

1. affiliated nonprofits;
2. donor networks;
3. think tanks;
4. litigation funds;
5. aligned media entities;
6. candidate-training organizations;
7. campaign data vendors;
8. lobbyist bundlers;
9. political consulting firms;
10. policy-drafting organizations;
11. issue advocacy groups;
12. foreign-linked entities.

## Section 5. Lawful Advocacy

Disclosure rules shall not suppress lawful advocacy, association, journalism, protest, scholarship, or civic participation.

---

# XVI. Regional Parties

## Section 1. Legitimacy

Regional parties are legitimate participants in Commonwealth democracy.

## Section 2. Qualification

Regional parties may qualify for representation through:

1. regional threshold;
2. local district victories;
3. constitutionally authorized regional support criteria.

## Section 3. Regional Leverage Cap

Regional parties may advocate regional interests and join coalitions, but they may not trade support for control over:

1. election administration;
2. courts;
3. emergency rules;
4. fiscal scoring;
5. technology stewardship;
6. integrity systems;
7. constitutional appointments;
8. transfer-of-power rules.

## Section 4. Rights Floor

Regional party influence may not be used to weaken the rights floor or public accountability requirements.

---

# XVII. Party Compliance and Sanctions

## Section 1. Compliance Areas

Party compliance may include:

1. funding disclosure;
2. internal governance;
3. anti-coercion;
4. anti-shell-party rules;
5. common-control review;
6. public funding conditions;
7. debate access conditions;
8. coalition transparency;
9. constitutional compliance.

## Section 2. Proportional Sanctions

Sanctions shall be proportionate and may include:

1. notice;
2. cure period;
3. correction order;
4. public finding;
5. repayment of public funds;
6. funding reduction;
7. temporary loss of public privilege;
8. aggregation under common-control rules;
9. integrity referral;
10. disqualification only for severe, lawful cause.

## Section 3. Cure

Good-faith or curable defects should receive cure opportunity where consistent with public integrity.

## Section 4. Due Process

Party sanctions require notice, public reasoning, opportunity to respond, and appeal path.

## Section 5. Anti-Weaponization

Party compliance rules may not be used in bad faith to disable lawful opposition, suppress dissent, or entrench incumbents.

---

# XVIII. Party Dispute Resolution

## Section 1. Forum

Party governance disputes may be heard by the Election and Party Court or other lawful forum.

## Section 2. Covered Disputes

Disputes may include:

1. party role assignment;
2. common-control findings;
3. coalition status;
4. confidence-and-supply classification;
5. public funding;
6. debate access;
7. party ledger entries;
8. regional party qualification;
9. public privilege sanctions;
10. party compliance findings.

## Section 3. Expedited Review

Disputes affecting government formation, election timing, ballot access, public funding, debate access, or opposition rights shall receive expedited review.

## Section 4. Provisional Relief

Courts may issue provisional relief to preserve democratic competition while disputes are resolved.

---

# XIX. Ideological Neutrality

## Section 1. Rule

Party regulation shall be ideologically neutral.

## Section 2. Permitted Regulation

Party regulation may target:

1. corruption;
2. coercion;
3. violence;
4. foreign control;
5. shell-party manipulation;
6. election sabotage;
7. refusal of lawful transfer;
8. fraud;
9. unlawful intimidation;
10. financing violations;
11. anti-constitutional action crossing defined thresholds.

## Section 3. Prohibited Regulation

Party regulation may not suppress:

1. lawful dissent;
2. unpopular ideology;
3. peaceful radicalism;
4. criticism of government;
5. constitutional reform advocacy;
6. lawful protest;
7. internal debate;
8. ordinary policy disagreement.

## Section 4. Anti-Constitutional Action

Anti-constitutional action shall be defined narrowly and may include violence, coercion, foreign control, rights-destroying conduct, election sabotage, or refusal of lawful constitutional transfer.
