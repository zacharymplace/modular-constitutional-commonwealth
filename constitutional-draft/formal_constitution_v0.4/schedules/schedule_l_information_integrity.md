# Schedule L — Information Integrity

## Purpose

This schedule defines information-integrity rules that protect free expression while exposing manipulation, hidden funding, synthetic deception, coordinated inauthentic behavior, and foreign control.

Core rule:

> Protect speech. Expose manipulation. Do not create a state monopoly on truth.

---

# I. Free Expression Doctrine

## Section 1. Protected Expression

The Commonwealth protects:

1. speech;
2. press;
3. disagreement;
4. criticism;
5. satire;
6. parody;
7. dissent;
8. protest;
9. artistic expression;
10. anonymous lawful expression;
11. vulnerable-speaker expression;
12. peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 2. No State Monopoly on Truth

The state shall not create a monopoly on truth.

Information-integrity powers shall not be used to suppress lawful disagreement, opposition, criticism, satire, minority views, unpopular views, or peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 3. Disclosure Preference

Disclosure shall be the default remedy for information-integrity concerns.

Suppression shall be limited to narrow unlawful categories defined by law and subject to independent review.

---

# II. Covered Information Integrity Risks

## Section 1. Covered Risks

This Schedule applies to information practices involving:

1. political ad funding;
2. synthetic media;
3. coordinated inauthentic behavior;
4. foreign control or covert foreign influence;
5. platform amplification systems;
6. media ownership;
7. major political data operations;
8. fraudulent impersonation;
9. material non-disclosure;
10. covert paid influence;
11. election-related deception;
12. public-authority manipulation.

## Section 2. Manipulation Defined

Covered manipulation may include:

1. funding opacity;
2. synthetic deception;
3. foreign control;
4. coordinated inauthentic behavior;
5. material non-disclosure;
6. fraudulent impersonation;
7. undisclosed paid political influence;
8. falsified source attribution;
9. covert amplification;
10. concealed platform or data manipulation.

## Section 3. Lawful Organizing Protected

Coordinated lawful organizing, coalition activity, advocacy, protest, journalism, scholarship, public-interest litigation, and ordinary political persuasion are not manipulation solely because they are coordinated.

---

# III. Viewpoint Neutrality

## Section 1. Rule

Information-integrity rules shall not discriminate based on lawful viewpoint.

## Section 2. Protected Viewpoints

The Commonwealth shall not target expression merely because it is:

1. unpopular;
2. radical;
3. anti-incumbent;
4. anti-government;
5. pro-reform;
6. critical of courts;
7. critical of parties;
8. critical of security forces;
9. critical of public technology;
10. offensive but lawful.

## Section 3. Enforcement Burden

Where information-integrity enforcement burdens political speech, the enforcing authority must show lawful basis, viewpoint neutrality, proportionality, and reviewability.

---

# IV. Political Advertising Transparency

## Section 1. Disclosure Requirement

Political advertising may be required to disclose:

1. sponsor;
2. funding source;
3. true source of funds where required by law;
4. major donor or funder class where required;
5. affiliated entity;
6. foreign-linked source where applicable;
7. targeting criteria where material and lawful;
8. synthetic media use where material;
9. public archive reference.

## Section 2. Public Ad Archive

The Commonwealth may maintain a public political ad archive.

The archive may include:

1. ad content;
2. sponsor;
3. funding disclosure;
4. targeting category;
5. dates shown;
6. platform or medium;
7. amount spent;
8. synthetic or altered media label;
9. appeal or correction history.

## Section 3. Small Speaker Protection

Political ad transparency rules shall not impose disproportionate burdens on small speakers, local civic groups, or ordinary citizens.

## Section 4. True Source Rule

Political ad funding may not be concealed through shell entities, pass-through organizations, false sponsors, or undisclosed paid intermediaries where disclosure is required by law.

---

# V. Synthetic Media

## Section 1. Synthetic Media Defined

Synthetic media includes materially altered, generated, simulated, or manipulated audio, video, image, or text used in public communication.

## Section 2. Material Deception

Synthetic media disclosure may be required where the media is likely to materially deceive a reasonable person about:

1. speaker identity;
2. event occurrence;
3. candidate conduct;
4. official statement;
5. emergency information;
6. voting procedure;
7. public authority action;
8. material political fact.

## Section 3. Satire and Parody Safe Harbor

Satire, parody, artistic expression, commentary, obvious fiction, and clearly expressive manipulation shall not be treated as unlawful deception solely because synthetic or altered media is used.

## Section 4. Labeling

Reasonable labeling may be required for materially deceptive political communication where lawful, proportionate, viewpoint-neutral, and reviewable.

## Section 5. Takedown Limits

Removal or suppression of synthetic media shall be reserved for narrow unlawful categories and shall be subject to independent review where political expression is affected.

---

# VI. Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## Section 1. Definition

Coordinated inauthentic behavior means coordinated action that materially misrepresents identity, source, scale, automation, affiliation, or sponsorship in order to manipulate public discourse or electoral participation.

## Section 2. Indicators

Indicators may include:

1. fake accounts;
2. bot networks;
3. undisclosed automation;
4. false identity clusters;
5. coordinated false source attribution;
6. hidden paid amplification;
7. cross-platform manipulation;
8. mass impersonation;
9. coordinated evasion of disclosure rules.

## Section 3. Lawful Coordination Excluded

The following are not inauthentic solely because they are coordinated:

1. campaign organizing;
2. union organizing;
3. protest organizing;
4. advocacy campaigns;
5. party messaging;
6. coalition communications;
7. civic association;
8. journalism networks;
9. public-interest litigation campaigns.

## Section 4. Remedies

Remedies should prioritize disclosure, labeling, platform transparency, public explanation, and enforcement against fraudulent conduct.

Suppression shall be limited to unlawful conduct or platform action under law.

---

# VII. Foreign-Linked Political Communication

## Section 1. Transparency

The Commonwealth may require transparency for foreign-linked political communication where foreign control, funding, direction, or material influence exists.

## Section 2. Protected International Speech

Foreign-linked transparency rules shall not stigmatize lawful diaspora speech, international journalism, academic exchange, human rights advocacy, or ordinary cross-border civic communication.

## Section 3. Foreign Control

Foreign control of party finance, candidate selection, campaign infrastructure, political data operations, or covert strategic direction is prohibited.

## Section 4. Required Disclosure

Disclosure may be required for:

1. foreign government funding;
2. foreign party direction;
3. foreign-controlled entities;
4. foreign-funded political ads;
5. covert foreign influence operations;
6. foreign-linked data operations;
7. foreign-directed synthetic media campaigns.

## Section 5. Review

Foreign-linked communication enforcement shall be subject to independent review and appeal.

---

# VIII. Platform Amplification Transparency

## Section 1. Covered Systems

Platform amplification transparency may apply to large-scale systems that materially shape political communication, public emergency information, election information, or public-interest discourse.

## Section 2. Required Transparency

Where lawful and proportionate, platforms may be required to disclose:

1. political ad policies;
2. amplification rules;
3. paid promotion rules;
4. synthetic media labeling rules;
5. coordinated inauthentic behavior policies;
6. major political data-use practices;
7. state-requested moderation actions;
8. appeal processes.

## Section 3. No Editorial Control by State

Transparency rules shall not give the state power to dictate lawful editorial judgment, lawful ranking preferences, or lawful moderation choices except as authorized by law and consistent with free expression.

## Section 4. Political Takedown Due Process

Where state action materially causes removal, suppression, demotion, or labeling of political speech, affected persons shall have notice and review where lawful and practicable.

---

# IX. Media Ownership Transparency

## Section 1. Disclosure

Media ownership transparency may require disclosure of:

1. beneficial ownership;
2. controlling interests;
3. major funders;
4. foreign ownership or control;
5. political entities with material control;
6. public funding where relevant;
7. affiliated entities.

## Section 2. Limit

Media ownership transparency shall not become licensing control over lawful press activity.

## Section 3. Public Interest

The purpose is to expose material control, not to punish viewpoint.

---

# X. Major Political Data Operations

## Section 1. Covered Operations

Major political data operations may include large-scale collection, brokerage, modeling, targeting, or use of personal data for election, party, campaign, political advertising, or public-opinion influence.

## Section 2. Transparency

Transparency may be required for:

1. data sources;
2. targeting categories;
3. data brokers;
4. model vendors;
5. affiliated entities;
6. foreign-linked entities;
7. consent or legal basis;
8. sensitive data use;
9. retention practices;
10. security practices.

## Section 3. Sensitive Data

Use of sensitive personal data for political targeting shall be subject to heightened regulation and rights review.

## Section 4. Public Technology Cross-Reference

Where political data operations interact with public systems, public records, election administration, or public authority, Schedule G and Schedule F may also apply.

---

# XI. Anonymous and Vulnerable Speech

## Section 1. Protection

The Commonwealth shall protect lawful anonymous, pseudonymous, unpopular, minority, dissident, and vulnerable-speaker expression.

## Section 2. Disclosure Limits

Disclosure may be required only where constitutionally justified by:

1. funding transparency;
2. foreign control;
3. fraud;
4. coordinated inauthentic conduct;
5. public-office conflict;
6. election integrity;
7. legally required campaign finance disclosure;
8. other lawful basis.

## Section 3. Safety Exception

Where public disclosure would create serious safety risk and public-interest need can be satisfied by confidential disclosure to an oversight body, confidential disclosure may be used.

## Section 4. No Retaliatory Exposure

Disclosure rules may not be used to expose vulnerable speakers for retaliation, harassment, discrimination, or political punishment.

---

# XII. State Action and Platform Action

## Section 1. State Action

Where the state requests, coerces, commands, significantly encourages, or materially participates in content restriction, constitutional free-expression protections apply.

## Section 2. Platform Independence

Private platforms retain lawful editorial and moderation discretion, subject to applicable law.

## Section 3. State Coercion Prohibited

The state may not pressure platforms to suppress lawful speech indirectly where it could not lawfully suppress the speech directly.

## Section 4. Public Record

Material state requests to restrict or label political speech shall be recorded where lawful.

---

# XIII. Enforcement Process

## Section 1. Independent Review

Major information-integrity enforcement affecting political speech, election communication, platform access, media ownership, synthetic media, or foreign-linked communication shall be subject to independent review and appeal.

## Section 2. Notice

Affected persons shall receive notice where lawful and practicable.

## Section 3. Public Reasoning

Enforcement decisions shall include public reasoning, subject to lawful privacy and security limits.

## Section 4. Appeal

Affected persons shall have appeal or review pathways.

## Section 5. Emergency Election Review

Election-period information-integrity disputes shall receive expedited review where delay would materially affect electoral fairness or speech rights.

---

# XIV. Enforcement Remedies

## Section 1. Remedy Hierarchy

Remedies should generally proceed from least restrictive to more restrictive.

Remedies may include:

1. disclosure;
2. labeling;
3. correction;
4. public archive entry;
5. funding disclosure;
6. removal of fraudulent impersonation;
7. sanction for unlawful concealment;
8. platform transparency requirement;
9. referral for campaign finance or integrity review;
10. suppression only where narrowly lawful.

## Section 2. Suppression Limit

Suppression of expression shall be limited to narrow unlawful categories and shall be subject to independent review.

## Section 3. Proportionality

Remedies shall be proportionate to harm, intent, reach, timing, and rights impact.

---

# XV. Enforcement Transparency

## Section 1. Public Statistics

Information-integrity enforcement bodies shall publish viewpoint-neutral enforcement statistics.

## Section 2. Required Metrics

Reports may include:

1. number of cases;
2. subject matter categories;
3. remedy types;
4. appeal outcomes;
5. political-ad disclosure cases;
6. synthetic media cases;
7. foreign-linked communication cases;
8. coordinated inauthentic behavior cases;
9. platform-related cases;
10. timing during election periods.

## Section 3. Privacy and Security

Reports may protect privacy, lawful anonymity, security, and investigation integrity.

## Section 4. Bias Review

Enforcement statistics shall be reviewed for viewpoint, party, ideology, region, or group bias.

# XVII. Schedule Review

## Section 1. Periodic Review

This Schedule shall be reviewed periodically to ensure information-integrity rules protect speech while exposing manipulation.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. free-expression protection;
2. viewpoint neutrality;
3. disclosure effectiveness;
4. synthetic media governance;
5. anonymous speaker protection;
6. foreign-linked communication enforcement;
7. platform transparency;
8. political data operation transparency;
9. enforcement bias;
10. public trust;
11. election-period performance;
12. censorship risk.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Schedule shall follow the major statutory architecture or framework amendment process, as applicable.
