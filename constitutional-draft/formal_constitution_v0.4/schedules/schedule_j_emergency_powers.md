## Purpose

This schedule defines emergency activation, duration, renewal, oversight, classified action, emergency spending, emergency technology, security deployment, normalcy restoration, and post-emergency audit rules.

Core rule:

> Emergency power is temporary permission, not a second constitution.

---

# I. Emergency Authority Scope

## Section 1. Covered Emergency Authority

This Schedule applies to:

1. emergency declarations;
2. emergency executive powers;
3. emergency regulations;
4. emergency spending;
5. emergency procurement;
6. emergency technology deployment;
7. emergency surveillance;
8. emergency security deployment;
9. emergency public-health measures;
10. emergency infrastructure measures;
11. emergency fiscal measures;
12. emergency restrictions on movement, assembly, commerce, or public services.

## Section 2. Emergency Definition

Emergency powers may be activated only for severe threats, including:

1. war;
2. invasion;
3. major cyberattack;
4. pandemic;
5. natural disaster;
6. infrastructure collapse;
7. constitutional crisis;
8. systemic financial collapse;
9. comparable threats defined by law.

## Section 3. Comparable Threats

Comparable threats must satisfy:

1. severity;
2. necessity;
3. proportionality;
4. urgency;
5. public authority relevance;
6. reviewability.

## Section 4. Ordinary Problems Not Enough

Political inconvenience, ordinary protest, routine crime, ordinary fiscal disagreement, ordinary administrative failure, or electoral disadvantage shall not by itself justify emergency authority.

---

# II. Emergency Activation

## Section 1. Required Activation Terms

Emergency activation must include:

1. legal basis;
2. defined emergency type;
3. defined scope;
4. named accountable executive;
5. duration limit;
6. rights impact statement;
7. fiscal impact statement where material;
8. public notice unless narrowly classified;
9. legislative notification;
10. judicial review availability;
11. normalcy restoration plan;
12. emergency public accountability record.

## Section 2. Public Notice

Public notice shall explain:

1. what emergency exists;
2. what powers are activated;
3. who is accountable;
4. what rights may be affected;
5. how long the authority lasts;
6. how it may be challenged;
7. what oversight applies;
8. what must happen for normal authority to return.

## Section 3. Emergency Public Accountability Record

Every emergency declaration shall be recorded in the Public Constitutional Interface.

The record shall identify:

1. legal authority;
2. accountable owner;
3. scope;
4. affected systems;
5. active powers;
6. expiration date;
7. renewal status;
8. review bodies;
9. spending authority;
10. technology deployments;
11. security deployments;
12. normalcy restoration benchmarks.

---

# III. Default Duration and Expiration

## Section 1. Initial Duration

Emergency authority shall have an initial limited duration defined by constitutional statute.

Unless otherwise set by constitutional statute, emergency authority should be short enough to require timely legislative review and long enough to permit immediate response.

## Section 2. Automatic Expiration

Emergency powers expire automatically unless lawfully renewed.

## Section 3. No Inertia Extension

Emergency authority shall not continue by administrative inertia, informal practice, or delayed review.

## Section 4. Expiration Notice

The Public Constitutional Interface shall display emergency expiration dates and renewal deadlines.

## Section 5. Continuity Measures

Narrow continuity measures may remain temporarily after expiration only where necessary to preserve rights, public safety, lawful service continuity, or orderly winding down, and only under legal authority and review.

---

# IV. Renewal Rules

## Section 1. Renewal Requirement

Emergency renewal requires:

1. public necessity statement;
2. evidence of continuing emergency;
3. rights review;
4. fiscal review;
5. normalcy restoration progress;
6. legislative approval;
7. judicial review availability;
8. updated expiration date.

## Section 2. Escalating Renewal Thresholds

Emergency renewal thresholds shall increase as emergency authority continues.

Constitutional statute shall define escalation requirements, which may include:

1. higher legislative vote threshold;
2. shorter renewal periods;
3. additional rights review;
4. regional chamber participation;
5. judicial certification of procedural legality;
6. independent resilience review;
7. enhanced public explanation.

## Section 3. Separate Renewal Vote

Emergency renewal votes shall be separately recorded and publicly justified.

Emergency renewal shall not be hidden inside ordinary legislation.

## Section 4. Renewal Limits

Repeated renewal shall require a stronger showing that:

1. emergency conditions remain active;
2. ordinary law is insufficient;
3. rights restrictions remain necessary and proportionate;
4. normalcy restoration is being pursued in good faith.

---

# V. Emergency Review Clock

## Section 1. Purpose

Emergency authority must be reviewable before emergency harm becomes irreversible.

## Section 2. Review Categories

Constitutional statute shall define expedited timelines for:

1. emergency declaration challenges;
2. emergency rights claims;
3. emergency detention claims;
4. emergency surveillance challenges;
5. emergency election measures;
6. emergency technology deployment;
7. emergency procurement challenges;
8. emergency renewal challenges.

## Section 3. Renewal Review

Where practicable, legal and rights review shall occur before emergency renewal.

## Section 4. Fast Rights Review

Emergency actions materially affecting rights shall remain subject to Fast Rights review.

## Section 5. Kernel Review

Emergency actions that threaten the Democratic Kernel shall receive Fast Kernel Review.

---

# VI. Non-Suspendable Principles

## Section 1. Non-Suspendable Core

Emergency powers may not abolish:

1. due process;
2. court access;
3. lawful opposition;
4. peaceful transfer of power;
5. prohibition on permanent emergency rule;
6. protection against secret punishment;
7. public ability to challenge government;
8. civilian control of security forces;
9. the basic rights floor.

## Section 2. Election Protection

Emergency authority may not suspend or delay elections except under strict legal conditions necessary to preserve safety, voting access, and democratic legitimacy.

Any emergency election modification must be:

1. narrow;
2. temporary;
3. rights-protecting;
4. publicly justified;
5. independently reviewed;
6. not beneficial to the officials enacting it until after an intervening election where practicable.

## Section 3. Opposition Protection

Emergency authority may not silence, disable, detain, intimidate, or materially obstruct lawful opposition except through ordinary law and individualized due process.

---

# VII. Emergency Council

## Section 1. Purpose

The Emergency Council may coordinate temporary crisis response.

It does not replace ordinary constitutional government.

## Section 2. Activation

Emergency Council activation requires:

1. lawful emergency declaration;
2. defined emergency type;
3. named accountable executive;
4. defined membership;
5. scope of authority;
6. duration;
7. reporting duty;
8. rights review;
9. fiscal review where spending is implicated;
10. normalcy restoration plan.

## Section 3. Composition

Emergency Council composition shall be defined by constitutional statute and may include:

1. accountable executive officials;
2. relevant emergency-management officials;
3. public health, infrastructure, cyber, security, or fiscal officials where relevant;
4. rights and justice liaison;
5. fiscal oversight liaison;
6. integrity or procurement liaison where spending is implicated;
7. regional or local coordination representatives where necessary.

## Section 4. Stewardship Role

Stewardship representatives may advise, review, audit, or trigger legal review.

They shall not govern in place of elected authority.

## Section 5. Limits

The Emergency Council may not:

1. create permanent authority;
2. suspend elections;
3. govern ordinary policy;
4. bypass courts;
5. extend its own authority;
6. rewrite law;
7. override the rights floor.

---

# VIII. Emergency Spending

## Section 1. Emergency Spending Ledger

Emergency spending shall be tracked through an Emergency Spending Ledger.

## Section 2. Required Fields

Emergency spending records shall include:

1. legal basis;
2. emergency authority;
3. spending category;
4. accountable officer;
5. vendor disclosure where possible;
6. conflict review where possible;
7. emergency procurement justification;
8. expiration date;
9. payment amount;
10. recipient;
11. audit status;
12. post-crisis review status.

## Section 3. Emergency Procurement

Emergency procurement may move faster than ordinary procurement but shall remain accountable.

Emergency procurement records shall identify:

1. urgency justification;
2. vendor;
3. beneficial ownership where available;
4. conflict review;
5. price reasonableness where possible;
6. delivery milestone;
7. audit path;
8. exception from ordinary procurement rules.

## Section 4. Post-Emergency Spending Review

Emergency spending shall receive:

1. audit;
2. fraud review;
3. procurement review;
4. rights impact review where applicable;
5. public report;
6. recovery and normalization plan.

## Section 5. Principle

Crisis may accelerate spending. It may not erase accountability.

---

# IX. Emergency Technology Deployment

## Section 1. Provisional Authorization

Emergency deployment of high-risk technology may occur only through time-limited provisional authorization.

## Section 2. Required Terms

Provisional authorization shall include:

1. legal basis;
2. emergency purpose;
3. accountable owner;
4. risk tier;
5. rights assessment where possible;
6. human review plan;
7. expiration date;
8. post-deployment audit;
9. public disclosure as soon as lawful;
10. rollback or suspension path.

## Section 3. No Black-Box Emergency Exception

Emergency deployment shall not bypass the No Black-Box Public Authority rule for rights-impacting decisions.

## Section 4. Continuation Review

Emergency technology may not continue after the emergency without ordinary Technology System review, rights review, audit, and public accountability.

---

# X. Emergency Surveillance and Security

## Section 1. Surveillance

Emergency surveillance requires:

1. legal authority;
2. necessity;
3. proportionality;
4. minimization;
5. access controls;
6. court supervision where required;
7. audit;
8. expiration;
9. remedy for abuse.

## Section 2. Domestic Deployment

Emergency deployment of military, intelligence, emergency, or extraordinary security forces requires:

1. legal authority;
2. defined scope;
3. responsible command;
4. duration limit;
5. rights safeguards;
6. public reporting where lawful;
7. oversight;
8. review.

## Section 3. Political Neutrality

Emergency security powers may not be used for partisan advantage, voter intimidation, opposition suppression, unlawful protest suppression, or political surveillance.

## Section 4. Election Periods

Emergency security actions during election periods require heightened neutrality, notice to election stewardship where lawful, and expedited review access.

---

# XI. Classified Emergency Actions

## Section 1. Classified Oversight

Classified emergency actions require classified legislative and judicial oversight where public disclosure is lawfully limited.

## Section 2. Oversight Standards

Classified oversight bodies shall have:

1. lawful access to necessary information;
2. members subject to security and conflict rules;
3. opposition participation where compatible with security;
4. judicial review path;
5. sealed public surrogate reporting where possible;
6. whistleblower channels;
7. post-emergency disclosure review.

## Section 3. No Corruption Shield

Classification may not be used to conceal corruption, unlawful surveillance, political abuse, rights violations, or public authority evasion.

## Section 4. Declassification Review

Classified emergency actions shall receive declassification or public-summary review after risk diminishes, where lawful.

---

# XII. Normalcy Restoration

## Section 1. Required Plan

Every emergency declaration shall include a normalcy restoration plan.

## Section 2. Plan Contents

The plan shall identify:

1. conditions for ending emergency authority;
2. steps to return to ordinary law;
3. agencies responsible for restoration;
4. rights restoration measures;
5. fiscal normalization;
6. technology wind-down;
7. procurement closeout;
8. security drawdown;
9. public reporting timeline.

## Section 3. Renewal Condition

Progress toward normalcy restoration shall be required for emergency renewal.

## Section 4. Failure to Restore

Failure to pursue normalcy restoration in good faith may trigger judicial review, legislative review, integrity review, or expiration of emergency powers.

---

# XIII. Public Emergency Dashboard

## Section 1. Dashboard Requirement

The Public Constitutional Interface shall maintain an emergency dashboard.

## Section 2. Dashboard Contents

The dashboard shall show:

1. active emergency declarations;
2. legal authority;
3. accountable executive;
4. scope;
5. active powers;
6. rights affected;
7. spending authority;
8. technology deployments;
9. security deployments where public;
10. expiration date;
11. renewal date;
12. court challenges;
13. normalcy restoration status;
14. post-emergency audit status.

## Section 3. Plain Language

The emergency dashboard shall include plain-language explanations understandable to ordinary persons.

## Section 4. Lawful Limits

Security-sensitive information may be withheld only under lawful, narrow, reviewable limits.

---

# XIV. Post-Emergency Audit

## Section 1. Required Audit

Every emergency shall receive post-emergency audit.

## Section 2. Audit Scope

The audit shall review:

1. legal basis;
2. rights impact;
3. fiscal impact;
4. procurement;
5. surveillance;
6. technology deployment;
7. security-force use;
8. public health or safety outcomes where relevant;
9. normalcy restoration;
10. lessons learned;
11. public accountability records.

## Section 3. Public Report

A public post-emergency report shall be issued, subject to lawful privacy and security limits.

## Section 4. Corrective Action

The audit may recommend corrective action, sanctions, statutory reform, system repair, or future emergency readiness changes.

---

# XV. Emergency Abuse and Anti-Sabotage

## Section 1. Abuse

Emergency power is abused when used to:

1. entrench incumbents;
2. suppress opposition;
3. evade courts;
4. conceal corruption;
5. bypass procurement accountability;
6. normalize extraordinary authority;
7. suppress lawful protest;
8. manipulate elections;
9. expand surveillance without lawful basis;
10. avoid fiscal transparency.

## Section 2. Anti-Sabotage

Emergency rules shall not be used to sabotage constitutional transition, delay lawful elections, obstruct court access, hide public records, or weaken stewardship bodies.

## Section 3. Remedies

Emergency abuse may trigger:

1. court review;
2. legislative investigation;
3. Integrity System referral;
4. public audit;
5. expiration or narrowing of emergency authority;
6. removal process where applicable;
7. criminal referral where lawful.
