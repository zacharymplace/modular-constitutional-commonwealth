## Purpose

This schedule defines the fiscal transparency, scoring, reporting, risk-band, procurement, local distress, and stewardship-funding rules required by the Fiscal Constitution.

Core rule:

> Fiscal governance is truth machinery, not austerity machinery.

---

# I. Fiscal Constitution Scope

## Section 1. Covered Fiscal Authority

This Schedule applies to:

1. budgets;
2. appropriations;
3. revenue measures;
4. taxation;
5. debt;
6. guarantees;
7. pensions;
8. public assets;
9. public liabilities;
10. tax expenditures;
11. procurement;
12. public-private partnerships;
13. emergency spending;
14. infrastructure maintenance;
15. local fiscal distress;
16. intergovernmental transfers;
17. stewardship funding;
18. fiscal scoring;
19. long-term fiscal outlooks;
20. intergenerational fiscal impact.

## Section 2. Fiscal Honesty

Government must disclose the real fiscal substance of public decisions.

Legal form, accounting structure, public-private partnership design, lease form, special-purpose vehicle, guarantee, or tax preference may not be used to hide public obligation or public risk.

# II. Required Fiscal Instruments

## Section 1. Instruments

The Commonwealth shall maintain:

1. Annual Public Budget;
2. Public Balance Sheet;
3. Long-Term Fiscal Outlook;
4. Independent Fiscal Score;
5. Infrastructure Maintenance Ledger;
6. Pension and Benefit Stress Test;
7. Emergency Spending Ledger;
8. Tax Expenditure Ledger;
9. Procurement Ledger;
10. Intergenerational Impact Statement;
11. Local Fiscal Distress Register;
12. Stewardship Funding Dashboard.

## Section 2. Public Access

Fiscal instruments shall be available through the Public Constitutional Interface in plain-language and machine-readable form where appropriate.

## Section 3. Audit

Fiscal instruments shall be subject to audit for completeness, accuracy, timeliness, assumptions, and public usability.

# III. Annual Public Budget

## Section 1. Budget Contents

The Annual Public Budget shall show:

1. projected revenue;
2. proposed spending;
3. current operating costs;
4. capital spending;
5. debt service;
6. emergency reserves;
7. intergovernmental transfers;
8. stewardship institution funding;
9. program changes;
10. tax changes;
11. public employment impact where material;
12. distributional impact;
13. regional impact;
14. maintenance obligations;
15. fiscal risk assumptions.

## Section 2. Plain-Language Budget

The budget shall include a public-facing plain-language explanation.

## Section 3. Budget Comparison

The budget shall compare:

1. prior-year budget;
2. actual prior-year results;
3. proposed changes;
4. long-term effect;
5. cost of action;
6. cost of inaction where material.

---

# IV. Public Balance Sheet

## Section 1. Required Categories

The Public Balance Sheet shall include:

1. public assets;
2. direct debt;
3. pension obligations;
4. healthcare and benefit obligations;
5. lease obligations;
6. public-private partnership obligations;
7. guarantees;
8. contingent liabilities;
9. disaster exposure;
10. insurance exposure;
11. infrastructure maintenance backlog;
12. litigation exposure where material;
13. tax expenditures;
14. emergency liabilities;
15. local fiscal exposure where material;
16. natural resource or public land obligations where material.

## Section 2. Economic Substance Rule

Public obligations shall be disclosed based on real fiscal risk, not legal form.

## Section 3. Contingent Liability Disclosure

Contingent liabilities shall be disclosed where reasonably estimable or materially relevant.

Where precise estimate is not possible, the responsible office shall disclose risk category, exposure range, and assumptions.

# V. Long-Term Fiscal Outlook

## Section 1. Time Horizons

The Commonwealth shall publish long-term fiscal outlooks over at least:

1. 10-year horizon;
2. 20-year horizon;
3. 30-year horizon.

## Section 2. Required Contents

The outlook shall include:

1. projected revenue;
2. projected spending;
3. debt path;
4. debt-service burden;
5. pension and benefit obligations;
6. infrastructure maintenance obligations;
7. demographic assumptions;
8. climate and disaster exposure;
9. healthcare or social cost drivers;
10. revenue volatility;
11. local fiscal distress exposure;
12. intergenerational impact.

## Section 3. Scenario Analysis

The outlook shall include multiple scenarios, including:

1. baseline;
2. optimistic;
3. pessimistic;
4. recession or downturn;
5. high-interest-rate;
6. disaster or climate shock where relevant;
7. demographic stress;
8. emergency spending stress.

# VI. Independent Fiscal Score

## Section 1. Requirement

Major fiscal measures shall receive an Independent Fiscal Score.

## Section 2. Measures Requiring Score

Fiscal scoring is required for:

1. budgets;
2. major spending bills;
3. major tax bills;
4. major debt issuance;
5. pension or benefit reforms;
6. public-private partnerships;
7. major procurement programs;
8. emergency spending packages;
9. major infrastructure commitments;
10. tax expenditures;
11. major regulatory programs with material fiscal effect;
12. major intergovernmental transfers.

## Section 3. Score Contents

A fiscal score shall include:

1. short-term cost;
2. long-term cost;
3. revenue effect;
4. debt effect;
5. distributional impact;
6. regional impact;
7. implementation cost;
8. administrative cost;
9. maintenance burden;
10. cost of inaction where material;
11. risk assumptions;
12. uncertainty range;
13. alternative scenarios.

## Section 4. Assumption Transparency

Fiscal scores shall publish:

1. assumptions;
2. data sources;
3. confidence ranges;
4. sensitivity analysis;
5. alternative scenarios;
6. discount rates where used;
7. risk bands;
8. dissenting technical notes where material;
9. model limitations.

---

# VII. Fiscal Model Governance

## Section 1. Model Review

Fiscal models used for major public scoring shall be subject to:

1. public methodology disclosure;
2. independent technical review;
3. sensitivity testing;
4. comparison against actual outcomes;
5. correction of persistent bias;
6. dissenting technical notes where material;
7. audit of material assumption changes.

## Section 2. Score vs Actual Review

The Independent Fiscal Authority shall periodically compare fiscal scores with actual outcomes.

## Section 3. Persistent Error

Persistent scoring error shall trigger methodological review.

Error alone does not imply misconduct where uncertainty was disclosed and methodology was reasonable.

## Section 4. Political Manipulation

Intentional manipulation of assumptions, scoring, model selection, or omitted risk shall trigger integrity review.

---

# VIII. Fiscal Warning Bands

## Section 1. Risk Bands

The Commonwealth shall use fiscal warning bands.

| Band | Meaning | Required Response |
|---|---|---|
| Green | sustainable | ordinary reporting |
| Yellow | elevated risk | monitoring and public warning |
| Orange | serious risk | corrective plan required |
| Red | structural danger | enhanced legislative action and public review |

## Section 2. Risk Indicators

Fiscal risk bands may consider:

1. debt-service burden;
2. recurring deficit ratio;
3. revenue volatility;
4. pension funding;
5. benefit funding;
6. maintenance backlog;
7. emergency reserve adequacy;
8. contingent liabilities;
9. disaster exposure;
10. local fiscal distress;
11. interest-rate sensitivity;
12. tax expenditure growth;
13. off-balance-sheet exposure.

## Section 3. Public Explanation

Each risk-band assignment shall include plain-language explanation.

## Section 4. Corrective Plan

Orange or Red status shall require a public corrective plan.

The plan may not violate the rights floor or Present Rights Priority.

---

# IX. Cost of Action and Cost of Inaction

## Section 1. Fiscal Balance of Harms

Fiscal review must assess both:

1. the cost of action;
2. the cost of inaction.

## Section 2. Required Analysis

Cost-of-inaction analysis shall be required where failure to act may materially affect:

1. infrastructure;
2. public health;
3. housing;
4. climate exposure;
5. disaster risk;
6. public safety;
7. education;
8. fiscal sustainability;
9. rights protection;
10. public technology failure;
11. local fiscal distress.

## Section 3. Examples

Cost of inaction may include:

1. deferred maintenance;
2. emergency repair;
3. disaster recovery;
4. health system strain;
5. lost productivity;
6. higher future debt;
7. rights litigation;
8. service collapse;
9. security breach;
10. public trust decline.

## Section 4. Anti-Austerity Principle

Fiscal review shall not treat inaction as costless.

---

# X. Infrastructure Maintenance Ledger

## Section 1. Required Ledger

The Commonwealth shall maintain an Infrastructure Maintenance Ledger.

## Section 2. Required Fields

The Ledger shall track:

1. asset inventory;
2. condition rating;
3. safety risk;
4. climate risk;
5. replacement timeline;
6. maintenance backlog;
7. deferred maintenance cost;
8. lifecycle cost;
9. maintenance funding plan;
10. responsible office;
11. geographic location;
12. equity or access impact where material.

## Section 3. New Infrastructure Rule

New infrastructure projects shall include:

1. lifecycle cost;
2. maintenance funding plan;
3. resilience assessment;
4. procurement risk review;
5. public benefit statement;
6. climate or disaster exposure where relevant.

## Section 4. Principle

Do not build what you will not maintain.

---

# XI. Pension and Benefit Stress Test

## Section 1. Requirement

The Commonwealth shall maintain pension and benefit stress tests.

## Section 2. Required Contents

Stress tests shall include:

1. funded status;
2. long-term obligation;
3. contribution requirements;
4. demographic assumptions;
5. investment assumptions where relevant;
6. sensitivity analysis;
7. legal obligations;
8. intergenerational impact;
9. risk-band classification;
10. corrective options where needed.

## Section 3. Transparency

Pension and benefit stress tests shall be public, understandable, and independently reviewed.

# XII. Tax Expenditure Ledger

## Section 1. Requirement

The Commonwealth shall maintain a Tax Expenditure Ledger.

## Section 2. Covered Preferences

The Ledger shall include:

1. tax credits;
2. deductions;
3. exemptions;
4. abatements;
5. deferrals;
6. preferential rates;
7. exclusions;
8. special tax treatments;
9. targeted incentives;
10. equivalent fiscal preferences.

## Section 3. Required Fields

Each entry shall show:

1. beneficiary class;
2. estimated cost;
3. purpose;
4. expiration or review date;
5. distributional impact;
6. regional impact;
7. performance evaluation;
8. lobbying or influence history where material;
9. renewal status;
10. responsible office.

## Section 4. Sunset Rule

No tax expenditure may continue indefinitely without public review.

## Section 5. Renewal Evidence

Renewal requires public review of:

1. purpose;
2. cost;
3. beneficiaries;
4. distributional effect;
5. regional effect;
6. performance evidence;
7. influence history where material;
8. alternatives.

# XIII. Procurement Ledger

## Section 1. Requirement

The Commonwealth shall maintain a Procurement Ledger for major public contracts.

## Section 2. Required Fields

The Procurement Ledger shall include:

1. bid announcement;
2. bidders;
3. award recipient;
4. beneficial ownership;
5. contract amount;
6. contract purpose;
7. contract term;
8. change orders;
9. amendments;
10. emergency exceptions;
11. vendor conflicts;
12. pre-procurement influence where material;
13. performance milestones;
14. payments;
15. audit findings;
16. responsible office.

## Section 3. Pre-Procurement Influence Disclosure

Major procurements shall disclose material pre-bid vendor influence, including:

1. consultants;
2. draft specifications;
3. technical advisors;
4. revolving-door relationships;
5. vendor-funded studies;
6. affiliated subcontractors;
7. emergency sole-source justifications.

## Section 4. High-Risk Procurement

High-risk procurement includes:

1. defense;
2. surveillance;
3. AI systems;
4. emergency contracts;
5. infrastructure megaprojects;
6. healthcare systems;
7. public data systems;
8. sole-source contracts.

High-risk procurement requires elevated Integrity and Technology review.

# XIV. Emergency Spending Ledger

## Section 1. Requirement

Emergency spending shall be tracked through an Emergency Spending Ledger.

## Section 2. Required Fields

Emergency spending records shall include:

1. legal basis;
2. emergency authority;
3. spending category;
4. accountable officer;
5. vendor disclosure where possible;
6. conflict review where possible;
7. emergency procurement justification;
8. expiration date;
9. payment amount;
10. recipient;
11. audit status;
12. post-crisis review status.

## Section 3. Post-Emergency Review

Emergency spending shall receive:

1. audit;
2. fraud review;
3. procurement review;
4. rights impact review where applicable;
5. public report;
6. recovery and normalization plan.

## Section 4. Principle

Crisis may accelerate spending. It may not erase accountability.

---

# XV. Intergenerational Impact Statement

## Section 1. Requirement

Major fiscal decisions shall include an Intergenerational Impact Statement.

## Section 2. Applies To

The Statement applies to:

1. major debt issuance;
2. pension reform;
3. climate infrastructure;
4. tax reforms;
5. entitlement expansions;
6. major defense commitments;
7. public-private partnerships;
8. long-term procurement;
9. emergency borrowing;
10. natural resource policy.

## Section 3. Required Contents

The Statement shall show:

1. who benefits now;
2. who pays later;
3. long-term cost;
4. risk scenarios;
5. alternative options;
6. maintenance obligations;
7. future citizen burden;
8. rights and present-obligation considerations.

## Section 4. Present Rights Priority

Future-generation stewardship may not justify violation of the rights floor or denial of minimum present public obligations.

---

# XVI. Local Fiscal Distress Register

## Section 1. Requirement

The Commonwealth shall maintain a Local Fiscal Distress Register.

## Section 2. Early Warning Indicators

The Register may track:

1. pension stress;
2. declining tax base;
3. infrastructure backlog;
4. climate exposure;
5. insurance retreat;
6. debt-service burden;
7. emergency reserve depletion;
8. cyber vulnerability;
9. repeated audit failures;
10. essential service decline;
11. public safety capacity;
12. school or public health fiscal stress.

## Section 3. Distress Support

Local fiscal distress processes shall be designed to:

1. restore capacity;
2. protect rights;
3. preserve essential services;
4. prevent corruption;
5. support local democracy;
6. provide technical assistance;
7. create restructuring paths where necessary.

## Section 4. Anti-Stigma Rule

Distress designation shall not be used to stigmatize communities, punish residents, or justify unnecessary withdrawal of public support.

## Section 5. Review

Distress designation shall be public, reasoned, reviewable, and paired with support or restructuring path.

---

# XVII. Stewardship Funding

## Section 1. Constitutional Function Funding Test

A budget cut is unconstitutional if it materially prevents a stewardship body from performing required constitutional duties without lawful, non-retaliatory justification.

## Section 2. Covered Bodies

Covered bodies include:

1. courts;
2. election administration;
3. Integrity System;
4. Technology System;
5. Public Audit Office;
6. Independent Fiscal Authority;
7. Civil Service Commission;
8. Public Constitutional Interface;
9. other stewardship bodies.

## Section 3. Funding Dispute Forum

Constitutional statute shall define a forum for Constitutional Function Funding disputes.

The forum shall provide timely review where funding cuts threaten constitutional duties.

## Section 4. Protected Sufficiency

Stewardship bodies require sufficient funding, not unlimited funding.

Funding protection does not exempt stewardship bodies from audit, efficiency review, or performance review.

---

# XVIII. Intergovernmental Transfers

## Section 1. Transfer Transparency

Intergovernmental transfers shall disclose:

1. purpose;
2. formula;
3. amount;
4. recipient;
5. conditions;
6. duration;
7. equity impact;
8. maintenance obligations;
9. audit path;
10. responsible office.

## Section 2. No Hidden Responsibility

Transfers shall not obscure which level of government is responsible for funding, implementation, audit, or rights protection.

## Section 3. Conditional Funding

Funding conditions may not require violation of the rights floor or public accountability duties.

---

# XIX. Anti-Raiding Rules

## Section 1. Protected Funds

Protected funds may include:

1. pension funds;
2. emergency reserves;
3. infrastructure maintenance funds;
4. insurance or risk pools;
5. stewardship institution budgets;
6. trust funds;
7. dedicated local service funds.

## Section 2. Transfer Requirements

Transfers from protected funds require:

1. legal authority;
2. public explanation;
3. independent fiscal score;
4. repayment plan where applicable;
5. enhanced approval for high-risk funds;
6. rights and service impact review.

## Section 3. Principle

Dedicated public funds are promises, not spare change.

---

# XX. Public Fiscal Interface

## Section 1. Required Dashboards

The Public Constitutional Interface shall include:

1. annual budget dashboard;
2. debt dashboard;
3. public balance sheet;
4. tax expenditure ledger;
5. procurement ledger;
6. emergency spending ledger;
7. infrastructure maintenance ledger;
8. stewardship funding dashboard;
9. fiscal risk bands;
10. long-term forecast;
11. local fiscal distress dashboard;
12. plain-language budget explainer.

## Section 2. Readability Standards

Fiscal dashboards shall be designed for public comprehension, not only expert analysis.

## Section 3. Machine Readability

Fiscal data shall be machine-readable where appropriate and consistent with privacy, security, and public usability.

## Section 4. Public Warning

Major fiscal risk changes shall be publicly flagged.
