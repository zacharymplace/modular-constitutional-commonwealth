## Purpose

This schedule defines the staged transition from existing lawful institutions into the Modular Constitutional Commonwealth.

Core rule:

> Sequence the transition by readiness, not ambition.

---

# I. Transition Doctrine

## Section 1. Required Qualities

The transition shall be:

1. lawful;
2. staged;
3. rights-preserving;
4. capacity-gated;
5. auditable;
6. anti-sabotage;
7. publicly legible;
8. legacy-continuous;
9. fiscally transparent;
10. reversible where possible.

## Section 2. Transition Principle

A better system must not arrive by breaking the lawful continuity people depend on.

## Section 3. No Temporary Sovereignty

Transition bodies may coordinate implementation. They may not become a temporary sovereign.

## Section 4. Public Legibility

Every transition phase must be understandable to ordinary persons through public guidance, public dashboards, and non-digital access channels.

---

# II. Minimum Viable Constitutional Package

## Section 1. Required First Foundation

Before full transition, the Commonwealth must secure:

1. Democratic Kernel;
2. Public Accountability Rule;
3. Fast Rights Protection initial pathway;
4. Fiscal Baseline Audit;
5. Independent Election Stewardship;
6. Public Party Status Ledger initial version;
7. Integrity System core;
8. Public Audit Office;
9. Independent Fiscal Authority;
10. Public Algorithm Registry initial version;
11. Public Constitutional Interface initial version;
12. Transition Anti-Sabotage Rule.

## Section 2. Purpose

The minimum package protects rights, elections, accountability, fiscal truth, and public review before complex institutional migration begins.

## Section 3. No Premature Complexity

No advanced module shall be activated before the basic rights, accountability, fiscal, election, and court foundations can function.

---

# III. Transition Phases

## Section 1. Phase 0 — Kernel Ratification

Purpose: lock the non-negotiable constitutional foundation.

Implements:

1. equal citizenship;
2. free and fair elections;
3. due process;
4. independent courts;
5. protected opposition;
6. peaceful transfer of power;
7. civilian control of security forces;
8. no permanent emergency rule;
9. public accountability right;
10. no hidden sovereign rule.

Activation condition:

> Constitutional ratification through legitimate public process.

## Section 2. Phase 1 — Rights, Accountability, and Fiscal Baseline

Purpose: give people remedies and reveal real fiscal conditions.

Implements:

1. Public Accountability Rule;
2. Fast Rights Protection initial pathway;
3. Rights Ombudsman initial function;
4. Public Constitutional Interface v1;
5. public decision records;
6. appeal and audit path requirements;
7. Fiscal Baseline Audit;
8. transition cost estimate;
9. local fiscal risk screen.

Activation condition:

> People must be able to challenge power before major institutional migration begins.

## Section 3. Phase 2 — Election Continuity and Electoral Reform Preparation

Purpose: reform elections without disrupting democratic legitimacy.

Implements:

1. independent election stewardship interim structure;
2. voter education campaign;
3. mock ballot testing;
4. districting and boundary review;
5. party registration audit;
6. coalition signal rules;
7. public explanation of mixed-member proportional voting;
8. election dispute calendar;
9. first-election readiness certification.

Activation condition:

> No new electoral system launches until election administrators, parties, courts, and voters are ready.

## Section 4. Phase 3 — Stewardship Appointments and Interim Oversight

Purpose: build nonpartisan guardians before full system activation.

Creates or transitions:

1. Election Stewardship Commission;
2. Integrity Commission;
3. Technology Stewardship Authority;
4. Public Audit Office;
5. Independent Fiscal Authority;
6. Civil Service Commission;
7. Public Constitutional Interface Authority;
8. other required stewardship bodies.

Activation condition:

> Stewardship bodies receive full powers only after appointment, funding, audit, appeal, and public-reporting systems are operational.

## Section 5. Phase 4 — Court Capacity and Rights Enforcement

Purpose: ensure remedies work before legal complexity increases.

Builds or designates:

1. Constitutional Court;
2. High Court or appellate system;
3. Administrative Courts;
4. Fast Rights Tribunal;
5. Election and Party Court;
6. Technology Review Tribunal.

Required before full activation:

1. judges appointed;
2. clerks trained;
3. one-door filing system;
4. public filing guidance;
5. emergency calendars;
6. triage process;
7. legal aid capacity;
8. appeal paths;
9. court-order enforcement protocol.

Activation condition:

> Courts must be able to process rights, election, administrative, and technology disputes before those systems fully activate.

## Section 6. Phase 5 — First Electoral Transition Cycle

Purpose: run the first election under the new electoral design with maximum stability.

Implements:

1. mixed-member proportional ballot;
2. independent boundary maps;
3. party thresholds;
4. semi-open lists;
5. coalition signal filings;
6. public party status ledger;
7. constructive no-confidence rules;
8. opposition, plurality, and emergent roles;
9. post-election audit.

Activation condition:

> Full party power roles activate after certified results and ledger publication.

## Section 7. Phase 6 — Full Fiscal Constitution

Purpose: make fiscal truth permanent.

Implements:

1. Annual Public Budget dashboard;
2. Public Balance Sheet;
3. Long-Term Fiscal Outlook;
4. Independent Fiscal Score;
5. Infrastructure Maintenance Ledger;
6. Pension and Benefit Stress Test;
7. Tax Expenditure Ledger;
8. Procurement Ledger;
9. Emergency Spending Ledger;
10. Local Fiscal Distress Register;
11. Fiscal Warning Bands.

Activation condition:

> Major new national policy modules cannot scale until fiscal scoring, maintenance burden, cost of action, and cost of inaction are visible.

## Section 8. Phase 7 — Technology Freeze, Register, and Audit

Purpose: prevent hidden algorithmic government during migration.

Implements:

1. high-risk system freeze;
2. legacy algorithm inventory;
3. public algorithm registry;
4. risk classification;
5. accountable human owner assignment;
6. appeal path publication;
7. data quality audit;
8. vendor contract review;
9. rollback plan;
10. public system cards.

Activation condition:

> Register first. Freeze risky drift. Migrate only after audit.

## Section 9. Phase 8 — Resilience System Buildout

Purpose: build long-term memory and crisis capacity.

Implements:

1. National Risk Register;
2. Resilience and Future Generations Commission;
3. climate and infrastructure stress tests;
4. cyber and systemic shock planning;
5. public health readiness;
6. demographic and labor transition analysis;
7. normalcy restoration plans;
8. emergency expiration records.

Activation condition:

> Resilience planning may recommend ordinary law and preparation, but emergency authority requires separate lawful activation.

## Section 10. Phase 9 — Distributed Public Ledger Modules

Purpose: add tamper-evident verification only after accountability and technology governance exist.

Deploys gradually:

1. Public Accountability Ledger;
2. Procurement Integrity Ledger;
3. Legislative Provenance Ledger;
4. Public Algorithm Registry Ledger;
5. Fiscal Flow Ledger;
6. Credential Verification Ledger;
7. Election Audit Ledger.

Activation condition:

> If a signed append-only database solves the problem, do not use blockchain.

## Section 11. Phase 10 — Federal, State, and Local Node Alignment

Purpose: interweave government levels without duplicating bureaucracy or overcentralizing.

Creates state and local nodes for:

1. Democratic System;
2. Rights and Justice System;
3. Integrity System;
4. Technology System;
5. Resilience System.

Activation condition:

> State and local nodes receive duties only when minimum capacity exists.

# IV. Transition Readiness Certification

## Section 1. Certifier

Constitutional statute shall designate a Transition Readiness Certifier or certification process.

The certifier shall be independent, public, reviewable, and barred from rewriting constitutional substance.

## Section 2. Certification Criteria

Readiness certification shall consider:

1. legal completion;
2. rights continuity;
3. fiscal readiness;
4. institutional capacity;
5. local capacity;
6. technology readiness;
7. court capacity;
8. public interface readiness;
9. unresolved disputes;
10. public understanding;
11. service continuity;
12. sabotage or delay risks.

## Section 3. Challenge

Readiness certification may be challenged where it is arbitrary, captured, incomplete, rights-threatening, or unsupported by evidence.

## Section 4. Partial Activation

A phase may partially activate where safe, but full activation requires readiness certification.

---

# V. Capacity Gates

## Section 1. Capacity Requirement

No state, regional, local, or stewardship node receives full constitutional duties until it has minimum operational capacity.

## Section 2. Required Capacity

Minimum operational capacity includes:

1. staffing;
2. funding;
3. public records capacity;
4. appeal path;
5. complaint path;
6. audit path;
7. secure technology;
8. accessibility;
9. non-digital access;
10. continuity planning;
11. public interface access;
12. legal authority;
13. rights-remedy capacity.

## Section 3. Support Nodes

Where capacity is absent, a higher-level support node may temporarily assist.

## Section 4. Support Node Requirements

A support-node arrangement must include:

1. public legal basis;
2. defined scope;
3. capacity-building plan;
4. rights safeguards;
5. local participation;
6. review date;
7. exit criteria;
8. appeal or challenge path.

## Section 5. No Permanent Centralization by Default

Support-node intervention shall not become permanent centralization unless separately authorized through constitutional process.

---

# VI. Public Transition Ledger

## Section 1. Requirement

Every transition step shall be published in a Public Transition Ledger.

## Section 2. Required Fields

The Ledger shall show:

1. phase;
2. responsible office;
3. legal authority;
4. deadline;
5. current status;
6. budget;
7. dependencies;
8. risks;
9. delay explanation if any;
10. appeal or complaint path;
11. public documents;
12. next review date.

## Section 3. Delay Record

Missed deadlines require public explanation, corrective plan, and review for delay, incapacity, or sabotage.

## Section 4. Public Access

The Ledger shall be available through digital and non-digital access channels.

---

# VII. Transition Anti-Sabotage

## Section 1. Covered Conduct

Bad-faith delay, defunding, disabling, withholding records, obstructing migration, hiding public systems, or manipulating constitutionally required transition duties shall be reviewable and correctable.

## Section 2. Examples

Transition sabotage may include:

1. refusing required milestones;
2. starving transition bodies of lawful funding;
3. delaying election reform for partisan advantage;
4. withholding records needed for migration;
5. manipulating appointments;
6. preserving old district maps without lawful basis;
7. using emergencies to suspend transition indefinitely;
8. hiding legacy algorithms;
9. hiding debts, contracts, or public records;
10. obstructing court or rights-system rollout.

## Section 3. Required Response

A finding or credible allegation of transition sabotage may trigger:

1. public explanation;
2. independent review;
3. corrective timeline;
4. Integrity System referral;
5. court review where rights or elections are affected;
6. sanctions where lawful.

---

# VIII. Transition Authority Limitation

## Section 1. Permitted Transition Authority

Transition bodies may:

1. coordinate schedules;
2. publish implementation plans;
3. manage migration;
4. audit readiness;
5. identify risks;
6. manage public reporting;
7. recommend corrections;
8. support institutional migration.

## Section 2. Prohibited Transition Authority

Transition bodies may not:

1. rewrite constitutional substance;
2. suspend rights;
3. alter election outcomes;
4. choose party winners;
5. bypass courts;
6. appoint permanent stewards outside the approved process;
7. extend emergency powers;
8. replace elected authority;
9. create permanent power for themselves.

## Section 3. Review

Transition authority actions shall be reviewable where they materially affect rights, elections, public authority, institutional activation, or constitutional duties.

---

# IX. Legacy Continuity and Handover

## Section 1. Legacy Continuity

Existing lawful institutions continue performing essential functions until successors are operational, funded, audited, and publicly accessible.

## Section 2. Covered Functions

Legacy continuity applies to:

1. courts;
2. election administration;
3. public benefits;
4. policing and emergency services;
5. tax collection;
6. budgeting;
7. public records;
8. procurement;
9. civil service;
10. local government service delivery;
11. public health;
12. infrastructure operations.

## Section 3. Handover Duty

Legacy institutions shall preserve and transfer required:

1. records;
2. data;
3. contracts;
4. budgets;
5. algorithms;
6. pending cases;
7. audit trails;
8. operational knowledge;
9. public interface records;
10. fiscal obligations.

## Section 4. Handover Sabotage

Bad-faith withholding, destruction, concealment, or manipulation of legacy records shall trigger integrity review and legal remedy.

---

# X. Technology Freeze and Register

## Section 1. Requirement

High-risk public algorithms, databases, and vendor systems shall be frozen, registered, risk-classified, audited, and assigned accountable owners before migration or expansion.

## Section 2. Required Steps

Before high-risk systems migrate, expand, or self-update:

1. freeze undisclosed model changes;
2. register existing systems;
3. classify risk tier;
4. identify human accountability owner;
5. publish appeal path;
6. audit data quality;
7. review vendor contracts;
8. create rollback plan;
9. test meaningful human review;
10. publish public system card.

## Section 3. Priority Systems

Priority systems include:

1. benefits;
2. policing;
3. surveillance;
4. taxation enforcement;
5. healthcare access;
6. housing;
7. education placement;
8. immigration or legal status where applicable;
9. election administration;
10. public finance.

## Section 4. Service Continuity

Technology freeze or migration shall include service-continuity plans so benefits, rights, public services, courts, elections, and emergency functions do not fail because of system review.

---

# XI. First Election Stabilization

## Section 1. Purpose

The first election under the new electoral design shall receive additional stabilization safeguards.

## Section 2. Required Safeguards

Before the first election under a new design, the Democratic System shall provide:

1. readiness certification;
2. public voter education;
3. accessible sample ballots;
4. district map challenge period;
5. party compliance audit;
6. election technology audit;
7. observer and audit procedures;
8. emergency election dispute calendar;
9. public certification timeline.

## Section 3. Public Comprehension Review

Before the first electoral transition cycle, the Commonwealth shall conduct public comprehension review assessing whether voters can reasonably understand:

1. ballot structure;
2. party roles;
3. coalition formation;
4. appeal routes;
5. public accountability tools.

## Section 4. Post-Election Review

After the first election, the Democratic System shall publish a post-election review assessing:

1. voter comprehension;
2. ballot usability;
3. proportionality;
4. coalition formation;
5. party role assignment;
6. dispute resolution;
7. access issues;
8. public trust;
9. recommended parameter adjustments.

---

# XII. Fiscal Baseline First

## Section 1. Requirement

A public fiscal baseline must be published before major institutional rollout.

## Section 2. Required Baseline

The baseline shall include:

1. public balance sheet;
2. debt and liabilities;
3. pension and benefit obligations;
4. infrastructure maintenance backlog;
5. existing tax expenditures;
6. local fiscal distress screen;
7. transition cost estimate;
8. stewardship funding needs;
9. court capacity cost;
10. technology migration cost;
11. public interface cost;
12. emergency reserve status.

## Section 3. Purpose

No transition should begin by hiding what the old system costs or what the new one requires.

---

# XIII. Court Capacity Staging

## Section 1. Requirement

Fast rights and specialized tribunals may not fully activate until capacity exists.

## Section 2. Required Capacity

Before activation, the Rights and Justice System shall provide:

1. appointed judges;
2. trained clerks;
3. public filing rules;
4. one-door filing;
5. triage process;
6. emergency calendars;
7. legal aid support;
8. technology review expertise;
9. election dispute timelines;
10. public guidance;
11. appeal path.

## Section 3. Purpose

Do not promise fast justice before building the path to deliver it.

---

# XIV. Civic Translation

## Section 1. Requirement

Every transition phase shall include plain-language guidance.

## Section 2. Required Materials

Each phase shall provide:

1. one-page summary;
2. what changes for you guide;
3. timeline;
4. responsible offices;
5. rights and remedies;
6. appeal paths;
7. help desks;
8. public FAQ;
9. multilingual access where needed;
10. disability-accessible materials;
11. local civic sessions;
12. public dashboard.

## Section 3. Completion Standard

No phase is complete until the public can understand the change.

---

# XV. No Gap Rules

## Section 1. No Rights Gap

Existing rights remedies continue until new remedies work.

## Section 2. No Accountability Gap

Old oversight bodies continue until successor oversight is operational.

## Section 3. No Election Gap

Election administration remains continuous during reform.

## Section 4. No Court Gap

Every case must have a lawful forum during court restructuring.

## Section 5. No Data Gap

Public records cannot migrate without verification, backup, and audit.

## Section 6. No Funding Gap

No constitutional body receives duties without functional funding.

## Section 7. No Public Access Gap

Citizens must always have a way to identify responsible offices and file challenges.

---

# XVI. State and Local Node Alignment

## Section 1. Node Creation

State and local nodes shall be created for:

1. Democratic System;
2. Rights and Justice System;
3. Integrity System;
4. Technology System;
5. Resilience System.

## Section 2. Rollout Sequence

Rollout shall proceed by:

1. defining national floors;
2. auditing state and local capacity;
3. creating state node charters;
4. building local service interfaces;
5. launching intergovernmental compacts;
6. reviewing after the first full cycle.

## Section 3. Capacity Condition

State and local nodes receive duties only when minimum capacity exists.

## Section 4. Support

Where capacity is lacking, technical assistance, fiscal support, training, or temporary support-node assistance may be provided.

## Section 1. Required Review

Each phase shall be reviewed before the next phase fully activates.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. legal completion;
2. rights impact;
3. public understanding;
4. fiscal readiness;
5. institutional capacity;
6. local capacity;
7. technology readiness;
8. court capacity;
9. public interface readiness;
10. unresolved disputes;
11. sabotage or delay risks.

## Section 3. Partial Activation

A phase may partially activate, but full activation requires readiness certification.

## Section 4. Correction

If review finds serious readiness failure, the phase shall be corrected, paused, narrowed, or supported before full activation.
