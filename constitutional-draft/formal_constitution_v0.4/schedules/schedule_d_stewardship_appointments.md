## Purpose

This schedule defines how stewardship institutions are appointed, balanced, reviewed, protected, and removed.

Core rule:

> Stewardship institutions must be independent enough to resist capture, accountable enough to prevent shadow government, and bounded enough not to govern in place of elected institutions.

---

# I. Stewardship Institutions Covered

## Section 1. Covered Bodies

This Schedule applies to leadership appointments for:

1. Election Stewardship Commission;
2. Integrity Commission;
3. Technology Stewardship Authority;
4. Resilience and Future Generations Commission;
5. Public Audit Office;
6. Civil Service Commission;
7. Judicial Appointments Commission;
8. Public Constitutional Interface Authority;
9. other stewardship bodies created by constitutional statute.

## Section 2. Stewardship Function

A stewardship body protects constitutional machinery, public accountability, rights, integrity, public technology, public finance, elections, courts, civil service neutrality, or transition continuity.

## Section 3. Stewardship Limit

Stewardship bodies may protect the rules of public power.

They may not govern in place of elected institutions, choose ordinary policy values, favor a party, or become a shadow government.

---

# II. Appointment Doctrine

## Section 1. No Single Capturing Source

No single party, coalition, branch, office, profession, vendor network, donor network, regulated sector, region, or level of government may control a stewardship institution.

## Section 2. Multi-Source Legitimacy

Appointments shall draw from multiple sources, including where appropriate:

1. People’s Chamber;
2. Regional Chamber;
3. courts or judicial councils;
4. professional qualification panels;
5. citizen review panels;
6. state or regional nodes;
7. civil service bodies;
8. public-interest representatives.

## Section 3. Independence with Accountability

Stewardship officials are not political agents, but they are not untouchable.

They must be:

1. qualified;
2. conflict-disclosed;
3. publicly reviewable;
4. removable for cause;
5. subject to audit;
6. bound by constitutional neutrality;
7. barred from partisan use of office.

---

# III. Standard Appointment Process

## Section 1. Public Vacancy Notice

Every vacancy shall be publicly announced with:

1. office duties;
2. required qualifications;
3. term length;
4. conflict restrictions;
5. cooling-off rules;
6. application and nomination process;
7. public comment process;
8. appointment timeline.

## Section 2. Qualification Screening

A nonpartisan Qualification Panel shall screen candidates for:

1. technical competence;
2. legal and constitutional literacy;
3. ethics history;
4. conflicts of interest;
5. independence risk;
6. public administration capacity;
7. prior misconduct;
8. regulated-entity ties.

## Section 3. Ideological Neutrality

The Qualification Panel may reject unqualified candidates.

It may not reject candidates for lawful ideology, dissent, prior criticism of government, lawful scholarship, lawful advocacy, or unpopular but constitutional views.

## Section 4. Public Qualification File

Before confirmation, each nominee’s public file shall include:

1. qualifications;
2. Independence Risk Statement;
3. relevant conflicts;
4. prior public service;
5. regulated-sector ties;
6. ethics certification;
7. public hearing record where applicable;
8. citizen panel summary where applicable;
9. Qualification Panel finding.

---

# IV. Plural Qualification Rule

## Section 1. Anti-Guild Principle

Stewardship institutions shall not become narrow professional guilds.

## Section 2. Required Balance

Each stewardship body, as a whole, should include a balance of:

1. technical expertise;
2. legal or constitutional expertise;
3. public administration experience;
4. regional representation;
5. public-interest perspective;
6. citizen or service-user perspective;
7. ethics or audit competence.

## Section 3. Expertise Requirement

Expertise is required where the office demands technical judgment.

But no expert class owns stewardship authority.

## Section 4. Public-Interest Representation

Where a stewardship body affects rights, public services, technology, or public accountability, public-interest and service-user perspectives shall be included in the appointment process.

---

# V. Independence Risk Statement

## Section 1. Requirement

Every nominee shall receive a public Independence Risk Statement.

## Section 2. Disclosures

The statement shall disclose material ties to:

1. political parties;
2. campaigns;
3. donors;
4. lobbying firms;
5. regulated industries;
6. public contractors;
7. vendors;
8. unions or trade associations where relevant;
9. ideological advocacy organizations;
10. foreign-linked entities;
11. family or business interests materially affected by the office.

## Section 3. Effect

An Independence Risk Statement does not automatically disqualify a nominee.

It makes independence risk visible, reviewable, and manageable.

## Section 4. Safe Harbor

Lawful public criticism, scholarship, civic advocacy, nonpartisan expertise, and ordinary professional experience shall not by themselves disqualify a nominee.

## Section 5. Recusal and Mitigation

Where a nominee has manageable conflicts, confirmation may require:

1. recusal;
2. divestment;
3. blind trust;
4. firewall;
5. cooling-off period;
6. public monitoring;
7. other lawful mitigation.

---

# VI. Multi-Source Nomination Model

## Section 1. Default Nomination Shares

Unless otherwise provided by constitutional statute, nominations shall be drawn from multiple sources.

A default nomination model may include:

| Source | Share of nomination pool |
|---|---:|
| People’s Chamber cross-bloc process | 25% |
| Regional Chamber cross-bloc process | 25% |
| Judicial or professional qualification body | 25% |
| Citizen review or public service panel | 25% |

## Section 2. No Source Majority

No nomination source may control a majority of the nomination pool.

## Section 3. Cross-Bloc Requirement

Where elected bodies nominate, nominations must include input from:

1. governing bloc;
2. opposition bloc;
3. qualifying plurality blocs;
4. regional representatives where relevant.

## Section 4. Public Nomination Record

Nomination sources, shortlist composition, recusals, and conflicts shall be publicly recorded.

---

# VII. Citizen Review Panels

## Section 1. Function

Citizen review panels test public-facing legitimacy.

They do not replace technical qualification screening.

## Section 2. Panel Scope

Citizen panels may assess:

1. clarity of nominee explanation;
2. public trust concerns;
3. visible conflicts;
4. service-user impact;
5. accessibility commitments;
6. civic accountability priorities.

## Section 3. Selection

Citizen review panel selection shall be fair, transparent, and resistant to capture.

Selection may use sortition, regional balancing, eligibility screening, conflict checks, and compensation where participation is burdensome.

## Section 4. Limits

Citizen panels shall not certify technical competence unless separately qualified and authorized.

## Section 5. Public Report

Citizen panels shall issue a public summary of findings.

The summary is advisory unless constitutional statute provides otherwise.

---

# VIII. Confirmation Process

## Section 1. Enhanced Confirmation

Stewardship nominees require enhanced confirmation.

Enhanced confirmation may include:

1. supermajority approval;
2. cross-bloc approval;
3. Regional Chamber approval;
4. joint chamber approval;
5. public hearing;
6. conflict review.

## Section 2. Default Confirmation Standard

Unless otherwise defined by constitutional statute, confirmation shall require cross-bloc approval sufficient to prevent unilateral control by the Governing Power.

## Section 3. High-Risk Offices

For high-risk stewardship offices, constitutional statute may require support from:

1. Governing Power representatives;
2. Opposition Power representatives;
3. Regional Chamber representatives;
4. qualified Plurality representatives.

## Section 4. Public Vote

Confirmation votes shall be public unless a narrow lawful security exception applies.

---

# IX. Deadlock Resolution Sequence

## Section 1. Purpose

Appointment rules must resist capture without allowing indefinite vacancies.

## Section 2. Default Sequence

If confirmation fails, the default deadlock sequence shall be:

1. standard confirmation vote;
2. public reasons for rejection;
3. reopened shortlist;
4. ranked list from Qualification Panel;
5. temporary acting appointment from prequalified pool;
6. final fallback selection from a highly qualified publicly vetted shortlist by neutral method.

## Section 3. Public Reasons

A rejecting body shall provide reasons tied to:

1. qualifications;
2. conflicts;
3. independence risk;
4. ethics;
5. statutory eligibility;
6. public trust concerns.

## Section 4. Acting Appointment

An acting appointment may be used only to preserve continuity.

Acting officials may not make major structural, enforcement, or appointment decisions except where necessary to preserve continuity, rights, elections, security, or legal duties.

## Section 5. Acting Appointment Limit

An acting stewardship official may serve only for a limited term defined by constitutional statute.

## Section 6. Final Fallback

Final fallback selection shall not allow unilateral control by the Governing Power.

Fallback methods may include selection from a ranked qualified shortlist by neutral lottery or other neutral public method.

---

# X. Composition Balance Rule

## Section 1. Anti-Domination Standard

No stewardship body may be dominated by one political, financial, professional, vendor, donor, sectoral, ideological, or regional bloc.

## Section 2. Review Factors

Composition review may consider:

1. recent party affiliation;
2. donor networks;
3. regulated-sector background;
4. vendor relationships;
5. professional concentration;
6. geographic concentration;
7. prior service under the same governing coalition;
8. shared advocacy infrastructure where materially relevant;
9. family or business networks;
10. conflicts of interest.

## Section 3. Not a Quota Rule

Composition balance is an anti-domination rule, not a rigid quota.

## Section 4. Viewpoint Protection

Composition balance shall not be used to exclude nominees based solely on lawful viewpoint, lawful dissent, prior criticism of government, or unpopular but constitutional views.

## Section 5. Review

Disputes over composition balance shall be reviewable through a neutral process.

---

# XI. Term Design

## Section 1. Term Principles

Stewardship terms shall be:

1. longer than ordinary election cycles;
2. staggered;
3. protected from partisan retaliation;
4. limited enough to prevent institutional ownership.

## Section 2. Default Term Ranges

Unless otherwise defined by constitutional statute:

| Institution | Default term range |
|---|---:|
| Election Stewardship Commission | 8 years, staggered |
| Integrity Commission | 7–9 years, staggered |
| Technology Stewardship Authority | 6–8 years, staggered |
| Resilience and Future Generations Commission | 8 years, staggered |
| Public Audit Office | 8–10 years |
| Civil Service Commission | 7 years, staggered |
| Judicial Appointments Commission | 6–8 years, staggered |
| Public Constitutional Interface Authority | 6 years, staggered |

## Section 3. Renewal

Terms may be nonrenewable or limited-renewal.

Renewal rules shall not create dependency on the appointing authority.

---

# XII. Removal for Cause

## Section 1. Cause Required

Stewardship officials may be removed only for defined cause.

## Section 2. Valid Causes

Valid causes include:

1. corruption;
2. incapacity;
3. serious misconduct;
4. gross negligence;
5. abuse of office;
6. undisclosed material conflict;
7. prohibited partisan activity;
8. persistent failure of statutory duty;
9. obstruction of lawful investigation;
10. repeated unlawful decisions;
11. severe breach of constitutional oath.

## Section 3. Removal Process

Removal requires:

1. public allegation or institutional complaint;
2. preliminary review;
3. written charges;
4. right to respond;
5. independent adjudication;
6. public reasoning;
7. appeal path;
8. final removal only after defined threshold.

## Section 4. Emergency Suspension

Temporary suspension may occur only where continued service creates immediate risk.

Suspension must be:

1. time-limited;
2. publicly reasoned where possible;
3. reviewable;
4. followed by full process.

---

# XIII. Performance Failure Review

## Section 1. Principle

Independence is not immunity from performance.

## Section 2. Triggers

Performance Failure Review may be triggered by:

1. missed required reports;
2. failure to maintain public records;
3. repeated unlawful decisions;
4. persistent audit failures;
5. failure to enforce required standards;
6. unusable public interface performance;
7. repeated rights-remedy delay;
8. failure to disclose conflicts;
9. chronic failure to meet statutory timelines;
10. persistent failure of constitutional duty.

## Section 3. Responses

Responses may include:

1. corrective order;
2. public performance plan;
3. temporary monitoring;
4. budget or staffing review;
5. leadership review;
6. cause-based removal process for severe or persistent failure.

## Section 4. Anti-Retaliation

Performance review may not be used as retaliation for lawful, independent, or politically inconvenient stewardship action.

---

# XIV. Bad-Faith Removal Petition Penalty

## Section 1. Bad-Faith Petition Defined

A bad-faith removal petition includes:

1. knowingly false allegations;
2. repeated harassment petitions;
3. partisan retaliation disguised as ethics enforcement;
4. fabricated evidence;
5. coercive threats tied to official action;
6. removal attempts based only on lawful disagreement.

## Section 2. Consequences

Possible consequences include:

1. public dismissal;
2. censure;
3. fines where lawful;
4. loss of procedural privilege;
5. referral to integrity review;
6. heightened scrutiny for repeat filers.

## Section 3. Protection for Good-Faith Accountability

Good-faith complaints, even if unproven, shall not be treated as bad faith solely because they fail.

---

# XV. Conflict and Cooling-Off Rules

## Section 1. Before Appointment

Nominees must disclose material:

1. assets and financial holdings;
2. recent employers;
3. clients;
4. lobbying activity;
5. party offices;
6. campaign work;
7. major donors or sponsors where relevant;
8. vendor relationships;
9. foreign ties where relevant;
10. family conflicts where material.

## Section 2. During Service

Stewards may not:

1. hold partisan leadership;
2. conduct campaign fundraising;
3. lobby regulated areas;
4. accept gifts from regulated entities;
5. retain conflicted assets without divestment or blind trust where required;
6. use office for party advantage;
7. suppress lawful dissent;
8. conceal material conflicts.

## Section 3. After Service

Cooling-off restrictions may apply to:

1. lobbying;
2. vendor employment;
3. regulated-sector employment;
4. party employment related to former authority;
5. paid strategic advising in matters previously overseen;
6. access brokerage;
7. shadow lobbying.

## Section 4. Duration

Cooling-off duration shall scale with office risk level and shall be defined by constitutional statute.

---

# XVI. Institution-Specific Safeguards

## Section 1. Election Stewardship Commission

Must include competence in:

1. election law;
2. districting;
3. voting rights;
4. election security;
5. public administration;
6. regional election systems.

Restrictions may include no recent party leadership, no current campaign role, no major party finance role, and no undisclosed party vendor ties.

## Section 2. Integrity Commission

Must include competence in:

1. anti-corruption;
2. audit;
3. public finance;
4. procurement;
5. ethics;
6. investigative procedure;
7. due process.

## Section 3. Technology Stewardship Authority

Must include competence in:

1. AI;
2. data governance;
3. cybersecurity;
4. privacy;
5. algorithmic audit;
6. public law;
7. accessibility;
8. procurement.

Vendor-linked nominees require cooling-off, recusal, or strict conflict review.

## Section 4. Resilience and Future Generations Commission

Must include competence in:

1. emergency management;
2. fiscal sustainability;
3. climate risk;
4. infrastructure;
5. public health;
6. cyber and systemic shocks;
7. demographics;
8. intergenerational policy.

The Commission may forecast, review, and recommend. It may not control emergency power directly.

## Section 5. Public Audit Office

Must include competence in:

1. accounting;
2. auditing;
3. public finance;
4. procurement;
5. fiscal modeling;
6. fraud detection.

The Public Audit Office shall have protected access to public financial records.

## Section 6. Civil Service Commission

Must include competence in:

1. public administration;
2. merit systems;
3. labor systems;
4. administrative due process;
5. anti-politicization.

## Section 7. Judicial Appointments Commission

Must include:

1. legal expertise;
2. rights literacy;
3. judicial ethics;
4. regional representation;
5. public-interest representation.

It must not become a closed legal guild.

## Section 8. Public Constitutional Interface Authority

Must include competence in:

1. public administration;
2. civic design;
3. accessibility;
4. data transparency;
5. privacy;
6. records management;
7. usability auditing.

---

# XVII. Public Accountability of Stewardship Bodies

## Section 1. Required Publications

Every stewardship body must publish:

1. appointments;
2. qualifications;
3. Independence Risk Statements;
4. conflicts;
5. votes;
6. decisions;
7. audit results;
8. enforcement statistics;
9. budget usage;
10. performance metrics;
11. complaints;
12. appeal outcomes;
13. recusal records.

## Section 2. Privacy and Security

Publication is subject to lawful privacy and security limits.

Where information is withheld, the body shall provide a public surrogate explanation where possible.

---

# XVIII. Budget Sufficiency

## Section 1. Protected Sufficiency

Stewardship bodies shall receive sufficient funding to perform constitutional duties.

## Section 2. Budget Sufficiency Review

Budget review asks:

1. Is the body funded enough to perform required duties?
2. Are requested increases justified?
3. Are proposed cuts retaliatory?
4. Are expenditures efficient?
5. Are public outcomes measurable?
6. Are staffing levels adequate?
7. Are technology systems secure and usable?

## Section 3. Challenge

A stewardship body may challenge funding cuts that materially impair constitutional duties.

## Section 4. Audit

Protected funding does not exempt stewardship bodies from performance audit.

---

# XIX. Stewardship Finding Classification

## Section 1. Permitted Classifications

Stewardship bodies may classify:

1. legality;
2. compliance;
3. risk;
4. integrity;
5. fiscal exposure;
6. technical safety;
7. rights impact;
8. procedural validity;
9. emergency authority status.

## Section 2. Prohibited Policy Substitution

Stewardship bodies may not declare:

1. which lawful tax policy is morally best;
2. which party platform is preferable;
3. which lawful ideology is acceptable;
4. which ordinary policy outcome voters should choose;
5. which coalition should govern.

## Section 3. Appeal

Major stewardship findings that block, suspend, materially burden, or publicly sanction public authority shall include public reasoning and appeal or review path.

---

# XX. Schedule Review

## Section 1. Periodic Review

This Schedule shall be reviewed periodically to ensure stewardship bodies remain independent, accountable, competent, and bounded.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. appointment fairness;
2. appointment timeliness;
3. independence risk;
4. composition balance;
5. performance;
6. public trust;
7. budget sufficiency;
8. removal process integrity;
9. resistance to capture;
10. resistance to shadow-government behavior.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Schedule shall follow the major statutory architecture or framework amendment process, as applicable.
