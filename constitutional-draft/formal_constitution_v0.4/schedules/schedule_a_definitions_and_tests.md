## Purpose

This schedule defines core terms and legal tests used throughout the Constitution.

Core rule:

> Definitions should make evasion harder without making lawful civic life harder.

---

# I. General Interpretation

## Section 1. Public-Purpose Interpretation

This Constitution shall be interpreted to preserve:

1. democratic self-correction;
2. equal citizenship;
3. the rights floor;
4. public accountability;
5. anti-capture protections;
6. lawful adaptability;
7. usable remedies;
8. peaceful transfer of power.

## Section 2. Substance Over Form

Public authority may not evade constitutional accountability through:

1. private form;
2. contractor status;
3. vendor ownership;
4. informal guidance;
5. automation;
6. delegation;
7. public-private partnership;
8. emergency classification;
9. technical complexity;
10. financial structuring.

The substance and public effect of the power controls over its formal label.

## Section 3. Materiality

A matter is material when it has practical significance for rights, public authority, public funds, public access, public services, elections, enforcement, legal status, public accountability, or democratic competition.

Minor, clerical, or non-substantive matters are not material unless repeated, concealed, or used to evade accountability.

---

# II. Public Authority

## Section 1. Public Authority Defined

Public authority includes any power, decision, process, action, omission, system, or delegated function that materially affects:

1. public rights;
2. obligations;
3. benefits;
4. legal status;
5. public services;
6. public money;
7. public records;
8. elections;
9. enforcement;
10. surveillance;
11. emergency authority;
12. access to government;
13. operation of public infrastructure;
14. administration of public duties.

## Section 2. Covered Actors

Public authority includes authority exercised by:

1. elected officials;
2. public agencies;
3. courts;
4. stewardship bodies;
5. state, regional, and local governments;
6. emergency bodies;
7. security forces;
8. contractors performing public functions;
9. public-private partnerships;
10. automated or algorithmic systems;
11. vendors operating public systems;
12. entities receiving delegated public power;
13. entities administering public funds;
14. entities administering legally required public services.

## Section 3. Public Authority Indicators

An actor or system is more likely to be exercising public authority when it:

1. acts under legal delegation;
2. controls access to a public benefit or public service;
3. administers public funds;
4. performs a legally required public function;
5. controls public records;
6. materially affects enforcement or surveillance;
7. determines or shapes public eligibility;
8. controls access to appeal or remedy;
9. operates essential public infrastructure;
10. performs a function the government would otherwise perform.

---

# III. Material Public Function Test

## Section 1. Test

A person, entity, contractor, vendor, public-private partnership, automated system, or other actor performs a material public function when its conduct materially shapes or determines:

1. access to public rights;
2. public benefits;
3. public services;
4. public obligations;
5. enforcement priority;
6. surveillance exposure;
7. legal status;
8. voting access;
9. public records;
10. public funds;
11. eligibility for public programs;
12. operation of public infrastructure;
13. administration of legally required public duties.

## Section 2. Effect

Where a material public function is performed, the function is subject to:

1. public accountability;
2. rights review;
3. audit;
4. appeal or review pathway;
5. conflict disclosure where relevant;
6. technology disclosure where relevant;
7. lawful correction where error occurs.

## Section 3. No Evasion by Form

An actor may not avoid constitutional duties by arguing that it is private, technical, advisory, automated, contracted, or informal where it performs a material public function.

---

# IV. Material Influence Test

## Section 1. Test

A system, process, recommendation, risk score, ranking, workflow, model, contractor action, or advisory input materially influences public authority when it changes or materially shapes:

1. eligibility;
2. priority;
3. access;
4. legal status;
5. enforcement likelihood;
6. surveillance likelihood;
7. benefit approval;
8. public-service timing;
9. human reviewer behavior;
10. official decision probability;
11. public funding allocation;
12. voting access;
13. emergency response;
14. investigation priority.

## Section 2. Advisory Label Not Controlling

A public authority may not avoid accountability by describing material influence as:

1. advisory;
2. technical;
3. internal;
4. informal;
5. preliminary;
6. experimental;
7. contractor-provided;
8. operational support.

## Section 3. Presumption in Rights-Impacting Contexts

Where a tool or process materially changes the likelihood of a rights-impacting outcome, it is presumed to materially influence public authority unless the responsible office shows otherwise.

---

# V. Hidden Sovereign Test

## Section 1. Hidden Sovereign Defined

A hidden sovereign is any person, institution, party, donor network, vendor, algorithm, emergency body, expert office, foreign actor, contractor, informal structure, or coordinated network that exercises material public authority without lawful basis, public accountability, appeal, audit, or review.

## Section 2. Test

A hidden sovereign exists where:

1. an actor or system has material control or material influence over public authority;
2. the control or influence is not visible to the public or affected persons;
3. the control or influence lacks adequate legal basis, accountability, audit, appeal, or review;
4. the effect materially shapes rights, obligations, benefits, enforcement, public money, public access, elections, emergency authority, or public decision-making.

## Section 3. Required Finding

A hidden sovereign finding must be:

1. evidence-based;
2. publicly reasoned where possible;
3. proportionate;
4. reviewable;
5. specific to the public authority affected;
6. protective of lawful civic activity.

## Section 4. Remedies

Remedies for hidden sovereignty may include:

1. disclosure;
2. audit;
3. recusal;
4. firewall;
5. correction of contract or delegation terms;
6. suspension of affected process;
7. loss of public privilege;
8. referral for integrity review;
9. legal sanction for bad-faith covert control.

Remedies shall be proportionate to the harm and shall not suppress lawful civic activity.

---

# VI. Lawful Advocacy Safe Harbor

## Section 1. Protected Activity

Nothing in this Constitution shall treat the following as hidden sovereignty solely because it seeks to influence public decision-making:

1. lawful advocacy;
2. journalism;
3. scholarship;
4. protest;
5. organizing;
6. public-interest litigation;
7. expert testimony;
8. lobbying disclosed under law;
9. civic association;
10. ordinary political persuasion;
11. lawful campaign activity;
12. peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 2. Limits of Safe Harbor

The safe harbor does not protect:

1. bribery;
2. coercion;
3. fraud;
4. covert foreign control;
5. shell-party manipulation;
6. undisclosed paid influence where disclosure is legally required;
7. unlawful threats;
8. concealed conflicts;
9. material control over public authority without accountability;
10. procurement manipulation;
11. secret public-law drafting control;
12. evasion of party finance or campaign finance rules.

---

# VII. Democratic Kernel Tests

## Section 1. Kernel Effectiveness Test

Formal preservation of a Kernel institution or right is insufficient if public power materially disables its practical function.

A Kernel impairment exists where public authority materially prevents or degrades:

1. meaningful electoral competition;
2. peaceful transfer of power;
3. access to independent courts;
4. opposition capacity;
5. equal citizenship;
6. due process;
7. civil liberties;
8. civilian control of coercive forces;
9. lawful review of emergency power;
10. public ability to challenge government.

## Section 2. Material Impairment Standard

A Kernel impairment must be substantial, practical, and connected to democratic self-correction or the rights floor.

In determining material impairment, reviewing authorities shall consider:

1. actual practical effect, not merely formal institutional existence;
2. cumulative effect of multiple actions;
3. intent where evidence exists;
4. whether the public retains meaningful correction mechanisms;
5. whether affected persons retain timely access to review;
6. whether the action favors incumbents, one party, or one faction in future competition;
7. whether the impairment is temporary, necessary, proportionate, and reviewable.

## Section 3. Ordinary Reform Safe Harbor

Good-faith institutional reform is permitted when it preserves meaningful democratic function, rights protection, judicial independence, electoral fairness, opposition capacity, and peaceful transfer.

Reform shall not be deemed Kernel impairment merely because it changes institutional design, unless it materially disables democratic self-correction or the rights floor.

# VIII. Rights Limitation Tests

## Section 1. Lawful Rights-Limitation Test

Rights may be limited only by law and only where the public authority proves the limitation is:

1. publicly authorized;
2. necessary;
3. proportionate;
4. non-discriminatory;
5. time-bounded where appropriate;
6. reviewable;
7. consistent with the Democratic Kernel.

## Section 2. Burden of Proof

The burden of justification rests on the public authority imposing the limitation.

## Section 3. Heightened Review for High-Risk Rights

Limitations on voting, candidacy, due process, detention, speech, press, peaceful assembly, political opposition, surveillance, court access, emergency review, or other Kernel-connected rights require heightened review.

Heightened review requires a public authority to show:

1. compelling or weighty public purpose;
2. lawful basis;
3. narrow tailoring;
4. lack of adequate less-restrictive alternative where applicable;
5. meaningful review;
6. expiration or periodic renewal where continuing.

---

# IX. Fast Rights and Fast Kernel Review

## Section 1. Fast Rights Standard

Fast rights relief requires:

1. plausible serious rights claim;
2. imminent or difficult-to-repair harm;
3. connection to public authority;
4. inadequate ordinary remedy;
5. proportional requested relief.

## Section 2. Narrow Relief

Relief shall be narrow where possible and broad only where necessary.

## Section 3. Fast Kernel Review

Fast Kernel Review shall be available where public authority threatens election integrity, transfer of power, court access, opposition rights, civil liberties, emergency review, or other core self-correction functions.

## Section 4. Anti-Abuse

Fast review may not be used in bad faith to paralyze ordinary administration, delay elections, harass public officials, or block lawful policy disagreement without serious rights or Kernel harm.

---

# X. High-Impact and Major Law Tests

## Section 1. High-Impact Legislation

High-impact legislation includes measures with material:

1. rights impact;
2. fiscal scale;
3. emergency authority;
4. constitutional structure;
5. technology risk;
6. environmental or infrastructure impact;
7. intergenerational burden;
8. public accountability effect;
9. democratic competition effect.

## Section 2. Major Law

A major law includes any measure with material fiscal scale, rights impact, emergency relevance, technology risk, infrastructure impact, climate impact, intergenerational burden, or constitutional-system effect.

## Section 3. Review Intensity

Review intensity shall scale with:

1. rights impact;
2. fiscal scale;
3. irreversibility;
4. emergency authority;
5. technology risk;
6. intergenerational burden;
7. infrastructure or climate exposure;
8. effect on democratic power.

---

# XI. Public Accountability Defect Tests

## Section 1. Defect Categories

Public accountability defects shall be classified as:

1. clerical defect;
2. material omission;
3. rights-impacting defect;
4. bad-faith concealment.

## Section 2. Clerical Defect

A clerical defect is a minor error that does not impair public understanding, audit, appeal, or rights protection.

## Section 3. Material Omission

A material omission is a missing or incomplete record that impairs public understanding, audit, appeal, conflict review, public oversight, or remedy.

## Section 4. Rights-Impacting Defect

A rights-impacting defect affects rights, benefits, enforcement, legal status, surveillance, emergency power, voting access, or other material interests.

## Section 5. Bad-Faith Concealment

Bad-faith concealment is intentional or reckless failure to disclose required public accountability information.

## Section 6. Effect

The consequence for a defect shall be proportionate to:

1. the category of defect;
2. intent;
3. harm;
4. urgency;
5. whether correction was attempted;
6. whether democratic competition or rights were affected.

---

# XII. Amendment Classification Tests

## Section 1. Classification

Proposed changes shall be classified as:

1. ordinary policy;
2. major statutory architecture;
3. election and power rule;
4. framework amendment;
5. Kernel alteration.

## Section 2. Major Statutory Architecture

Major statutory architecture includes changes to:

1. election administration;
2. courts;
3. stewardship systems;
4. emergency powers;
5. fiscal constitution;
6. technology governance;
7. civil service;
8. party finance;
9. public accountability infrastructure.

## Section 3. Election and Power Rules

Election and power rules include changes to:

1. election rules;
2. party rules;
3. districting;
4. office powers;
5. court structure;
6. campaign finance;
7. emergency authority;
8. public funding rules;
9. stewardship appointment rules;
10. transfer-of-power rules.

## Section 4. Kernel Alteration

A Kernel alteration is any change that removes, suspends, hollows out, or materially impairs the Democratic Kernel.

# XIII. Anti-Weaponization Principle

## Section 1. Principle

Safeguards designed to protect democracy may not be used in bad faith to disable lawful opposition, suppress dissent, obstruct ordinary administration, entrench incumbents, harass public servants, or evade accountability.

## Section 2. Applies To

This principle applies to:

1. integrity complaints;
2. public accountability defects;
3. fast rights petitions;
4. Kernel impairment claims;
5. common-control allegations;
6. capacity-gate findings;
7. information-integrity enforcement;
8. Public Trust License processes;
9. transition readiness certifications;
10. emergency powers;
11. stewardship findings.

## Section 3. Remedy

Bad-faith weaponization may result in dismissal, cure order, public finding, loss of procedural privilege, sanction, or integrity review, consistent with due process.

---

# XIV. Public Routing Principle

## Section 1. Principle

The public shall not be required to know the internal structure of government to access review.

Government must route good-faith claims, complaints, appeals, records requests, and rights petitions to the correct forum.

## Section 2. Anti-Circular-Transfer Rule

A matter may not be transferred indefinitely between offices or systems.

Where routing is disputed, a provisional accountable office shall be identified until final assignment.

---

# XV. Risk-Tiered Process Rule

## Section 1. Principle

Process burden shall scale with the risk, reversibility, rights impact, fiscal significance, and democratic-power impact of the action.

## Section 2. Tiers

| Tier | Action Type | Process Level |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low-risk administration | Light logging and ordinary appeal |
| 2 | Material public authority | Owner, legal basis, audit, appeal |
| 3 | Rights-impacting action | Heightened notice, review, remedy |
| 4 | Democratic machinery, emergency, constitutional power | Enhanced process |
| 5 | Kernel-impacting action | Strictest process and expedited review |

---

# XVI. Interpretation Against Evasion

Where two interpretations are plausible, reviewing authorities should prefer the interpretation that best preserves:

1. democratic self-correction;
2. rights protection;
3. public accountability;
4. anti-capture safeguards;
5. lawful adaptability;
6. public usability;
7. peaceful transfer of power.

This principle shall not be used to suppress lawful dissent, ordinary political competition, or good-faith institutional reform.

---

# XVII. Schedule Review

## Section 1. Periodic Review

This Schedule shall be reviewed periodically to ensure its definitions remain clear, public, usable, and resistant to evasion.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. definition clarity;
2. cross-schedule consistency;
3. anti-evasion effectiveness;
4. overbreadth risk;
5. rights protection;
6. public usability;
7. anti-weaponization performance;
8. court interpretability.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Schedule shall follow the major statutory architecture or framework amendment process, as applicable.
