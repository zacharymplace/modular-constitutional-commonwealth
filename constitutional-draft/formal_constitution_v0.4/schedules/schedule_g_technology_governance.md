## Purpose

This schedule defines the rules for public AI, algorithms, automated decision systems, data systems, surveillance technology, public digital infrastructure, cyber systems, and technology procurement.

Core rule:

> Technology may help government learn and heal. It may not govern.

---

# I. Technology Governance Scope

## Section 1. Covered Systems

This Schedule applies to public use of:

1. artificial intelligence;
2. algorithms;
3. automated decision systems;
4. risk scoring systems;
5. ranking or prioritization systems;
6. predictive systems;
7. public data systems;
8. surveillance systems;
9. biometric systems;
10. cyber systems;
11. public digital infrastructure;
12. public technology procurement;
13. vendor systems used for public authority;
14. self-learning or self-updating systems.

## Section 2. Covered Uses

This Schedule applies where technology materially affects:

1. rights;
2. benefits;
3. legal status;
4. enforcement;
5. surveillance;
6. voting access;
7. public service priority;
8. public housing;
9. healthcare access;
10. education placement;
11. taxation enforcement;
12. licensing;
13. public employment;
14. emergency response;
15. public funding;
16. access to remedy.

## Section 3. Public Authority

A technology system used by a contractor, vendor, public-private partnership, or delegated entity is covered when it performs or materially influences a public function.

---

# II. Technology Sovereignty Limits

## Section 1. No Algorithmic Sovereignty

No technology system may exercise sovereign public authority without legal basis, public accountability, audit, appeal, and human responsibility.

## Section 2. Prohibited Automated Actions

No algorithm or automated system may independently:

1. change voting eligibility;
2. certify elections;
3. remove officials;
4. suspend rights;
5. issue criminal punishment;
6. authorize surveillance without legal process;
7. rewrite law;
8. extend emergency powers;
9. secretly deny benefits;
10. determine legal status without review;
11. disqualify candidates;
12. override courts;
13. alter constitutional authority.

## Section 3. Human Responsibility

Every rights-impacting or high-risk public technology system must have a named human accountability owner.

The owner is responsible for legal authority, auditability, appeal access, records, review, correction, and public explanation.

## Section 4. No Decorative Accountability

Human responsibility is invalid if the human owner lacks authority, information, training, time, or legal power to change the outcome.

---

# III. Material Influence Test

## Section 1. Material Influence

A technology system materially influences public authority when it changes or materially shapes:

1. eligibility;
2. ranking;
3. priority;
4. risk score;
5. enforcement likelihood;
6. surveillance likelihood;
7. benefit approval;
8. service timing;
9. legal status;
10. voting access;
11. investigation priority;
12. public funding allocation;
13. human reviewer behavior;
14. likelihood of official approval.

## Section 2. Advisory Label Not Controlling

A public authority may not avoid this Schedule by calling a system advisory, internal, preliminary, experimental, technical, vendor-provided, or workflow support.

## Section 3. Presumption in Rights-Impacting Contexts

If a technology system changes the likelihood of a rights-impacting outcome, it is presumed to materially influence the decision unless the responsible public authority proves otherwise.

---

# IV. Risk Classification

## Section 1. Risk Tiers

Public technology systems shall be classified by risk.

Risk tiers shall include at minimum:

| Tier | Description |
|---|---|
| Low | administrative support with minimal public impact |
| Moderate | affects public service delivery or operational priority |
| High | materially affects rights, benefits, enforcement, surveillance, legal status, or public access |
| Critical | affects elections, courts, emergency powers, public safety, constitutional rights, or large-scale public infrastructure |

## Section 2. Risk Factors

Risk classification shall consider:

1. rights impact;
2. affected population;
3. scale;
4. reversibility;
5. surveillance risk;
6. legal-status impact;
7. enforcement impact;
8. bias or discrimination risk;
9. opacity;
10. vendor dependency;
11. security risk;
12. data sensitivity;
13. model autonomy;
14. self-learning capability;
15. emergency use.

## Section 3. Reclassification

A system must be reclassified when its use, data, model behavior, deployment context, affected population, or legal effect materially changes.

---

# V. Public Algorithm Registry

## Section 1. Registry Requirement

The Commonwealth shall maintain a public algorithm and technology registry.

## Section 2. Required Registry Fields

The registry shall include, subject to lawful privacy and security limits:

1. system name;
2. responsible agency;
3. named human owner;
4. vendor or contractor where applicable;
5. legal authority;
6. purpose;
7. risk tier;
8. affected population;
9. deployment date;
10. review date;
11. system status;
12. audit status;
13. appeal path;
14. human review process;
15. system-card link;
16. model version or system version;
17. known limitations;
18. suspension or rollback history.

## Section 3. Non-Public Details

Sensitive details may be withheld only where legally justified.

Withholding shall not prevent oversight, audit, court review, or affected-person appeal.

## Section 4. Registry Defect

Failure to register a covered high-risk or critical system is a rights-impacting public accountability defect.

---

# VI. System Cards

## Section 1. System Card Requirement

Every high-risk or critical public technology system shall publish a system card.

## Section 2. Minimum System Card Fields

A system card shall include:

1. system name;
2. purpose;
3. legal authority;
4. accountable human owner;
5. agency owner;
6. vendor or contractor where applicable;
7. risk tier;
8. affected population;
9. data sources;
10. system or model type;
11. decision role;
12. human review process;
13. appeal path;
14. audit status;
15. performance measures;
16. known limitations;
17. known risks;
18. bias or rights review;
19. security review;
20. rollback procedure;
21. deployment date;
22. review or expiration date.

## Section 3. Plain-Language Summary

Each system card shall include a plain-language summary understandable to ordinary users.

## Section 4. Boilerplate Prohibited

A vague, generic, or boilerplate system card does not satisfy this Schedule.

---

# VII. No Black-Box Public Authority

## Section 1. Rule

No unauditable system may make or materially shape rights-impacting public decisions.

## Section 2. Vendor Secrecy Not a Defense

Trade secrecy, vendor control, proprietary software, technical complexity, or contract terms shall not defeat public audit, court review, rights protection, or meaningful human review.

## Section 3. Minimum Audit Access

For covered high-risk systems, the Commonwealth must have sufficient access to:

1. system documentation;
2. model behavior information;
3. data provenance;
4. input and output records;
5. performance testing;
6. bias testing;
7. security testing;
8. update history;
9. failure logs;
10. vendor change records.

## Section 4. Limited Public Disclosure

Where full public disclosure would create privacy, security, or legitimate trade-secret risk, independent auditors, courts, and oversight bodies must still receive sufficient access.

---

# VIII. Meaningful Human Review

## Section 1. Required Review

Meaningful human review is required when technology materially affects:

1. public benefits;
2. legal status;
3. liberty;
4. criminal justice;
5. policing;
6. taxation enforcement;
7. public housing;
8. education placement;
9. healthcare access;
10. surveillance targeting;
11. licensing;
12. public employment;
13. voting access;
14. rights-impacting public decisions.

## Section 2. Review Standards

Human review must be:

1. staffed;
2. trained;
3. authorized;
4. timely;
5. documented;
6. accessible;
7. auditable;
8. capable of reversing or modifying the automated outcome.

## Section 3. Review Theater Prohibited

A human review process is invalid if the reviewer merely rubber-stamps the automated outcome or lacks authority, evidence, time, discretion, or training.

## Section 4. Review Capacity

Agencies shall maintain sufficient human review capacity to prevent overload, delay, and decorative review.

---

# IX. Algorithmic Review Metrics

## Section 1. Required Metrics

Rights-impacting systems shall publish or report, subject to lawful privacy and security limits:

1. appeal rate;
2. reversal or modification rate;
3. override rate;
4. error rate where measurable;
5. disparity indicators;
6. average review time;
7. unresolved review backlog;
8. model drift events;
9. rollback events;
10. human-review staffing sufficiency;
11. complaint volume;
12. audit findings.

## Section 2. Privacy and Aggregation

Metrics shall be reported in a manner that protects privacy, prevents reidentification, and avoids exposing vulnerable groups.

## Section 3. Gaming Prevention

Metrics shall be audited to detect suppression of appeals, undercounting of errors, narrow definitions of reversal, or manipulation of review statistics.

---

# X. Model Versioning and Drift

## Section 1. Versioning

Covered systems shall maintain version records for:

1. model versions;
2. rule sets;
3. data sources;
4. training data updates where applicable;
5. deployment context;
6. risk tier;
7. vendor updates;
8. audit status;
9. rollback points.

## Section 2. Drift Monitoring

Covered systems shall monitor for drift, including:

1. performance drift;
2. bias drift;
3. data drift;
4. outcome drift;
5. rights-impact drift;
6. security drift;
7. scope drift.

## Section 3. Material Drift

Material drift requires review, documentation, and possible suspension, rollback, or reauthorization.

## Section 4. Unauthorized Drift

Unauthorized material drift in a high-risk or critical system shall trigger review and possible suspension.

---

# XI. Self-Learning Approval Gates

## Section 1. Approval Required

A public system that learns, updates, or changes model behavior after deployment must have approval gates for:

1. rights-impacting updates;
2. material model changes;
3. changed data sources;
4. changed deployment contexts;
5. changed risk classifications;
6. emergency deployment;
7. rollback after harmful drift.

## Section 2. Prohibited Self-Amendment

Self-learning systems may not self-amend law, rights, elections, punishment, eligibility, emergency authority, surveillance authority, or constitutional authority.

## Section 3. Self-Healing Limits

Self-healing systems may repair reversible operational failures.

They may not alter legal entitlements, enforcement rules, appeal rights, or public authority without lawful review.

---

# XII. Technology Procurement

## Section 1. Procurement Rule

No public contract for a rights-impacting technology system shall be valid unless the Commonwealth has sufficient:

1. audit access;
2. security review;
3. documentation;
4. appeal support;
5. exit rights;
6. portability;
7. model or system evaluation rights;
8. incident reporting;
9. update review;
10. rights-impact assessment.

## Section 2. Vendor Requirements

Vendors must disclose:

1. system purpose;
2. data requirements;
3. known limitations;
4. known risks;
5. audit constraints;
6. subcontractors;
7. ownership or control conflicts;
8. prior material failures where relevant;
9. model update practices;
10. security practices.

## Section 3. No Lock-In

Public technology procurement shall include exit and portability requirements.

Vendor lock-in shall not be permitted for constitutional records, rights-impacting systems, election systems, emergency systems, or critical public infrastructure unless independently justified and reviewable.

## Section 4. Pre-Procurement Review

High-risk technology procurement shall receive pre-procurement review by the Technology System and Integrity System.

Rights-impacting systems shall also receive Rights and Justice review where appropriate.

---

# XIII. Emergency Technology Deployment

## Section 1. Provisional Authorization

Emergency deployment of high-risk technology may occur only through time-limited provisional authorization.

## Section 2. Required Terms

Provisional emergency authorization shall include:

1. legal basis;
2. emergency purpose;
3. accountable owner;
4. risk tier;
5. rights assessment where possible;
6. human review plan;
7. expiration date;
8. post-deployment audit;
9. public disclosure as soon as lawful;
10. rollback or suspension path.

## Section 3. No Black-Box Emergency Exception

Emergency deployment shall not bypass the No Black-Box Public Authority rule for rights-impacting decisions.

## Section 4. Post-Deployment Review

Emergency technology deployments shall be reviewed after use for:

1. legality;
2. rights impact;
3. performance;
4. bias or disparity;
5. privacy;
6. security;
7. necessity;
8. public accountability;
9. continuation or termination.

---

# XIV. Surveillance and Biometric Systems

## Section 1. Heightened Review

Surveillance and biometric systems require heightened review when used for enforcement, public access, identification, tracking, risk scoring, or emergency response.

## Section 2. Required Safeguards

Such systems require:

1. legal authority;
2. necessity finding;
3. proportionality review;
4. minimization;
5. retention limits;
6. access controls;
7. audit logs;
8. rights review;
9. appeal or challenge path where applicable;
10. public reporting where lawful.

## Section 3. Prohibited Uses

Surveillance or biometric systems may not be used for partisan targeting, unlawful protest suppression, unlawful political monitoring, or discrimination.

## Section 4. Security Exception

Security-sensitive details may be withheld only under lawful, narrow, reviewable limits.

---

# XV. Data Governance

## Section 1. Data Principles

Public data systems shall follow:

1. legality;
2. purpose limitation;
3. data minimization;
4. accuracy;
5. correction;
6. security;
7. access control;
8. retention limits;
9. auditability;
10. rights protection.

## Section 2. Sensitive Data

Sensitive data includes:

1. health data;
2. biometric data;
3. precise location data;
4. legal-status data;
5. benefit data;
6. financial data;
7. protected identity data;
8. minor data;
9. surveillance data;
10. political activity data where protected by law.

## Section 3. Data Quality

A rights-impacting system may not rely on data known to be materially inaccurate, incomplete, biased, stale, or unlawfully obtained.

## Section 4. Correction

Affected persons shall have correction pathways where inaccurate data materially affects public authority.

---

# XVI. Cybersecurity and Resilience

## Section 1. Cybersecurity Duty

Public technology systems shall maintain cybersecurity protections proportionate to risk.

## Section 2. Critical Systems

Critical systems include technology affecting:

1. elections;
2. courts;
3. emergency powers;
4. public benefits;
5. healthcare access;
6. public safety;
7. legal status;
8. public finance;
9. public records;
10. critical infrastructure.

## Section 3. Incident Reporting

Material cybersecurity incidents shall be reported to appropriate oversight bodies and publicly disclosed where lawful and safe.

## Section 4. Continuity

Critical systems shall maintain continuity, backup, and recovery plans.

---

# XVII. Public Appeals and Remedies

## Section 1. Appeal Right

Affected persons shall have appeal or challenge paths where technology materially affects rights, benefits, legal status, enforcement, surveillance, or public access.

## Section 2. Remedies

Remedies may include:

1. explanation;
2. human review;
3. correction;
4. reversal;
5. pause;
6. rollback;
7. system audit;
8. individual relief;
9. class relief where appropriate;
10. suspension of unlawful systems.

## Section 3. Notice

Affected persons shall receive notice of technology involvement where material and lawful.

## Section 4. No Secret Denial

No public benefit, right, legal status, or remedy may be secretly denied by automated system without notice and review.

---

# XVIII. Public Reporting

## Section 1. Annual Technology Report

The Technology System shall publish an annual public technology report.

## Section 2. Report Contents

The report shall include:

1. registered systems;
2. high-risk systems;
3. critical systems;
4. audits completed;
5. unresolved audit findings;
6. appeals and outcomes;
7. emergency deployments;
8. drift events;
9. rollbacks;
10. procurement risks;
11. vendor dependencies;
12. cybersecurity incidents where reportable;
13. public interface performance.

## Section 3. Plain-Language Summary

The report shall include a plain-language summary.

# XX. Schedule Review

## Section 1. Periodic Review

This Schedule shall be reviewed periodically to ensure technology governance remains effective, rights-protecting, adaptable, and resistant to capture.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. public registry completeness;
2. system-card quality;
3. audit access;
4. human review quality;
5. appeal outcomes;
6. drift monitoring;
7. procurement compliance;
8. vendor concentration;
9. cybersecurity;
10. public usability;
11. rights impact;
12. emerging technology risks.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Schedule shall follow the major statutory architecture or framework amendment process, as applicable.
