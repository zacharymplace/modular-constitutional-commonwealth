# Preamble

We establish the Modular Constitutional Commonwealth so that the people may govern themselves through institutions that are democratic, rights-protecting, transparent, accountable, adaptable, and resilient.

The purpose of this Constitution is to preserve the people’s lawful power to correct, reform, restrain, and replace their government.

The Commonwealth’s shortest promise is:

> Make democracy hard to kill.  
> Make policy easy to improve.  
> Make power impossible to hide.

---

# Article I — Foundational Principles

## Section 1. Popular Sovereignty

Sovereignty resides in the people.

All public authority is exercised in trust, under law, and subject to review.

## Section 2. Democratic Self-Correction

No institution, party, official, emergency power, algorithm, donor network, security force, private entity, foreign actor, vendor, contractor, public-private partnership, or expert body may permanently remove the people’s ability to vote, organize, oppose, challenge, reform, and replace public power through lawful means.

## Section 3. Constitutional Layers

The Commonwealth is organized into three layers:

1. the Democratic Kernel;
2. the Constitutional Framework;
3. policy and administrative modules.

The Kernel protects the conditions of self-government.

The Framework organizes public power.

Modules include ordinary policies, programs, budgets, services, technologies, regulations, agencies, and pilots.

## Section 4. No Hidden Sovereign

No public power may operate without visible ownership, legal authority, public accountability, review, and remedy.

No person, office, party, vendor, contractor, donor network, algorithm, emergency body, expert office, or informal structure may exercise material public authority without lawful accountability.

Technical definitions and tests are established in **Schedule A — Definitions and Tests**.

---

# Article II — The Democratic Kernel

## Section 1. Kernel Definition

The Democratic Kernel contains the minimum conditions of legitimate self-government.

It includes:

1. free and fair elections;
2. equal citizenship;
3. civil liberties;
4. due process;
5. independent courts;
6. protected opposition;
7. peaceful transfer of power;
8. civilian control of security forces;
9. prohibition on permanent emergency rule;
10. public ability to challenge government action.

## Section 2. Kernel Protection

The Democratic Kernel may not be removed, suspended, hollowed out, or rendered ineffective by ordinary law, emergency decree, party rule, administrative action, algorithmic system, court restructuring, fiscal starvation, delegated private action, or ordinary constitutional amendment.

## Section 3. Kernel Review

Formal preservation of a Kernel institution is not enough if public power materially disables its practical function.

Alleged Kernel impairment shall have expedited constitutional review.

## Section 4. Refounding

The Democratic Kernel may be altered only through full constitutional refounding, public ratification, rights continuity, and lawful transition.

# Article III — Rights

## Section 1. Equal Citizenship

All citizens are equal under this Constitution and entitled to equal protection of the law.

## Section 2. Political Rights

Citizens have the right to vote, run for office subject to neutral qualifications, form political parties, support candidates, organize peacefully, oppose government, and access fair electoral processes.

## Section 3. Rights of Persons

All persons under Commonwealth authority are entitled to due process, bodily security, basic legal protection, court access, privacy, protection from unlawful detention, protection from unlawful surveillance, and protection from secret punishment.

## Section 4. Civil Liberties

The Commonwealth shall protect speech, press, conscience, religion, peaceful assembly, association, petition, lawful protest, lawful dissent, and peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 5. Due Process

No person may be deprived of liberty, legal status, public entitlement, property, or protected rights without lawful process.

## Section 6. Lawful Limitations

Rights may be limited only by law and only when the limitation is necessary, proportionate, non-discriminatory, reviewable, and consistent with the Democratic Kernel.

The public authority imposing the limitation bears the burden of justification.

## Section 7. Digital Rights

The people and persons under Commonwealth authority have rights against unlawful surveillance, secret automated punishment, and unauthorized use of personal data.

Rights-impacting automated systems must provide notice, explanation, meaningful human review, appeal, auditability, and remedy.

## Section 8. Fast Rights Protection

A fast rights path shall exist where delay would cause serious or irreversible harm.

---

# Article IV — Public Accountability

## Section 1. Public Accountability Right

Every person has the right to know, for any exercise of public authority:

1. who made the decision;
2. what law authorized it;
3. how it may be challenged;
4. who audits it;
5. when it expires, renews, or is reviewed;
6. whether an algorithm was materially involved;
7. whether relevant conflicts were disclosed.

## Section 2. Public Constitutional Interface

The Commonwealth shall maintain a plain-language public interface showing decisions, accountable owners, legal authority, appeal paths, audit paths, algorithms used, conflicts disclosed, party status, coalition agreements, public funding, emergency powers, review dates, and expiration dates.

## Section 3. Usability

Transparency that cannot be used does not satisfy this Constitution.

The public interface shall be searchable, timely, accessible, understandable, auditable, and available through digital and non-digital channels.

Detailed interface standards are established in **Schedule F — Public Accountability and Interface Standards**.

---

# Article V — Federal, State, and Local Authority

## Section 1. Competent Subsidiarity

Public authority shall be exercised at the lowest level capable of protecting rights, delivering results, managing spillover effects, maintaining capacity, and preserving accountability.

## Section 2. National Floor and Local Ceiling

The national government shall set minimum floors for rights, election integrity, anti-corruption, public accountability, public technology safety, emergency limits, fiscal transparency, and civil service neutrality.

State, regional, and local governments may exceed those floors unless uniformity is constitutionally necessary.

## Section 3. Rights Everywhere

Local autonomy may not be used to violate the rights floor.

National authority may not be used as a pretext for unnecessary centralization.

## Section 4. Capacity

No level of government shall receive full constitutional duties without the capacity to perform them.

Capacity standards and support-node rules are established by constitutional schedule and statute.

---

# Article VI — Constitutional Systems

## Section 1. Five Systems

The Commonwealth is organized through five constitutional systems:

1. the Democratic System;
2. the Rights and Justice System;
3. the Integrity System;
4. the Technology System;
5. the Resilience System.

## Section 2. System Duties

Each system shall have public responsibilities, accountable offices, review paths, audit paths, and public-facing jurisdiction maps.

## Section 3. Lead Responsibility

When a matter involves multiple systems, one system shall be designated as lead.

Shared responsibility may not become shared evasion.

Routing rules are established in **Schedule A — Definitions and Tests** and **Schedule E — Court Jurisdiction and Remedy Paths**.

---

# Article VII — Democratic System

## Section 1. Purpose

The Democratic System protects representation, competition, participation, and peaceful transfer of power.

## Section 2. Election Administration

Election administration belongs to nonpartisan Stewardship Power.

It shall be independent, transparent, auditable, rights-compliant, insulated from partisan control, and subject to judicial review.

## Section 3. Electoral Design

The Commonwealth shall use mixed-member proportional representation unless amended through the election and power rules procedure.

The electoral system shall preserve local representation, proportional correction, independent boundary drawing, fair ballot access, public party role assignment, coalition disclosure, and delayed self-dealing protection.

Electoral parameters are established in **Schedule B — Electoral Parameters**.

## Section 4. Citizen Participation

Citizen assemblies, public comment, civic forums, petitions, and referendums may supplement representative government.

They may not replace rights, courts, elections, or lawful institutions.

---

# Article VIII — Party Power Roles

## Section 1. Recognized Roles

The Commonwealth recognizes five public power functions:

1. Governing Power;
2. Opposition Power;
3. Plurality Power;
4. Emergent Power;
5. Stewardship Power.

Stewardship Power is a nonpartisan constitutional function, not a party role.

## Section 2. Governing Power

The Governing Power is the party or coalition with the lawful mandate to form government.

It may govern, propose budgets, appoint executive officials subject to law, and implement policy.

It may not own election machinery, weaken opposition rights, capture stewardship systems, or change power rules for immediate benefit.

## Section 3. Opposition Power

The Opposition Power is the largest non-governing party or coalition.

It receives protected authority to audit, investigate, question, challenge, publish minority reports, participate in emergency review, and access budget and government information.

## Section 4. Plurality Power

Plurality Power belongs to qualifying minority blocs beyond the principal opposition.

It broadens debate and receives defined agenda, committee, review, and participation rights.

## Section 5. Emergent Power

Emergent Power protects new, regional, reformist, or growing parties by providing lawful paths to ballot access, debate access, limited public funding, and progression into Plurality Power.

## Section 6. Party Governance

Party governance rules, common-control standards, coalition disclosure, public party ledgers, and role thresholds are established in **Schedule C — Party Governance**.

---

# Article IX — Legislature

## Section 1. Chambers

The legislature shall contain:

1. the People’s Chamber;
2. the Regional Chamber;
3. a Civic Review Mechanism.

## Section 2. People’s Chamber

The People’s Chamber provides population-based representation and ordinary legislative authority.

## Section 3. Regional Chamber

The Regional Chamber represents state or regional governments and preserves federal balance.

## Section 4. Civic Review

The Civic Review Mechanism may review, delay, request explanation, or trigger public scrutiny for high-impact legislation.

It shall not function as a permanent veto chamber.

## Section 5. Legislative Powers

The legislature may pass laws, approve budgets, authorize taxation, oversee the executive, confirm major appointments, renew emergency powers, investigate public authority, and amend the constitutional framework through prescribed procedures.

## Section 6. Legislative Limits

The legislature may not abolish the Democratic Kernel, cancel elections for ordinary political convenience, eliminate opposition rights, place election machinery under party control, authorize permanent emergency rule, or legalize secret public authority.

---

# Article X — Executive

## Section 1. Structure

The executive shall consist of:

1. Chief Executive or Prime Minister;
2. Governing Council or Cabinet;
3. Civic President or Constitutional Steward;
4. Emergency Council.

## Section 2. Chief Executive

The Chief Executive derives authority from the Governing Power and administers law, proposes budgets, coordinates policy, directs executive agencies, and responds to crises under law.

The Chief Executive may not control election administration, ignore courts, suspend opposition rights, politicize security forces, extend emergency authority unilaterally, or use public technology for partisan advantage.

## Section 3. Civic President

The Civic President is nonpartisan and protects lawful continuity, peaceful transfer of power, and constitutional order.

The office may not govern ordinary policy.

## Section 4. Emergency Council

The Emergency Council may coordinate temporary crisis response under emergency law.

It may not create permanent authority, suspend elections, govern ordinary policy, or bypass courts.

## Section 5. Removal and Confidence

Executive authority may be ended through election, constructive no-confidence, impeachment, incapacity process, resignation, or narrow constitutional finding.

---

# Article XI — Rights and Justice System

## Section 1. Judicial Authority

Judicial authority shall protect this Constitution, the rights floor, due process, and the legality of public power.

## Section 2. Courts

The court system may include:

1. Constitutional Court;
2. High Court or Supreme Appellate Court;
3. Administrative Courts;
4. Fast Rights Tribunal;
5. Election and Party Court;
6. Technology Review Tribunal.

## Section 3. Judicial Role

Courts shall remedy illegality, rights violations, procedural failures, and constitutional defects.

Courts shall not choose ordinary policy where lawful democratic choices remain.

## Section 4. Access

A timely and plausible claim filed in the wrong forum in good faith shall be transferred to the proper forum, not dismissed.

Court jurisdiction, appeal paths, technical expert rules, and remedy standards are established in **Schedule E — Court Jurisdiction and Remedy Paths**.

---

# Article XII — Integrity System

## Section 1. Purpose

The Integrity System prevents corruption, capture, hidden influence, and misuse of public authority.

## Section 2. Functions

It oversees lobbying, ethics, conflicts of interest, procurement, public spending, campaign finance, party finance, whistleblower protection, influence disclosure, and public audits.

## Section 3. Influence and Public Money

Major laws, public contracts, and material public spending shall disclose influence, conflicts, beneficiaries, procurement history, and public money flows as required by law.

## Section 4. Enforcement

Integrity enforcement shall distinguish honest mistakes from corruption.

Punish corruption severely.  
Cure honest mistakes fairly.

Detailed integrity procedures are established in the **Integrity System Charter** and related schedules.

---

# Article XIII — Stewardship Institutions

## Section 1. Purpose

Stewardship institutions protect the machinery of democracy.

They include election stewardship, integrity, technology stewardship, resilience, public audit, civil service, judicial appointments, and the public constitutional interface.

## Section 2. Appointment and Control

No single party, branch, level of government, profession, vendor network, donor network, or sector may control a stewardship institution.

## Section 3. Limits

Stewardship institutions may audit, disclose, review, certify, block unlawful action, trigger judicial or legislative review, and enforce neutral rules.

They may not govern in place of elected institutions.

## Section 4. Appointment Rules

Stewardship appointment mechanics are established in **Schedule D — Stewardship Appointments**.

---

# Article XIV — Technology and Algorithmic Governance

## Section 1. Purpose

The Technology System governs public use of AI, algorithms, data, surveillance systems, automated decision tools, public digital infrastructure, cyber systems, and public technology procurement.

## Section 2. Algorithmic Accountability

No public algorithm may materially affect rights, benefits, liberty, legal status, surveillance, enforcement, public access, voting access, or public service priority without legal basis, public registry, named human owner, audit path, appeal path, rights review, model versioning, and meaningful human review.

## Section 3. No Black-Box Public Authority

No unauditable system may make or materially shape rights-impacting public decisions.

Trade secrecy, vendor control, or technical complexity shall not defeat public audit, court review, or rights protection.

## Section 4. Prohibited Automated Sovereignty

No algorithm may independently change voting eligibility, certify elections, remove officials, suspend rights, issue criminal punishment, authorize surveillance without legal process, rewrite law, extend emergency powers, or secretly deny benefits.

## Section 5. Autonomic Governance

Predictive, self-learning, and self-healing systems may improve forecasting, service delivery, corruption detection, policy simulation, infrastructure monitoring, cyber resilience, and administrative repair.

They may not self-amend law, rights, elections, punishment, eligibility, or constitutional authority.

Technology rules are established in **Schedule G — Technology and Algorithmic Governance**.

---

# Article XV — Distributed Public Ledger System

## Section 1. Purpose

Distributed ledgers may be used as constitutional evidence infrastructure.

They may verify public records. They may not govern the public.

## Section 2. Permitted Uses

Permitted uses include public accountability records, procurement records, legislative provenance, fiscal flows, public algorithm registries, credential verification, and election audit trails.

## Section 3. Prohibited Uses

Distributed ledgers may not replace voting, courts, human review, privacy rights, emergency limits, benefit eligibility, punishment decisions, legal status decisions, or democratic authority.

## Section 4. Legal Truth

The ledger proves record history. It does not determine final legal truth.

Ledger rules are established in **Schedule H — Distributed Public Ledger Rules**.

---

# Article XVI — Resilience and Future Generations

## Section 1. Purpose

The Resilience System protects the Commonwealth against systemic and foreseeable risks.

## Section 2. Functions

The Resilience System maintains risk registers, emergency readiness, fiscal sustainability analysis, infrastructure stress testing, public health readiness, cyber and systemic shock planning, demographic and labor transition analysis, and normalcy restoration planning.

## Section 3. Future Generations

Major laws shall be reviewed for fiscal impact, rights impact, infrastructure impact, climate impact, implementation risk, and intergenerational burden.

Future-generation stewardship shall not justify violation of the rights floor or denial of minimum present public obligations.

---

# Article XVII — Emergency Powers

## Section 1. Activation

Emergency powers may be activated only for severe threats under law.

## Section 2. Required Terms

Emergency activation must include legal basis, defined scope, named accountable executive, duration limit, rights impact statement, public notice where possible, legislative notification, judicial review, and normalcy restoration plan.

## Section 3. Expiration

Emergency powers expire automatically unless renewed.

Renewal requires public justification, evidence of continuing emergency, rights review, fiscal review, legislative approval, and progress toward normalcy restoration.

## Section 4. Non-Suspendable Principles

Emergency powers may not abolish due process, court access, lawful opposition, transfer-of-power rules, prohibition on permanent emergency rule, or protection against secret punishment.

Emergency procedures are established in **Schedule J — Emergency Powers**.

---

# Article XVIII — Fiscal Constitution

## Section 1. Fiscal Honesty

Government must account honestly for revenue, spending, debt, public assets, future obligations, pensions, infrastructure maintenance, emergency liabilities, tax expenditures, procurement, local fiscal distress, and intergenerational burden.

## Section 2. Cost of Action and Inaction

Fiscal review must assess both the cost of action and the cost of inaction.

## Section 3. Economic Substance

Public obligations must be disclosed based on real fiscal risk, not legal form.

## Section 4. Stewardship Funding

A budget cut is unconstitutional if it materially prevents a stewardship body from performing required constitutional duties without lawful, non-retaliatory justification.

## Section 5. Purpose

Fiscal governance is truth machinery, not austerity machinery.

Fiscal parameters are established in **Schedule I — Fiscal Constitution Parameters**.

---

# Article XIX — Public Service and Anti-Capture

## Section 1. Fiduciary Duty

Public office is a fiduciary trust, not private property.

## Section 2. Integrity

Officials may be required to disclose conflicts, assets, gifts, beneficial ownership, affiliated entities, financial interests, and post-service employment risks.

## Section 3. Public Trust License

Certain high offices may require a Public Trust License based on objective, public, non-ideological standards.

The license may not exclude candidates based on ideology alone.

## Section 4. Revolving Door

The Commonwealth may restrict lobbying, access brokerage, paid strategic advising, vendor employment, regulated-sector employment, and shadow lobbying related to former public authority.

Public service rules are established in **Schedule M — Public Service and Anti-Capture**.

---

# Article XX — Information Integrity

## Section 1. Free Expression

The Commonwealth protects speech, disagreement, criticism, satire, parody, dissent, anonymous lawful expression, vulnerable-speaker expression, and peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 2. Transparency

The Commonwealth may require transparency for political ad funding, synthetic media, coordinated inauthentic behavior, foreign control, platform amplification, media ownership, and major political data operations.

## Section 3. Limits

Disclosure is the default remedy.

Suppression shall be limited to narrow unlawful categories and subject to independent review.

The state shall not create a monopoly on truth.

Information integrity rules are established in **Schedule L — Information Integrity**.

---

# Article XXI — Security Neutrality

## Section 1. Civilian Control

Military, police, intelligence, and emergency forces are subordinate to lawful constitutional civilian authority.

## Section 2. Lawful Command

Security forces owe obedience to constitutional authority, not personal, partisan, unlawful, or anti-constitutional orders.

## Section 3. Nonpartisanship

Security forces may not serve a party, interfere in elections, intimidate opposition, suppress lawful protest, conduct unlawful surveillance, or enforce emergency orders beyond legal scope.

## Section 4. Oversight

Security institutions are subject to legislative oversight, judicial review, inspector oversight, whistleblower protection, public reporting where security permits, and strict domestic deployment rules.

Security rules are established in **Schedule K — Security Neutrality**.

---

# Article XXII — Amendment and Refounding

## Section 1. Ordinary Policy

Ordinary policy may be changed through normal legislative procedure.

## Section 2. Major Architecture

Major statutory architecture requires enhanced approval, public review, fiscal analysis, and rights analysis.

## Section 3. Election and Power Rules

Changes to election rules, party rules, districting, office powers, court structure, campaign finance, emergency authority, or public funding rules require enhanced approval, independent review, public impact explanation, and delayed effect after an intervening election.

## Section 4. Framework Amendments

Framework amendments require elevated approval, election delay, public ratification or regional ratification, and rights review.

## Section 5. Kernel Protection

The Democratic Kernel may not be removed through ordinary amendment.

Full constitutional refounding is required to alter the Kernel.

Amendment and refounding procedures are established in **Schedule O — Constitutional Amendment and Refounding**.

---

# Article XXIII — Transition

## Section 1. Transition Doctrine

The transition to this Constitution shall be lawful, staged, rights-preserving, capacity-gated, auditable, anti-sabotage, publicly legible, and legacy-continuous.

## Section 2. No Gaps

During transition there shall be no rights gap, accountability gap, election gap, court gap, data gap, funding gap, or public access gap.

## Section 3. Legacy Continuity

Existing lawful institutions shall continue performing essential functions until successors are operational, funded, audited, and publicly accessible.

## Section 4. Readiness

Transition shall proceed by readiness, not ambition.

Transition procedures are established in **Schedule N — Transition Roadmap**.

# Closing Clause

This Constitution is adopted to ensure that public power remains visible, lawful, challengeable, audited, bounded, and correctable.

Its promise is:

> Make democracy hard to kill.  
> Make policy easy to improve.  
> Make power impossible to hide.
