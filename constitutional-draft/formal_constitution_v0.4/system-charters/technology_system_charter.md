## Purpose

This charter defines how the Technology System governs public AI, algorithms, automated decision systems, data systems, surveillance technology, public digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, distributed ledgers, technology procurement, and emergency technology deployment.

Core rule:

> Technology may help government learn and heal. It may not govern.

---

# I. Technology System Mandate

## Section 1. Function

The Technology System governs:

1. artificial intelligence;
2. algorithms;
3. automated decision systems;
4. public data systems;
5. surveillance technology;
6. biometric systems;
7. cybersecurity;
8. public digital infrastructure;
9. technology procurement;
10. public algorithm registries;
11. system cards;
12. public technology audits;
13. distributed ledger coordination;
14. emergency technology deployment.

## Section 2. Constitutional Position

The Technology System is one of the five constitutional systems of the Commonwealth.

It operates subject to the Democratic Kernel, rights floor, public accountability rules, due process, privacy, security neutrality, and stewardship limits.

## Section 3. Sovereignty Limit

The Technology System may review, audit, register, classify, suspend, rollback, and recommend correction of public technology.

It may not govern ordinary policy, rewrite law, alter rights, certify elections, extend emergency powers, or replace courts and elected institutions.

---

# II. Technology System Institutions

## Section 1. Core Institutions

The Technology System may include:

1. Technology Stewardship Authority;
2. Public Algorithm Registry Office;
3. Public Technology Audit Office;
4. Public Data Governance Office;
5. Cybersecurity and Resilience Unit;
6. Technology Procurement Review Unit;
7. System Card Office;
8. Human Review Standards Office;
9. Distributed Ledger Review Unit;
10. Emergency Technology Review Cell;
11. Local and regional technology nodes.

## Section 2. Independence

Technology institutions shall be independent enough to resist vendor capture and accountable enough not to become technical sovereigns.

## Section 3. Public Interface

Technology records shall be published through the Public Constitutional Interface, subject to lawful privacy and security limits.

---

# III. Covered Systems

## Section 1. Covered Technology

The Technology System covers public use of:

1. AI systems;
2. algorithms;
3. automated eligibility systems;
4. risk scoring systems;
5. ranking or prioritization systems;
6. public data platforms;
7. surveillance systems;
8. biometric systems;
9. cyber systems;
10. public digital infrastructure;
11. vendor systems used for public authority;
12. self-learning systems;
13. public ledger systems;
14. emergency technology systems.

## Section 2. Covered Uses

Coverage applies where technology materially affects rights, benefits, legal status, enforcement, surveillance, voting access, public service priority, public funding, emergency response, or public remedies.

## Section 3. Contractor and Vendor Systems

Vendor or contractor systems are covered when they perform or materially influence a public function.

---

# IV. Risk Classification

## Section 1. Risk Tiers

The Technology System shall classify public technology systems by risk.

Risk tiers include:

1. Low;
2. Moderate;
3. High;
4. Critical.

## Section 2. Risk Factors

Risk classification shall consider:

1. rights impact;
2. affected population;
3. scale;
4. reversibility;
5. surveillance risk;
6. legal-status impact;
7. enforcement impact;
8. discrimination risk;
9. opacity;
10. vendor dependency;
11. security risk;
12. data sensitivity;
13. model autonomy;
14. self-learning capability;
15. emergency use.

## Section 3. Reclassification

A system must be reclassified when use, data, behavior, deployment context, affected population, or legal effect materially changes.

---

# V. Public Algorithm Registry

## Section 1. Registry Requirement

The Technology System shall maintain a public algorithm and technology registry.

## Section 2. Registry Fields

The registry shall identify:

1. system name;
2. responsible agency;
3. named human owner;
4. vendor or contractor where applicable;
5. legal authority;
6. purpose;
7. risk tier;
8. affected population;
9. deployment date;
10. review date;
11. system status;
12. audit status;
13. appeal path;
14. human review process;
15. system-card link;
16. model or system version;
17. known limitations;
18. suspension or rollback history.

## Section 3. Registry Defect

Failure to register a covered high-risk or critical system is a rights-impacting public accountability defect.

# VI. System Cards

## Section 1. Requirement

Every high-risk or critical public technology system shall publish a system card.

## Section 2. Minimum Fields

A system card shall include:

1. system name;
2. purpose;
3. legal authority;
4. accountable human owner;
5. agency owner;
6. vendor or contractor where applicable;
7. risk tier;
8. affected population;
9. data sources;
10. system or model type;
11. decision role;
12. human review process;
13. appeal path;
14. audit status;
15. performance measures;
16. known limitations;
17. known risks;
18. bias or rights review;
19. security review;
20. rollback procedure;
21. deployment date;
22. review or expiration date.

## Section 3. Public Summary

Each system card shall include a plain-language summary understandable to ordinary users.

## Section 4. No Boilerplate

Vague, generic, or boilerplate system cards do not satisfy this Charter.

---

# VII. No Black-Box Public Authority

## Section 1. Rule

No unauditable system may make or materially shape rights-impacting public decisions.

## Section 2. Vendor Secrecy

Trade secrecy, vendor control, proprietary software, technical complexity, or contract terms shall not defeat public audit, court review, rights protection, or meaningful human review.

## Section 3. Minimum Audit Access

High-risk systems require sufficient access to documentation, model behavior information, data provenance, input and output records, performance testing, bias testing, security testing, update history, failure logs, and vendor change records.

## Section 4. Limited Public Disclosure

Where full public disclosure would create privacy, security, or legitimate trade-secret risk, auditors, courts, and oversight bodies must still receive sufficient access.

---

# VIII. Meaningful Human Review

## Section 1. Requirement

Meaningful human review is required when technology materially affects rights, benefits, legal status, enforcement, surveillance, voting access, public service priority, or public remedies.

## Section 2. Standards

Human review must be:

1. staffed;
2. trained;
3. authorized;
4. timely;
5. documented;
6. accessible;
7. auditable;
8. capable of reversing or modifying the automated outcome.

## Section 3. Review Theater Prohibited

A review process is invalid if the reviewer merely rubber-stamps the automated outcome or lacks authority, evidence, time, discretion, or training.

## Section 4. Capacity

Public authorities shall maintain sufficient human review capacity to prevent overload, delay, and decorative review.

---

# IX. Technology Audits

## Section 1. Audit Requirement

High-risk and critical systems shall receive technology audits.

## Section 2. Audit Scope

Audits may assess:

1. legal authority;
2. data quality;
3. model behavior;
4. accuracy;
5. error rates;
6. bias and disparity;
7. appeal outcomes;
8. human review quality;
9. drift;
10. cybersecurity;
11. vendor dependency;
12. public accountability;
13. rights impact;
14. rollback readiness.

## Section 3. Public Summary

Audit summaries shall be public, subject to lawful privacy and security limits.

## Section 4. Corrective Action

Audit findings may require correction, suspension, rollback, procurement review, or referral to courts or integrity bodies.

---

# X. Algorithmic Review Metrics

## Section 1. Metrics

Rights-impacting systems shall publish or report:

1. appeal rate;
2. reversal or modification rate;
3. override rate;
4. error rate where measurable;
5. disparity indicators;
6. average review time;
7. unresolved review backlog;
8. model drift events;
9. rollback events;
10. human-review staffing sufficiency;
11. complaint volume;
12. audit findings.

## Section 2. Privacy

Metrics shall protect privacy, prevent reidentification, and avoid exposing vulnerable groups.

## Section 3. Gaming Prevention

Metrics shall be audited to detect suppression of appeals, undercounting of errors, narrow definitions of reversal, or manipulation of review statistics.

---

# XI. Model Versioning and Drift

## Section 1. Versioning

Covered systems shall maintain version records for model versions, rule sets, data sources, training updates where applicable, deployment context, risk tier, vendor updates, audit status, and rollback points.

## Section 2. Drift Monitoring

Covered systems shall monitor performance drift, bias drift, data drift, outcome drift, rights-impact drift, security drift, and scope drift.

## Section 3. Material Drift

Material drift requires review, documentation, and possible suspension, rollback, or reauthorization.

## Section 4. Unauthorized Drift

Unauthorized material drift in a high-risk or critical system shall trigger review and possible suspension.

---

# XII. Self-Learning and Self-Healing Systems

## Section 1. Approval Gates

Self-learning public systems must have approval gates for rights-impacting updates, material model changes, changed data sources, changed deployment contexts, changed risk classification, emergency deployment, and rollback after harmful drift.

## Section 2. Prohibited Self-Amendment

Self-learning systems may not self-amend law, rights, elections, punishment, eligibility, emergency authority, surveillance authority, or constitutional authority.

## Section 3. Self-Healing Limits

Self-healing systems may repair reversible operational failures.

They may not alter legal entitlements, enforcement rules, appeal rights, or public authority without lawful review.

---

# XIII. Technology Procurement

## Section 1. Procurement Review

High-risk public technology procurement shall receive pre-procurement review by the Technology System and Integrity System.

## Section 2. Contract Requirements

Contracts for rights-impacting technology shall include:

1. audit access;
2. security review;
3. documentation;
4. appeal support;
5. exit rights;
6. portability;
7. model or system evaluation rights;
8. incident reporting;
9. update review;
10. rights-impact assessment.

## Section 3. No Lock-In

Vendor lock-in shall not be permitted for constitutional records, rights-impacting systems, election systems, emergency systems, or critical public infrastructure unless independently justified and reviewable.

---

# XIV. Emergency Technology

## Section 1. Provisional Authorization

Emergency deployment of high-risk technology may occur only through time-limited provisional authorization.

## Section 2. Required Terms

Provisional authorization shall include legal basis, emergency purpose, accountable owner, risk tier, rights assessment where possible, human review plan, expiration date, post-deployment audit, public disclosure as soon as lawful, and rollback or suspension path.

## Section 3. No Black-Box Emergency Exception

Emergency deployment shall not bypass the No Black-Box Public Authority rule for rights-impacting decisions.

## Section 4. Continuation Review

Emergency technology may not continue after the emergency without ordinary Technology System review, rights review, audit, and public accountability.

---

# XV. Surveillance and Biometric Technology

## Section 1. Heightened Review

Surveillance and biometric systems require heightened review when used for enforcement, public access, identification, tracking, risk scoring, or emergency response.

## Section 2. Safeguards

Such systems require legal authority, necessity finding, proportionality review, minimization, retention limits, access controls, audit logs, rights review, appeal or challenge path where applicable, and public reporting where lawful.

## Section 3. Prohibited Uses

Surveillance or biometric systems may not be used for partisan targeting, unlawful protest suppression, unlawful political monitoring, or discrimination.

---

# XVI. Data Governance

## Section 1. Principles

Public data systems shall follow legality, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, correction, security, access control, retention limits, auditability, and rights protection.

## Section 2. Sensitive Data

Sensitive data includes health data, biometric data, precise location data, legal-status data, benefit data, financial data, protected identity data, minor data, surveillance data, and protected political activity data.

## Section 3. Data Quality

A rights-impacting system may not rely on data known to be materially inaccurate, incomplete, biased, stale, or unlawfully obtained.

## Section 4. Correction

Affected persons shall have correction pathways where inaccurate data materially affects public authority.

---

# XVII. Cybersecurity and Resilience

## Section 1. Cybersecurity Duty

Public technology systems shall maintain cybersecurity protections proportionate to risk.

## Section 2. Critical Systems

Critical systems include technology affecting elections, courts, emergency powers, benefits, healthcare access, public safety, legal status, public finance, public records, or critical infrastructure.

## Section 3. Incident Reporting

Material cybersecurity incidents shall be reported to appropriate oversight bodies and publicly disclosed where lawful and safe.

## Section 4. Continuity

Critical systems shall maintain continuity, backup, and recovery plans.

---

# XVIII. Distributed Ledger Coordination

## Section 1. Ledger Governance

Distributed ledger systems are governed by Schedule H, but the Technology System shall coordinate technical review, cybersecurity review, procurement review, and public interface integration.

## Section 2. DLT Necessity

The Technology System shall help evaluate whether a distributed ledger materially outperforms simpler audited systems.

## Section 3. Ledger Limits

Ledger systems may verify record history. They may not determine final legal truth, replace courts, automate sovereignty, or replace human review.

---

# XIX. Local and Regional Technology Nodes

## Section 1. Node Function

Local and regional technology nodes may administer technology governance where capacity exists.

## Section 2. National Floor

All nodes must comply with national floors for rights, auditability, public accountability, privacy, cybersecurity, and technology safety.

## Section 3. Capacity Gates

Nodes may not receive full duties until staffing, funding, records, appeal, audit, secure technology, and public access capacity exist.

## Section 4. Support Nodes

Where capacity is insufficient, support nodes may temporarily assist under Schedule N.

This Charter shall be reviewed periodically to ensure the Technology System remains rights-protecting, auditable, adaptable, secure, and resistant to vendor capture.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. registry completeness;
2. system-card quality;
3. audit access;
4. human review quality;
5. appeal outcomes;
6. drift monitoring;
7. procurement compliance;
8. vendor concentration;
9. cybersecurity;
10. public usability;
11. rights impact;
12. emerging technology risks.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Charter shall follow the process applicable to technology system architecture and major statutory architecture.
