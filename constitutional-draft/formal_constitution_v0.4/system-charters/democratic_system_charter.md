## Purpose

This charter defines how the Democratic System operates across elections, party roles, electoral administration, citizen participation, coalition formation, public ledgers, and democratic accountability.

Core rule:

> Parties compete for power. They do not own the state.

---

# I. Democratic System Mandate

## Section 1. Function

The Democratic System protects:

1. representation;
2. electoral competition;
3. lawful party formation;
4. voter access;
5. public participation;
6. opposition rights;
7. peaceful transfer of power;
8. public confidence in democratic machinery.

## Section 2. Constitutional Position

The Democratic System is one of the five constitutional systems of the Commonwealth.

It operates subject to the Democratic Kernel, rights floor, public accountability rules, stewardship limits, and amendment safeguards.

## Section 3. Nonpartisan Machinery

The machinery of democratic competition shall be administered by nonpartisan Stewardship Power.

No Governing Power, Opposition Power, party, candidate, donor network, vendor, or security body may own election machinery.

---

# II. Democratic System Institutions

## Section 1. Core Institutions

The Democratic System may include:

1. Election Stewardship Commission;
2. Boundary Commission;
3. Party Status Office;
4. Public Party Status Ledger Office;
5. Electoral Education Office;
6. Citizen Participation Office;
7. Coalition Certification Office;
8. Election Technology Audit Unit;
9. Local Election Nodes;
10. Election and Party Court liaison function.

## Section 2. Stewardship Boundary

Democratic System institutions may administer, certify, disclose, audit, review, and route disputes.

They may not choose party winners, favor lawful ideology, govern ordinary policy, or manipulate electoral competition.

## Section 3. Public Interface

The Democratic System shall publish election, party, coalition, boundary, funding, debate, and certification records through the Public Constitutional Interface.

---

# III. Election Administration

## Section 1. Independence

Election administration shall be independent, transparent, auditable, rights-compliant, insulated from partisan control, and subject to judicial review.

## Section 2. Duties

Election administration includes:

1. voter registration administration;
2. ballot design;
3. polling and voting access;
4. vote counting;
5. certification;
6. recount procedures;
7. chain-of-custody procedures;
8. election technology audit;
9. public voter education;
10. dispute routing.

## Section 3. Public Records

Election records shall identify accountable offices, legal authority, audit path, appeal path, certification status, dispute status, and public access channels.

## Section 4. Local Election Nodes

Local election nodes may administer local election operations where capacity exists.

Where capacity is insufficient, temporary support nodes may assist under Schedule N.

---

# IV. Electoral Model

## Section 1. Mixed-Member Proportional System

The Commonwealth shall use mixed-member proportional representation unless amended through the election and power rules procedure.

## Section 2. Two Votes

Each voter shall cast, where constitutionally practicable:

1. a local representative vote;
2. a party or proportional vote.

## Section 3. Seat Balance

The default seat structure is:

| Seat type | Default share |
|---|---:|
| Local district seats | 50% |
| Compensatory proportional seats | 50% |

## Section 4. Proportional Correction

Proportional seats shall correct disproportionality between local district outcomes and the party or proportional vote.

## Section 5. Public Explanation

The Democratic System shall explain, in plain language:

1. local votes;
2. party votes;
3. proportional correction;
4. overhang seats;
5. adjustment seats;
6. final seat totals;
7. party role assignment.

---

# V. Boundary Commission

## Section 1. Independence

District boundaries shall be drawn by an independent Boundary Commission.

## Section 2. Criteria

Boundary drawing shall consider:

1. population equality;
2. contiguity;
3. community coherence;
4. minority rights;
5. accessibility;
6. geographic reality;
7. regional integrity;
8. anti-gerrymandering metrics;
9. public comment;
10. transparent map publication.

## Section 3. Prohibited Criteria

Districts shall not be drawn to entrench a party, incumbent, faction, donor network, or governing coalition.

## Section 4. Public Challenge

Boundary maps shall have a public challenge period and expedited judicial review.

---

# VI. Party Registration and Qualification

## Section 1. Party Registration

Political parties may register according to neutral, public, and rights-compliant rules.

## Section 2. Registration Requirements

Registration may require:

1. party name;
2. public officers;
3. internal governance statement;
4. funding disclosure compliance;
5. constitutional compliance certification;
6. anti-coercion certification;
7. public contact information;
8. common-control disclosures where relevant.

## Section 3. No Ideological Exclusion

Party registration may not be denied solely because of lawful ideology, peaceful radicalism, dissent, criticism of government, or constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 4. Disqualification

Disqualification requires lawful cause, public reasoning, due process, and appeal.

---

# VII. Party Power Roles

## Section 1. Recognized Roles

The Democratic System recognizes:

1. Governing Power;
2. Opposition Power;
3. Plurality Power;
4. Emergent Power;
5. Stewardship Power as a nonpartisan constitutional function.

## Section 2. Governing Power

The Governing Power is the party or coalition with a lawful governing mandate.

## Section 3. Opposition Power

The Opposition Power is the largest non-governing party or coalition and receives protected rights to audit, investigate, question, challenge, and offer a public alternative.

## Section 4. Plurality Power

Plurality Power belongs to qualifying minority blocs beyond the principal opposition.

## Section 5. Emergent Power

Emergent Power protects new, regional, reformist, or growing parties.

## Section 6. Stewardship Power

Stewardship Power protects democratic machinery and may not become a political party.

---

# VIII. Public Party Status Ledger

## Section 1. Requirement

The Democratic System shall maintain a Public Party Status Ledger.

## Section 2. Required Events

The Ledger shall be updated after:

1. final election certification;
2. coalition formation;
3. coalition collapse;
4. confidence vote;
5. material party split;
6. material party merger;
7. common-control finding;
8. party role reassignment;
9. public funding change;
10. debate access change.

## Section 3. Ledger Contents

The Ledger shall show:

1. vote share;
2. district seats;
3. proportional seats;
4. overhang seats;
5. adjustment seats;
6. final seats;
7. governing bloc;
8. principal opposition;
9. plurality blocs;
10. emergent parties;
11. coalition agreement;
12. confidence-and-supply agreements;
13. committee control;
14. public funding;
15. debate access;
16. common-control findings;
17. compliance status;
18. next confidence or review date.

## Section 4. Public Explanation

The Ledger shall explain party status in plain language.

---

# IX. Coalition Formation

## Section 1. Coalition Deadline

A governing coalition shall be formed within the timeline set by Schedule B.

## Section 2. Public Coalition Agreement

A governing coalition must publish:

1. member parties;
2. governing priorities;
3. cabinet allocation;
4. budget commitments;
5. committee and control arrangements;
6. constitutional red lines;
7. dispute-resolution process;
8. duration or review date;
9. confidence agreement terms.

## Section 3. Side Agreements

Material side agreements affecting offices, budgets, appointments, public funding, procurement, regional concessions, committee control, or constitutional procedure must be disclosed.

## Section 4. Secret Terms

Secret coalition terms affecting public authority are unenforceable and subject to integrity review.

---

# X. Confidence and Transfer of Power

## Section 1. Confidence Certification

The Democratic System shall certify whether a governing coalition has obtained lawful confidence under applicable rules.

## Section 2. Constructive No-Confidence

No-confidence shall be constructive and must identify a replacement governing bloc or lawful transition mechanism sufficient to maintain continuity.

## Section 3. Transfer of Power

The Democratic System shall coordinate with lawful offices to support peaceful transfer of power after certified results, confidence changes, or lawful removal.

## Section 4. No Delay by Dispute

Disputes shall be routed to the appropriate forum without creating indefinite transfer paralysis.

---

# XI. Debate Access

## Section 1. Public Debate Access

Debate access rules shall balance openness, seriousness, public relevance, and anti-cartel protection.

## Section 2. Criteria

Debate access may consider:

1. polling;
2. prior vote share;
3. petition signatures;
4. regional support;
5. verified emergent status;
6. local district representation;
7. public funding qualification;
8. constitutional compliance.

## Section 3. Emergent Access

Verified emergent parties shall have a realistic path to debate access.

## Section 4. Appeal

Debate access decisions shall be public, reasoned, and appealable.

---

# XII. Public Funding

## Section 1. Purpose

Public funding supports democratic competition, not fake parties, donor laundering, or shell organizations.

## Section 2. Funding Basis

Public funding shall be staged by:

1. demonstrated support;
2. transparency compliance;
3. internal governance compliance;
4. anti-shell-party compliance;
5. prior audit results.

## Section 3. Emergent Party Support

Verified emergent parties may receive limited public support sufficient to compete, subject to compliance.

## Section 4. Due Process

Public funding suspension, reduction, or recovery requires due process and appeal.

---

# XIII. Citizen Participation

## Section 1. Permitted Mechanisms

The Democratic System may use:

1. citizen assemblies;
2. civic forums;
3. public comment;
4. petitions;
5. constitutional referendums;
6. public consultation;
7. participatory review where authorized.

## Section 2. Supplemental Role

Citizen participation supplements representative government.

It does not replace rights, courts, elections, or lawful institutions.

## Section 3. Integrity Standards

Citizen participation mechanisms shall include:

1. transparent selection;
2. fair access;
3. compensation where burdensome;
4. public mandate;
5. public record;
6. nonpartisan facilitation;
7. clear legal effect;
8. protection against capture.

---

# XIV. Election Dispute Routing

## Section 1. Primary Forum

Election and party disputes shall be routed to the Election and Party Court unless another forum has primary jurisdiction.

## Section 2. Covered Disputes

Covered disputes include:

1. ballot access;
2. election certification;
3. districting;
4. party role assignment;
5. public funding;
6. debate access;
7. coalition status;
8. common-control findings;
9. party ledger errors;
10. emergency election procedures.

## Section 3. Urgent Timeline

Election disputes shall be resolved quickly enough to preserve rights, certification, peaceful transfer, and public trust.

## Section 4. Bad-Faith Claims

Knowingly false, repetitive, or bad-faith election claims designed to delay certification may be sanctioned.

---

# XV. Election Technology

## Section 1. Audit

Election technology shall be auditable, explainable, secure, and reviewable.

## Section 2. No Private Control

Vendors may not control election authority through technical systems, proprietary secrecy, data access, or infrastructure lock-in.

## Section 3. Public Testing

Election technology shall receive public testing or public audit procedures where appropriate.

## Section 4. Ledger Limits

Election audit ledgers may verify chain-of-custody or certification history, but shall not become default public voting systems without separate constitutional authorization.

---

# XVI. Public Education

## Section 1. Required Education

The Democratic System shall provide public education about:

1. voter registration;
2. ballot structure;
3. two-vote system;
4. proportional correction;
5. party roles;
6. coalitions;
7. public party ledger;
8. appeal rights;
9. election dispute process.

## Section 2. Ballot Comprehension

Major electoral changes require public testing of ballot comprehension, accessibility, and usability.

## Section 3. Non-Digital Access

Election education shall be available through digital and non-digital means.

# XVIII. Charter Review

## Section 1. Periodic Review

This Charter shall be reviewed periodically to ensure the Democratic System remains fair, plural, accountable, usable, and resistant to capture.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. voter access;
2. proportionality;
3. local representation;
4. party competition;
5. emergent party access;
6. public ledger usability;
7. coalition transparency;
8. election dispute timing;
9. public education quality;
10. election technology integrity;
11. public trust;
12. peaceful transfer performance.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Charter shall follow the process applicable to democratic system architecture and election or power rules.
