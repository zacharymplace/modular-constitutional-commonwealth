## Purpose

This charter defines how the Rights and Justice System protects rights, due process, courts, remedies, judicial review, fast rights relief, election and party adjudication, technology review, and public access to justice.

Core rule:

> Courts protect the constitutional floor. They do not govern ordinary policy where lawful democratic choices remain.

---

# I. Rights and Justice System Mandate

## Section 1. Function

The Rights and Justice System protects:

1. the Democratic Kernel;
2. the rights floor;
3. due process;
4. court access;
5. lawful public authority;
6. fast rights remedies;
7. judicial independence;
8. review of emergency powers;
9. review of election and party disputes;
10. review of rights-impacting technology;
11. remedies against unlawful government action.

## Section 2. Constitutional Position

The Rights and Justice System is one of the five constitutional systems of the Commonwealth.

It operates subject to the Democratic Kernel, public accountability rules, judicial restraint, stewardship limits, and amendment safeguards.

## Section 3. Rights Usability

A right that people cannot find, file, understand, or enforce is not practically secure.

The Rights and Justice System shall prioritize usable rights, visible remedies, fair process, and timely review.

---

# II. Rights Floor

## Section 1. Protected Rights

The Rights and Justice System protects the rights identified in the Core Constitution, including:

1. equal citizenship;
2. political rights;
3. rights of persons under Commonwealth authority;
4. civil liberties;
5. due process;
6. privacy and digital rights;
7. fast rights protection;
8. public accountability rights.

## Section 2. Rights Limitation

Rights may be limited only by law and only when the limitation is necessary, proportionate, non-discriminatory, reviewable, and consistent with the Democratic Kernel.

## Section 3. Burden

The public authority imposing a rights limitation bears the burden of justification.

## Section 4. Heightened Review

Limitations affecting voting, candidacy, detention, speech, press, peaceful assembly, political opposition, surveillance, court access, emergency review, or Kernel-connected rights require heightened review.

---

# III. Court Structure

## Section 1. Courts and Tribunals

The Rights and Justice System may include:

1. Constitutional Court;
2. High Court or Supreme Appellate Court;
3. Administrative Courts;
4. Fast Rights Tribunal;
5. Election and Party Court;
6. Technology Review Tribunal;
7. other courts or tribunals created by constitutional statute.

## Section 2. Specialized Function

Specialized tribunals exist to provide speed, expertise, accessibility, and usable remedies.

They do not eliminate constitutional review where Kernel, rights floor, emergency, election, federalism, or framework issues are implicated.

## Section 3. Public Routing

The public shall not be required to know the internal structure of courts to access review.

Good-faith filings must be routed to the proper forum.

---

# IV. Constitutional Court

## Section 1. Jurisdiction

The Constitutional Court protects:

1. Democratic Kernel;
2. constitutional framework;
3. rights floor;
4. federal, state, regional, and local authority boundaries;
5. emergency power limits;
6. amendment classification;
7. refounding procedures;
8. court independence;
9. election-system constitutionality;
10. party-system constitutional disputes;
11. stewardship overreach;
12. public authority evasion.

## Section 2. Powers

The Constitutional Court may:

1. invalidate unconstitutional laws;
2. issue constitutional injunctions;
3. require lawful process;
4. enforce Kernel protections;
5. resolve federalism disputes;
6. review emergency powers;
7. require compliance plans;
8. enforce opposition and party role rights;
9. enforce court order compliance.

## Section 3. Limits

The Constitutional Court may not govern ordinary policy, select governing coalitions, set ordinary budget priorities, administer agencies directly except as necessary for remedy, or substitute judicial preference for lawful democratic choice.

---

# V. High Court or Supreme Appellate Court

## Section 1. Function

The High Court or Supreme Appellate Court is the final ordinary appellate court for non-constitutional civil, criminal, statutory, and procedural matters.

## Section 2. Jurisdiction

It may hear:

1. civil appeals;
2. criminal appeals;
3. statutory interpretation questions;
4. conflicts between lower courts;
5. ordinary administrative appeals where constitutional issues are not primary;
6. procedural questions of general importance.

## Section 3. Constitutional Referral

Substantial constitutional, Kernel, framework, emergency, federalism, or rights-floor questions may be referred to or reviewed by the Constitutional Court.

---

# VI. Administrative Courts

## Section 1. Function

Administrative Courts review agency action, administrative decision-making, and public-service disputes.

## Section 2. Jurisdiction

Administrative Courts may hear matters involving:

1. public benefits;
2. licensing;
3. taxation administration;
4. public housing;
5. education placement;
6. healthcare access;
7. agency enforcement;
8. civil penalties;
9. procurement decisions;
10. civil service disputes;
11. public records administration;
12. public-service denial or delay;
13. regulatory compliance.

## Section 3. Review Standard

Administrative action must be legally authorized, reasoned, non-arbitrary, procedurally fair, rights-compliant, publicly accountable, and auditable where technology is used.

---

# VII. Fast Rights Tribunal

## Section 1. Function

The Fast Rights Tribunal hears urgent rights claims where delay may cause serious or irreversible harm.

## Section 2. Covered Claims

The Tribunal may hear urgent claims involving:

1. voting access;
2. ballot access;
3. unlawful detention;
4. due process denial;
5. censorship or speech restriction;
6. unlawful surveillance;
7. protest restriction;
8. emergency overreach;
9. discrimination;
10. severe benefit termination;
11. algorithmic denial without meaningful review;
12. denial of court access;
13. local or state rights floor violations.

## Section 3. Serious Claim Gate

Fast rights relief requires plausible serious rights claim, imminent or difficult-to-repair harm, connection to public authority, inadequate ordinary remedy, and proportional requested relief.

## Section 4. Narrow Relief

The Tribunal shall use the narrowest effective interim remedy where possible.

---

# VIII. Election and Party Court

## Section 1. Function

The Election and Party Court protects democratic competition and resolves election, districting, party-role, and campaign-process disputes.

## Section 2. Jurisdiction

The Court may hear:

1. election certification disputes;
2. ballot access disputes;
3. districting challenges;
4. campaign finance enforcement appeals;
5. party finance enforcement appeals;
6. party role assignment disputes;
7. common-control findings;
8. coalition status disputes;
9. debate access disputes;
10. public funding eligibility disputes;
11. party ledger corrections;
12. delayed self-dealing violations;
13. emergency election procedure disputes.

## Section 3. Certification Continuity

Election litigation may correct unlawful results or procedures, but may not create indefinite certification paralysis.

---

# IX. Technology Review Tribunal

## Section 1. Function

The Technology Review Tribunal reviews public algorithms, automated decision systems, AI, surveillance tools, public data systems, and technology-enabled public authority.

## Section 2. Jurisdiction

The Tribunal may hear disputes involving:

1. algorithmic benefit denial;
2. automated public-service prioritization;
3. predictive policing tools;
4. surveillance systems;
5. public AI procurement;
6. model audit disputes;
7. human review failures;
8. data misuse;
9. algorithmic discrimination;
10. black-box public authority;
11. model drift;
12. unauthorized self-learning;
13. public technology rollback;
14. technology registry noncompliance.

## Section 3. Powers

The Tribunal may require disclosure, order meaningful human review, suspend unsafe systems, require model audit, require rollback, require correction, require system-card correction, or refer constitutional questions to the Constitutional Court.

---

# X. One-Door Filing and Routing

## Section 1. One-Door Filing

A timely and plausible claim filed in the wrong court or tribunal in good faith shall be transferred to the proper forum, not dismissed.

## Section 2. Wrong-Door Transfer

A good-faith filing, appeal, complaint, records request, or rights petition made through the wrong public office shall be routed to the proper forum.

## Section 3. Preservation of Timeliness

A claim filed in good faith in the wrong forum shall preserve filing date for limitations, appeal, or emergency-review purposes where the claim is timely and plausible.

## Section 4. Anti-Circular Transfer

A matter may not be transferred indefinitely between offices or systems.

---

# XI. Primary Harm Rule

## Section 1. Purpose

The Primary Harm Rule assigns lead jurisdiction according to the main harm or authority at issue.

## Section 2. Default Lead Forums

| Primary harm or authority | Lead forum |
|---|---|
| Immediate rights harm | Fast Rights Tribunal |
| Election process harm | Election and Party Court |
| Algorithmic or public technology harm | Technology Review Tribunal |
| Agency action or public-service harm | Administrative Court |
| Constitutional Kernel or framework harm | Constitutional Court |
| Ordinary civil or criminal appeal | High Court or ordinary appellate path |

## Section 3. Multiple Harms

Where multiple harms are materially implicated, courts may coordinate, refer, or convene joint review according to law.

---

# XII. Urgent Review

## Section 1. Urgent Categories

Expedited timelines shall exist for:

1. emergency petitions;
2. election certification disputes;
3. ballot access disputes;
4. districting disputes;
5. detention claims;
6. emergency surveillance challenges;
7. emergency-power renewal challenges;
8. Fast Rights Tribunal petitions;
9. technology suspensions where rights are at immediate risk.

## Section 2. Timeline Standard

Urgent review shall be fast enough to preserve practical remedy and disciplined enough to prevent sabotage.

## Section 3. Emergency Review Clock

Emergency power and emergency rights cases shall receive review under a defined emergency review clock.

---

# XIII. Remedies

## Section 1. Remedial Hierarchy

Courts shall generally prefer the least intrusive remedy sufficient to cure the violation.

## Section 2. Remedy Types

Remedies may include:

1. notice;
2. disclosure;
3. procedural correction;
4. reconsideration;
5. individual relief;
6. limited class relief;
7. narrow injunction;
8. broader injunction where systemic harm exists;
9. compliance plan;
10. structural remedy only where necessary to correct persistent constitutional failure.

## Section 3. Policy Choice

Courts shall leave lawful policy choices to democratic institutions where multiple constitutional options remain.

---

# XIV. Court Order Enforcement

## Section 1. Enforcement Duty

Public authorities shall comply with final court and tribunal orders.

## Section 2. Enforcement Ladder

Failure to comply may trigger:

1. compliance deadline;
2. public noncompliance notice;
3. responsible-officer identification;
4. remedial order;
5. budget or administrative compliance review;
6. contempt or sanction where lawful;
7. Integrity System referral if bad faith exists;
8. legislative oversight trigger for persistent noncompliance.

## Section 3. Court Compliance Dashboard

The Public Constitutional Interface shall include a court compliance dashboard showing final rights and constitutional orders, responsible offices, deadlines, status, and escalation steps.

---

# XV. Technical Expert Pool

## Section 1. Purpose

Courts and tribunals may require technical expertise in technology, fiscal, infrastructure, election, security, and algorithmic cases.

## Section 2. Governance

The independent technical expert pool shall be governed by conflict disclosure, rotating assignments, vendor cooling-off rules, public-interest participation, professional competence, adversarial challenge, and public explanation of assumptions where possible.

## Section 3. Anti-Capture

Technical experts assist courts. They do not substitute technical preference for constitutional reasoning.

---

# XVI. Access to Justice

## Section 1. Public Guidance

The Rights and Justice System shall publish plain-language guidance explaining forums, filing steps, deadlines, appeal paths, urgent review, technology rights, election deadlines, and available remedies.

## Section 2. Support

Constitutional statute shall provide access support for high-risk rights claims, including legal aid, filing assistance, translation, disability access, non-digital access, and public navigators where needed.

## Section 3. No Practical Denial

A right shall not be practically denied because the person cannot navigate court complexity.

---

# XVII. Abuse Prevention

## Section 1. Frivolous or Bad-Faith Claims

Courts may sanction knowingly false claims, repetitive harassment filings, bad-faith emergency petitions, fabricated evidence, and strategic obstruction with no plausible legal basis.

## Section 2. Government Delay

Courts may sanction or correct refusal to produce records, missing appeal notices, failure to implement orders, use of complexity to evade review, algorithmic opacity, improper emergency secrecy, and bad-faith transfer between forums.

## Section 3. Balance

Courts shall remain open to serious claims and closed to procedural sabotage.

# XIX. Charter Review

## Section 1. Periodic Review

This Charter shall be reviewed periodically to ensure the Rights and Justice System remains accessible, independent, timely, rights-protecting, and resistant to capture.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. time to remedy;
2. access to courts;
3. filing success;
4. wrong-door transfer success;
5. urgent review speed;
6. appeal clarity;
7. court order compliance;
8. technical expert independence;
9. public usability;
10. risk of judicial overreach;
11. risk of judicial capture;
12. public trust.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Charter shall follow the process applicable to rights and justice system architecture and court structure rules.
