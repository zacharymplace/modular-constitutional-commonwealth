## Purpose

This charter defines how the Integrity System prevents corruption, capture, hidden influence, public money abuse, procurement manipulation, lobbying opacity, ethics failure, and misuse of public authority.

Core rule:

> Public power is a fiduciary trust. It must be visible, auditable, and protected from capture.

---

# I. Integrity System Mandate

## Section 1. Function

The Integrity System protects:

1. public office integrity;
2. anti-corruption enforcement;
3. ethics compliance;
4. lobbying transparency;
5. conflict disclosure;
6. procurement integrity;
7. public spending integrity;
8. campaign and party finance integrity;
9. whistleblower protection;
10. influence disclosure;
11. public audits;
12. anti-capture safeguards.

## Section 2. Constitutional Position

The Integrity System is one of the five constitutional systems of the Commonwealth.

It operates subject to the Democratic Kernel, rights floor, public accountability rules, due process, stewardship limits, and anti-weaponization principles.

## Section 3. Integrity Limit

The Integrity System may investigate, disclose, audit, sanction, refer, and enforce neutral rules.

It may not choose ordinary policy, suppress lawful dissent, determine lawful ideology, or govern in place of elected institutions.

---

# II. Integrity System Institutions

## Section 1. Core Institutions

The Integrity System may include:

1. Integrity Commission;
2. Public Ethics Office;
3. Lobbying and Influence Registry;
4. Procurement Integrity Office;
5. Public Spending Review Office;
6. Whistleblower Protection Office;
7. Party and Campaign Finance Integrity Unit;
8. Conflict Disclosure Office;
9. Public Audit liaison function;
10. Enforcement Review Panel;
11. Local and regional integrity nodes.

## Section 2. Independence

Integrity institutions shall be independent enough to investigate power and accountable enough to prevent selective enforcement.

## Section 3. Public Interface

The Integrity System shall publish public-facing records through the Public Constitutional Interface, subject to lawful privacy, security, and investigation limits.

---

# III. Anti-Corruption Scope

## Section 1. Covered Conduct

The Integrity System may review conduct involving:

1. bribery;
2. extortion;
3. fraud;
4. embezzlement;
5. procurement manipulation;
6. public funds misuse;
7. undisclosed conflicts;
8. revolving-door abuse;
9. shadow lobbying;
10. beneficial ownership concealment;
11. donor capture;
12. vendor capture;
13. shell-party manipulation;
14. emergency spending abuse;
15. public technology capture;
16. influence concealment.

## Section 2. Capture Defined Operationally

Capture exists where a public office, process, program, rule, or institution is materially controlled or distorted by private, partisan, vendor, donor, foreign, or institutional interests contrary to lawful public purpose.

## Section 3. Lawful Advocacy Protected

Lawful advocacy, journalism, scholarship, protest, lobbying disclosed under law, civic association, and ordinary political persuasion are not corruption or capture solely because they seek to influence public decisions.

---

# IV. Ethics and Conflict Disclosure

## Section 1. Covered Officials

Ethics and conflict rules may apply to:

1. elected officials;
2. appointed officials;
3. executive officers;
4. legislative officers;
5. stewardship officials;
6. senior civil servants;
7. procurement officials;
8. public finance officials;
9. public technology officials;
10. emergency officials;
11. public contractors performing material public functions;
12. party officers receiving public privileges;
13. candidates for covered offices.

## Section 2. Required Disclosures

Covered persons may be required to disclose:

1. financial interests;
2. employment interests;
3. family interests where material;
4. beneficial ownership;
5. business relationships;
6. donor relationships;
7. vendor relationships;
8. lobbying relationships;
9. party relationships;
10. foreign-linked interests;
11. regulated-entity relationships;
12. consulting relationships;
13. nonprofit or foundation interests where material.

## Section 3. Conflict Mitigation

Mitigation may include recusal, divestment, blind trust, firewall, reassignment, independent review, public disclosure, or other lawful remedy.

## Section 4. Privacy

Disclosure shall reveal material public-interest conflicts without exposing irrelevant private details.

---

# V. Lobbying and Influence Registry

## Section 1. Registry Requirement

The Integrity System shall maintain a lobbying and influence registry.

## Section 2. Required Records

The registry may include:

1. lobbyist or advocate identity;
2. client or sponsor;
3. subject matter;
4. public offices contacted;
5. draft language submitted;
6. compensation source where material;
7. affiliated entities;
8. former officials involved;
9. procurement interest;
10. campaign or party finance links;
11. beneficial ownership where relevant;
12. foreign-linked interest where relevant.

## Section 3. Influence Ledger

Major legislation, major procurement, major regulatory action, major emergency spending, and major public technology adoption may require an Influence Ledger.

## Section 4. Influence Ledger Fields

An Influence Ledger may show:

1. lobbying contacts;
2. draft language sources;
3. major donors where legally required;
4. affiliated advocacy groups;
5. party finance relationships;
6. procurement beneficiaries;
7. conflicts;
8. late amendments;
9. common-control findings where relevant;
10. public response or agency explanation.

## Section 5. No Speech Suppression

Influence disclosure shall not suppress lawful speech, ordinary civic participation, journalism, protest, scholarship, or public-interest advocacy.

---

# VI. Procurement Integrity

## Section 1. Procurement Review

The Integrity System shall review procurement integrity for material public contracts.

## Section 2. Covered Procurement Risks

Procurement review may address:

1. sole-source abuse;
2. bid rigging;
3. beneficial ownership concealment;
4. emergency procurement abuse;
5. vendor conflicts;
6. revolving-door influence;
7. pre-procurement specification capture;
8. change-order abuse;
9. subcontractor concealment;
10. public technology lock-in;
11. price manipulation;
12. delivery milestone fraud.

## Section 3. Pre-Procurement Influence

Material pre-procurement influence shall be disclosed where required by Schedule I and related law.

## Section 4. Procurement Ledger

The Integrity System shall coordinate with fiscal and public accountability authorities to maintain procurement ledgers and audit trails.

---

# VII. Public Spending Integrity

## Section 1. Spending Review

The Integrity System may review public spending for legality, integrity, conflicts, fraud, waste, abuse, and public-purpose compliance.

## Section 2. Covered Spending

Covered spending may include:

1. appropriations;
2. grants;
3. contracts;
4. emergency spending;
5. intergovernmental transfers;
6. tax expenditures where integrity concerns exist;
7. public-private partnerships;
8. infrastructure projects;
9. technology procurements;
10. stewardship funding disputes where integrity concerns exist.

## Section 3. Emergency Spending

Emergency spending may move quickly, but remains subject to accountability, audit, conflict review, and post-emergency review.

## Section 4. Fiscal Coordination

The Integrity System shall coordinate with fiscal authorities where spending integrity overlaps with fiscal scoring, procurement ledgers, emergency spending ledgers, or public balance sheet risks.

---

# VIII. Campaign and Party Finance Integrity

## Section 1. Scope

The Integrity System may review campaign and party finance compliance in coordination with the Democratic System.

## Section 2. Covered Risks

Covered risks include:

1. unlawful contributions;
2. donor concealment;
3. pass-through funding;
4. shell entities;
5. foreign control;
6. common-control evasion;
7. misuse of public funding;
8. fake parties;
9. coordinated evasion of party finance limits;
10. undisclosed campaign infrastructure.

## Section 3. Party Autonomy

Integrity review shall not control party ideology, lawful platform, internal debate, lawful dissent, or peaceful constitutional reform advocacy.

## Section 4. Due Process

Campaign and party finance enforcement affecting ballot access, public funding, party status, or office eligibility requires notice, evidence, response opportunity, public reasoning, and appeal.

---

# IX. Whistleblower Protection

## Section 1. Protected Reports

Whistleblower protection applies to good-faith reports of:

1. corruption;
2. abuse of office;
3. hidden influence;
4. unlawful surveillance;
5. procurement fraud;
6. fiscal concealment;
7. algorithmic abuse;
8. emergency overreach;
9. security abuse;
10. retaliation;
11. public records concealment;
12. rights violations.

## Section 2. Protected Channels

The Commonwealth shall maintain protected reporting channels, including confidential and classified channels where lawful.

## Section 3. Anti-Retaliation

Retaliation may include termination, demotion, reassignment, clearance retaliation, harassment, contract loss, blacklisting, legal threats, benefit denial, or reputational attack using information asymmetry.

## Section 4. Remedies

Whistleblower retaliation remedies may include reinstatement, correction, damages where lawful, public finding, disciplinary action, integrity referral, or protective order.

## Section 5. False or Bad-Faith Reports

Knowingly false or bad-faith reports may be sanctioned after due process.

Good-faith reports are not bad faith solely because allegations are unproven.

---

# X. Enforcement Standards

## Section 1. Case-Opening Standards

Integrity bodies shall publish case-opening standards, enforcement categories, sanction categories, and appeal paths.

## Section 2. Evidence and Process

Enforcement requires:

1. lawful authority;
2. defined scope;
3. evidence standard;
4. notice where appropriate;
5. opportunity to respond;
6. public reasoning where appropriate;
7. proportional remedy;
8. appeal path.

## Section 3. Sanction Categories

Sanctions shall distinguish:

1. clerical defect;
2. curable error;
3. negligence;
4. bad faith;
5. corruption;
6. foreign or covert influence;
7. abuse of office;
8. obstruction;
9. retaliation.

## Section 4. Cure

Honest or curable mistakes should receive cure opportunity where consistent with public integrity.

## Section 5. Severe Sanctions

Severe sanctions affecting candidacy, party status, office eligibility, public funding, or constitutional office require heightened due process.

---

# XI. Enforcement Remedies

## Section 1. Remedy Types

Integrity remedies may include:

1. warning;
2. cure order;
3. public correction;
4. recusal;
5. divestment;
6. firewall;
7. repayment;
8. fine where lawful;
9. public censure;
10. suspension of privilege;
11. public funding reduction;
12. license suspension;
13. removal process;
14. contract correction;
15. procurement pause;
16. civil or criminal referral where lawful.

## Section 2. Proportionality

Remedies shall be proportional to harm, intent, public impact, repeat conduct, rights impact, and democratic-process impact.

## Section 3. Public Explanation

Material integrity findings shall include public explanation, subject to lawful privacy, security, and investigation limits.

---

# XII. Anti-Weaponization Safeguards

## Section 1. Rule

Integrity rules may not be used in bad faith to disable lawful opposition, harass public servants, punish dissent, suppress journalism, exclude lawful candidates, or entrench incumbents.

## Section 2. Pre-Election Claims

Integrity claims materially affecting candidates, parties, ballot access, public funding, or election timing shall receive expedited process sufficient to protect both electoral integrity and due process.

## Section 3. Leak Discipline

Integrity bodies shall protect preliminary investigations from bad-faith leaks designed to damage reputations before due process.

## Section 4. Good-Faith Accountability

Good-faith integrity complaints remain protected even when not ultimately proven.

---

# XIII. Public Audit and Audit-the-Auditors

## Section 1. Public Audit Coordination

The Integrity System shall coordinate with public audit institutions to detect corruption, fiscal concealment, procurement abuse, and public authority evasion.

## Section 2. Audit-the-Auditors

Integrity bodies shall themselves be subject to:

1. transparent case standards;
2. enforcement statistics;
3. appeal;
4. staggered appointments;
5. external review;
6. budget protection;
7. removal for cause;
8. performance review.

## Section 3. Enforcement Statistics

Integrity bodies shall publish enforcement statistics sufficient to detect selective enforcement, underenforcement, overenforcement, or political bias.

---

# XIV. Local and Regional Integrity Nodes

## Section 1. Purpose

State, regional, and local integrity nodes may administer integrity functions where capacity exists.

## Section 2. National Floor

All nodes must comply with national floors for rights, due process, public accountability, anti-corruption, and public records.

## Section 3. Capacity Gates

Local or regional nodes may not receive full duties until staffing, funding, records, appeal, audit, technology, and public access capacity exist.

## Section 4. Support Nodes

Where capacity is insufficient, a higher-level support node may temporarily assist under Schedule N.

---

# XV. Public Interface and Records

## Section 1. Public Records

The Integrity System shall publish public records for:

1. lobbying registrations;
2. influence ledgers;
3. conflict disclosures;
4. procurement integrity findings;
5. public spending reviews;
6. enforcement outcomes;
7. whistleblower aggregate statistics where lawful;
8. public funding compliance;
9. party finance compliance;
10. audit-the-auditors reports.

## Section 2. Privacy and Security

Public records shall protect privacy, whistleblower identities, protected investigations, classified information, and security-sensitive information where lawful.

## Section 3. Surrogate Explanation

Where details are lawfully withheld, the Integrity System shall provide a public surrogate explanation where possible.

# XVII. Charter Review

## Section 1. Periodic Review

This Charter shall be reviewed periodically to ensure the Integrity System prevents capture without becoming a political weapon.

## Section 2. Review Criteria

Review shall assess:

1. corruption detection;
2. enforcement fairness;
3. enforcement speed;
4. selective enforcement risk;
5. whistleblower protection;
6. procurement integrity;
7. lobbying disclosure quality;
8. public spending review;
9. party finance integrity;
10. audit-the-auditors effectiveness;
11. public trust;
12. anti-weaponization performance.

## Section 3. Amendment

Amendment of this Charter shall follow the process applicable to integrity system architecture and major statutory architecture.
